# The Teachings: traps

Read by every agent through its batch's brief (prep-batch.js copies the plan's section). Each trap is a way a plausible teaching goes wrong. When a new one is found, it is added here, not only fixed in the batch where it was found.

## all

- A reading not found in a source you opened does not ship. A confident false attribution is worse than naming a different commentator.
- The commentator's own words go into the evidence verbatim, in the language they were written in, with your English gloss. A paraphrase of what you remember is not evidence.
- The plain sense says what the day's text says, in its own terms. It is not the commentator's reading restated, and it is not doctrine.
- A key verse is copied from `day.js`, never typed from memory: a remembered translation is almost never the app's translation, and five of the chambers' epigraphs prove it.
- The key verse's translation may carry its translator's interpretation (Legge's parentheses, Besant's Theosophy, Pickthall's additions). The reading attributed to the commentator must be his, not the translator's.

## tao

- Wang Bi and the Heshang Gong commentary comment on their own base texts, which differ from each other and in places from Legge's. Check that the line commented on is the line in the day's chapter before building a reading on it.
- The Heshang Gong commentary's chapter titles (for chapter 8, 易性, which Legge renders as the placid and contented nature) are part of that commentary and may be cited as its reading of the chapter.
- The name Heshang Gong is a legend (the Old Man by the River). Name the commentary and nothing about him: no life, no dates, no "he wrote".
- Wang Bi has no commentary on chapters 31 and 66 in the received text. On those days name the Heshang Gong commentary, or Legge's notes where they report a reading, in the "as given in" form.
- Legge's notes are OCR from the 1891 printing: romanisations come out as "Lao-jze", "Tdo", "T&o". Quote evidence exactly as the packet has it, noise included, so the machine recheck finds it; gloss it cleanly.
- Legge's parentheses are words he supplied. They stay in the key verse exactly as printed, and the plain sense does not lean on them.
- Say "the chapter" or "the text". Never "Laozi says" or "Lao Tzu wrote": the authorship is traditional, not known.
- The Heshang Gong commentary is on two Wikisource pages: revision 2019760 holds chapters 1 to 37 and revision 1552800 chapters 38 to 81. Cite the one packet.js names for the day, never the other. (Found in the dry run.)
- On a hard passage the commentary's limit may sit on a line inside the key verse or just after it (chapter 31: the victor mourns, and arms are used only when they cannot be avoided). Read the comment on every line of the key verse and the line after before writing the reading, and carry the limit (rule 6). (Found in the dry run.)
- A phrase can occur twice in a chapter with different comments (chapter 8: 不爭 in 水善利萬物而不爭 and again in 夫唯不爭, glossed differently by the Heshang Gong commentary). A reading of the phrase uses the comment on the line it names. (Found in the dry run.)

## pali

- The commentary is ascribed by tradition to Buddhaghosa: never state the ascription as fact. Name it "the Theravāda commentary".
- Burlingame translates the stories the commentary tells to frame each verse. The reading is the lesson the story draws from the verse, located by book, story, volume and page.
- Müller prints nine pairs of verses as one paragraph: cite the pair as "Dhammapada 58 to 59".

## gita

- Besant was a Theosophist and her English carries it in places. Compare each key verse with Telang (SBE 8, 1882) before building on a word.
- Name the school of Rāmānuja (Viśiṣṭādvaita) and Madhva (Dvaita) whenever either appears, and Śaṅkara's (Advaita) whenever two are named.

## upanishads

- Rāmānuja wrote no commentary on the Isa, Kena or Katha: name him only where the Śrī Bhāṣya treats the verse, and name the Śrī Bhāṣya.
- Paramananda's own commentary is printed under each verse and is never a key verse or a reading.
- Paramananda divides the Katha into parts and sections; the standard numbering follows the same parts. Check the section against Śaṅkara's.

## analects

- Zhu Xi's chapter divisions differ from Legge's in places: locate by the opening words, not the number.
- He Yan's collection gathers earlier glosses under their own authors' names: name the gathered author as the collection gives it, or say "He Yan's collected commentary".
- Legge's text carries "--" before a list and supplied words in parentheses: a key verse holding the "--" is not allowed; choose another sentence.

## zhuangzi

- Giles' arguments (the italic summary at a chapter's head) and his notes are printed in the text but are neither the Zhuangzi nor Guo Xiang.
- Guo Xiang comments on the 33-chapter recension that Giles follows; locate by opening words.

## veda

- Sāyaṇa is usually reached through Wilson's translation, which follows him: the source line then reads "Sāyaṇa, as given in Wilson's translation".
- Wilson also numbers by aṣṭaka and adhyāya: cite mandala.hymn.verse.
- Griffith leaves some verses in Latin or untranslated: no key verse from those.

## quran

- Paraphrase only: never quote a tafsir in the sentence.
- The Jalalayn has two authors: al-Mahalli on al-Fatiha and surahs 18 to 114, al-Suyuti on 2 to 17.
- The English "Ibn Kathir" online is an abridgement: find the reading in the Arabic.
- Pickthall's parentheses are his additions, and he writes "Allah": a key verse keeps them as printed.

## tanakh

- The commentary on Chronicles printed under Rashi's name is not his.
- The JPS translation prints the divine name as LORD in capitals: a key verse keeps it; a sentence says "the LORD" or "God", and never spells the four-letter name.
- No Christian vocabulary, ever: the gate fails the plan on it.

## bible

- The Catena Aurea's "Pseudo-Chrys." is the Opus imperfectum, not Chrysostom; "Gloss." is the Glossa Ordinaria; Ambrosiaster is not Ambrose.
- Augustine numbers the Psalms as the Greek and Latin Bibles do: his Psalm 22 is the WEB's 23.
- The WEB prints "Yahweh": a key verse keeps it as printed.
- On an Old Testament day a Father's Christian reading is his reading ("Augustine, City of God 16.32, reads it as"), never the plain sense and never a claim about Jews or the Jewish reading.
