## The Zhuangzi, days 1 to 22 (zhuangzi-01)

Forty four candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the twenty two days, in four lanes (days 1 to 6, 7 to 12, 13 to 18 and 19 to 22). All twenty two primaries were judged VERIFIED at first judgement. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the packet source for the key verse’s own line, and on days 10 to 12 and 16 to 21 read the raw wikitext of the cited Wikisource revision as well. One primary was refuted, day 8, on the plain sense alone; it was judged again, CORRECTED, and lived. Five critics then read the whole batch in turn, and their ten problems became twelve revisions over five rounds. Eleven revisions changed an entry and were each attacked again by a refuter: one was refuted and ten were cleared. The twelfth (day 11) found that the problem did not hold and returned the entry unchanged. Status now gives twenty two living primaries, and all twenty two survive and are used. The twenty two standbys were never verified, because no primary fell. In all, one candidate was refuted at first refutation (day 8, which lived after correction) and one revision was refuted (the first revision of day 4, which never landed). The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, and none held a straight quote to be made curly.

The spread: Guo Xiang, the only commentator on this plan’s roster, is named on all twenty two days, and no day names two commentators. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from Guo Xiang’s own Chinese in the Nanhua zhenjing zhushu of the Zhengtong Daozang on zh.wikisource, juan 1 to 19, as the packet gives it, each at the revision its source line names, and every refuter confirmed that the note used is Guo Xiang’s and sits on the Chinese of the line the key verse holds. Neither conduit was needed: Legge’s notes and Giles’ notes were not used for any reading, and on days 4, 5 and 8 the refuters checked that the reading was Guo Xiang’s and not Giles’ gloss. Every day gives the place in the same form, “on chapter N”, in the sentence and the source line alike.

The corrected entries. Eight of the twenty two land CORRECTED: day 8 after refutation, and days 4, 9, 14, 19, 20, 21 and 22 by revision. The other fourteen, day 11 among them, land as first VERIFIED. Day 8: the reading held (Guo Xiang’s note on 然而夜半有力者負之而走 gives 夫無力之力，莫大於變化者也；故乃揭天地以趨新 and closes 故向者之我，非復今我也, verbatim the evidence), but the plain sense said the passage imagines the boat stolen, which restated the key verse printed just above it and broke rule 2. The second judgement changed only that clause, to the refuter’s wording, which gives the boat hidden in a creek at 6.62 and the warning later in 6.64 that anything hidden may still be lost; the reading, the key verse and the source line are unchanged, and the evidence was made one unbroken span. The other seven are set out below, round by round.

The first round of revisions, from the first critic, who raised four problems; three were answered in this round. Day 9, cleared: the critic found “darkly one” an obscure rendering of 相冥 and saw that the reading dropped Guo Xiang’s reason for lodging the roaming beyond the bounds in the three friends. The revision reads outside and inside as one (外內相冥), keeps the sage roaming outside to enlarge the inside (故聖人常遊外以弘內), and restores the reason, that said outright of Confucius it would be refused by readers going on what they saw (若直就稱仲尼之如此，或者將據所見以排之，故…寄方外於數子). Plain sense, key verse, place and source line are unchanged. Day 20, cleared: the critic found the old plain sense ambiguous, since its trailing “blaming the times” could be read as the scholars blaming the times, where Giles at 16.27 has the chapter say the age was unsuited to their mission. The critic’s suggested wording brought the sentence to fifty words and entry check refused it, so the reviser cut the plain sense to a clause saying the retired scholars did not hide but met an unsuited age, and the reading was left alone. Day 4, refuted: the critic asked for Guo Xiang’s image of the napper who dreams through a hundred years (世有假寐，而夢經百年者) in place of “the present hundred years cannot be told from a dream”, which also overstated 無以明…非假寐之夢 (it cannot be shown not to be such a dream). The revision wrote that a hundred year life may be one long nap’s dream. The refuter found that this inverts the image: 假寐 is a brief doze, and the length belongs to the dream, not to the nap. That revision never landed, and the refuter’s wording, “the dream of a single doze”, was held for the next round.

The second round of revisions, from the second critic, who found two problems still standing. Day 4, cleared: the sentence was made to read that each state suits its moment, so a hundred year life may be dreamt in a single doze, and grieving at death while alive is an error. It kept the doze brief (假寐) and the claim modal (無以明…非), and removed both the refuted long nap and the overstatement “cannot be told from”; its refuter found 而各適一時之志, the whole napper sentence and 知夫在生而哀死者誤也 verbatim on the key verse’s line and its neighbour. This wording was itself replaced in the fourth round. Days 19, 20, 21 and 22, all cleared: the first critic had observed, and no first round revision had answered, that these four gave the place as Giles’ section numbers (on 14.132, on 16.27, on 17.10, on 17.149) in the sentence and added the same number to the source line, where days 1 to 18 say “on chapter N”. Those numbers are the app’s numbering of Giles, not places in Guo Xiang’s commentary. Each revision changed only the place, to “on chapter 14”, “on chapter 16” and “on chapter 17”, and dropped the number from the source line. On day 20 the extra word would have taken the sentence past the limit, so “rooted deep, he waits” was made the compound adjective, still 深根. Each refuter reopened the packet, found the evidence verbatim under the same revision, and confirmed that the reading, the key verse and the plain sense had not moved.

The third round of revisions, from the third critic, who raised two problems. Day 20, cleared: the critic found that the plain sense from the first round, that the scholars met an unsuited age, restated the key verse’s own opening clause, which is paraphrase and not context under rule 2, and doubled “age” against the reading’s “a busy age” in one sentence. The revision takes the critic’s wording, that the retired scholars of old hid neither body, words nor wisdom, which is what 16.27 says just before the key verse; the reading, key verse, place and source line are unchanged. Its refuter reopened packet.js for day 20 at 16.27 and the live page, found Guo Xiang’s note marked 〔注〕 directly after 則深根寧極而待, 雖有事之世，而聖人未始不澹漠也，故深根寧極而待其自為耳，斯道之所以不喪也, verbatim under revision 6365330, confirmed through the Wikisource API that the revision is 卷十八 of 2026, and found no four words of the key in the new clause. Day 11, unchanged: the critic held that calling Poh Loh famed as a horse tamer went outside the passage and leaned on Giles’ note, and asked for “claiming to understand horses” instead. The reviser found that the problem does not hold: the day’s own text at 9.10, a citable unit in day.js, says every age extols Poh Loh for his skill in managing horses, and 9.6 has him claim to understand their management, so the fame is context from the passage around the key verse, which rule 2 allows, and not from the note. The entry was returned as it was, VERIFIED, and the reviser reopened packet.js for day 11 at 9.7 and found Guo Xiang’s note on 飢之，渴之 … 而馬之死者已過半矣 still carrying the reading verbatim under revision 6355259.

The fourth round of revisions, from the fourth critic, who raised one problem, on day 4 again. Day 4, cleared: the critic found that the second round wording, a hundred year life dreamt in a single doze, read as a remark about how long dreams can seem and dropped Guo Xiang’s point, which is doubt about this present life: 則無以明今之百年，非假寐之夢者也, the present hundred years cannot be shown not to be such a doze’s dream. That doubt is what ties the image to the butterfly and to the clause on grieving at death. The revision now reads that each state suits its moment, so the present life may be a brief doze’s dream, and grieving at death while alive is an error: “the present life” for 今之百年, “a brief doze” for 假寐, and “may be” for 無以明…非, not “is”. The plain sense, key verse, place and source line are unchanged, and the sentence is forty four words. Its refuter reopened packet.js for day 4 at 2.154 and the page itself (南華真經註疏 卷三, revision 6364018, dated June 2026, so the year in the source line holds), found 而各適一時之志 and the whole napper sentence on 不知周之夢為胡蝶與 and 知夫在生而哀死者誤也 on 不知周也 in the same paragraph, and judged that joining them under one “so” does not drift, since the premise of the second, 所在無不適志, is the same idea as 各適一時之志.

The fifth round of revisions, from the fifth critic, who raised one problem, on day 14. Day 14, cleared: the reading had said that things left in chaos to return of themselves never leave their root. The critic found that “chaos” renders Guo Xiang’s 渾沌, which in his note names an undivided, unknowing simplicity (渾沌無知而任其自復), while in plain English it means disorder, and it follows a passage in which the Vital Principle blames government for the confusion of the empire, so the sentence seemed to have Guo Xiang recommend leaving things in disorder, the opposite of his point, and at odds with the key verse’s own “simple purity”. The revision changes that one phrase to “things left unknowing”, which follows 渾沌無知 directly; the plain sense, the rest of the reading, the key verse, the place and the source line are unchanged, and the sentence is forty two words. Its refuter reopened packet.js for day 14 at 11.102 (chapter 11, juan 13, revision 7907032), matched the text lines 各復其根而不知, 渾渾沌沌，終身不離 and 若彼知之，乃是離之 to Giles 11.102 by their opening words, and found Guo Xiang’s notes on them verbatim: 不知而復，乃真復也, 渾沌無知而任其自復，乃能終身不離其本也 and 知而復之，與復乖矣. It judged the causal “for” a light join of two adjacent notes, and found the plain sense faithful to 11.98 to 11.101 and free of any four words of the key. Every entry now stands within the forty five word limit, as entry check counts it.

Reservations the refuters recorded and judged minor, not misstatements. Day 2: the reading leaves out Guo Xiang’s wider claim that all living kinds join in Heaven’s music, but claims nothing beyond him. Day 4: the place “on chapter 2” is right but broad, and the last refuter offered, as optional, “on the butterfly dream at the close of chapter 2” if the word limit allowed; it was not taken, so that the place keeps the form of the other twenty one days. Day 5: “for” is a light causal join between two notes that the text itself makes, and the reading follows Guo’s breath (氣), not Giles’ “soul”. Day 9: dropping “darkly” loses a shade of 冥 but claims nothing beyond it. Day 11: the description of Poh Loh lies close to Giles’ note, but the text’s own praise of him at 9.10 supports it, as the third round confirmed. Day 12: Guo’s closing qualifier, that it would be better still if all knowing were gone (未若都亡之無害), is omitted, and “lawlessness” for 無治 slightly sharpens “no order”. Day 17: Guo’s concession that the ruled are called acting because they handle affairs is omitted; the refuter judged the passage one of hierarchy but not a hard passage under rule 6. Day 19: “whose Six Canons no ruler adopted” compresses Confucius’ “adopted my suggestions”, but the suggestions were drawn from the Canons (一君無所鉤用). Day 21: “belittle” is fair, since Guo’s next line has the great calling the small insufficient. Day 22: “anyone but a fish” generalises a little, following Hui Tzŭ’s own inference at 17.148 and Guo’s summary 非魚則無緣相知.

The holes. There are none: all twenty two days are used.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it, the candidate gate joins this batch to the set already landed (days 23 to 44, from the second batch of this plan) and passes on forty four days with no error and no warning. No day had to be withdrawn.

Traps. Ten observations may belong in traps.md. First, the Nanhua zhenjing zhushu pages interleave Cheng Xuanying’s subcommentary (疏) with Guo Xiang’s notes (注); only a line marked 〔注〕 is Guo’s. On day 18 the plainest statement that nothing makes the heavens move, 無物使之然也, is Cheng’s, and the sentence stands only because Guo’s own 皆自爾 answers the same question; on days 16, 19 and 20 the refuters checked the 〔注〕 mark line by line for the same reason. Second, the packet’s alignment of Chinese lines to Giles’ numbering is approximate: on day 9 the Chinese of the key verse at 6.148 is labelled 6.145, and on day 15 the Chinese of the key verse’s second sentence at 12.66 is labelled 12.68, so the plan’s rule to locate by opening words holds for the packet as well as for the paragraph numbers. Third, the juan do not follow the chapters: chapter 2 runs into juan 3 from 2.74 and chapter 6 into juan 8 from 6.103, so the revision cited must be the juan that holds the key verse, not the one that opens the chapter. Fourth, Giles’ notes carry his own readings (“Metempsychosis” beside the butterfly dream, “our mortal coil” beside the strong man, “soul” for 氣), and each must be kept off Guo Xiang. Fifth, from days 8 and 20: a plain sense that tells what happens in the key verse, or restates its opening clause in other words, is a paraphrase of it even when it shares no four words with it and the gate accepts it; the context must come from the units around the verse. Sixth, from day 4: 假寐 is a doze, not a long sleep, and Guo’s image sets a dream of a hundred years inside a brief nap; a gloss that lengthens the nap inverts it. Seventh, also from day 4: an image kept without its point can still mislead; Guo’s napper exists to cast doubt on the present life (今之百年), and a paraphrase that keeps the doze and drops 今 turns his argument into a remark about dreams, which took two rounds to see. Eighth, from days 19 to 22: Giles’ section numbers are the app’s, not places in Guo Xiang’s commentary, so the place is the chapter; one lane used the section number, the gate accepted it, and only a reader of the whole batch caught it. Ninth, from day 11: before calling a detail of the plain sense a borrowing from Giles’ note, search the day’s own citable units, since the text may say the same thing elsewhere in the passage (Poh Loh’s fame at 9.10). Tenth, from day 14: a single English word can reverse a commentator’s term; Guo Xiang’s 渾沌 is undivided, unknowing simplicity, and “chaos” in plain English reads as the disorder the passage condemns, so a gloss of a technical term must be checked against the sense the English word carries on its own. Three smaller observations: a critic’s suggested wording must itself go through entry check before a reviser takes it, since on day 20 two such suggestions would have broken the forty five word limit; on day 5 the transcription carries a stray 他 after Guo’s note, which was kept out of the evidence; and on day 14 the key verse opens with Giles’ unclosed quotation mark, which verse.js accepts as printed.
