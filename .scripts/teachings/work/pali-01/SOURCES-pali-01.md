## The Dhammapada, days 1 to 26 (pali-01)

Fifty two candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the twenty six days, in nine lanes; the Dhammapada is read in Max Müller’s translation (Sacred Books of the East 10, 1881), a chapter a day. All twenty six primaries were verified: twenty three as VERIFIED at first judgement and three (days 24, 25 and 26) as CORRECTED. Each primary was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the packet source. Three were refuted (days 15, 24 and 26); each was judged a second time, CORRECTED, and lived. A critic then read the whole batch three times, and sixteen revisions followed in three rounds, each attacked by a second refuter; one revision was refuted (the first attempt on day 25) and fifteen were cleared. Status gives twenty six living primaries, and all twenty six are used. The twenty six standbys were never verified, because no primary fell. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, revised where a revision was cleared, and none held a straight quote to be made curly. The candidate gate passes with twenty six days, no error and no warning.

The spread. The roster for this plan has one commentator, and the Theravāda commentary is named on all twenty six days; no day names two. Seventeen readings are taken from the commentary’s own Pali explanation of the verse, in the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana text of the Vipassana Research Institute (s0502a.att.xml, commit 60c7fb3), with the verifier’s gloss: days 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 19, 20, 21, 22 and 24. Nine are taken from the story that frames the verse, as Burlingame translates it in Buddhist Legends (Harvard Oriental Series 28 to 30, 1921), which is the commentary’s own and so no hedge: days 4, 6, 8, 16, 17, 18, 23, 25 and 26. No entry is hedged, and Müller’s notes were used only as a check (on day 15 his note confirms that the commentator takes saṅkhāra as the five aggregates). The ascription of the commentary to Buddhaghosa is stated nowhere.

The CORRECTED entries at first judgement. Day 24: the proposal said four things are each named above all their kind, but the fourth clause of verse 354 sets the extinction of thirst over all pain, not over its kind; the verifier rewrote the plain sense and let the reading refer to the law’s gift. Day 25: “praise of” a former ploughman became “spoken for” him, since in story XXV.10 the Teacher answers a charge of falsehood and states the monk’s attainment, and “rebuked his discontent” became that he admonished himself whenever discontent came, which is the story’s own act of self by self; the plain sense was tightened to stay within forty five words. Day 26: “Assaji, his first teacher” is not what story XXVI.9 says, since Sariputta had an earlier teacher and only first heard the Law from Assaji, and the clause was reworded to say so.

The refutations and the second judgements. Day 15 was refuted on its plain sense and for drift. The plain sense said the chapter turns to hunger and the body at verse 203, but verse 202 already names the body, and the clause restated the key verse’s own nouns rather than giving context. The reading took the whole verse through story XV.5, the poor man whom the Teacher fed before preaching, but that story speaks only to the first line, on hunger; the commentary’s own lesson on the whole verse is its Pali explanation of 203. At the second judgement the plain sense became context only (the chapter on happiness, after the verse on passion, ending on Nirvana), and the reading, the source line and the evidence moved to the Pali: hunger is the chief of diseases because other illnesses pass once treated while hunger must always be treated, and the body is the five aggregates. Day 24 was refuted a second time on its plain sense, which still said the verse sets the law and thirst’s end above all and so summarised the key verse; at the second judgement it became context from verse 353, where one freed by the destruction of thirst asks whom he shall teach, and the chapter then praises the law. “Spoonful” became “ladleful” at the same time, so that the sentence agrees with uḷuṅka in the Pali and with the gloss. Day 26 was refuted for drift: its corrected sentence said Sariputta reverenced whatever quarter Assaji stayed in, which is the very charge the monks bring and the Teacher denies in story XXVI.9; the Teacher says Sariputta reverences his own teacher. At the second judgement the clause became that Sariputta raised joined hands towards wherever Assaji stayed, which the story states, and “his fire rite” became “the fire rite”, since the rite belongs to the ritual Brahmana and not to the chapter’s true one.

The first revisions, after the critic’s first reading. Day 19: the reading had the commentary take the balance as weighing, which is circular, since a balance is for weighing, and left the image apart from its point. The revision reads the sage as a weigher at the scales, removing evil like an excess and adding the wholesome where short, which is the Pali simile on 268 to 269 word for word (tulaṃ gahetvā ṭhito atirekaṃ ce hoti, harati; ūnaṃ ce hoti, pakkhipati); the plain sense lost “of honour” to stay within the cap. Day 20: the reading said the Teacher was stopping new monks felling real trees, but the Pali on 283 says only that a wish to cut trees arose in the newly ordained (rukkhaṃ chinditukāmatā uppajji) and that he checked it; the revision has him checking new monks who took him to mean trees, since he meant the forest of defilements such as lust, and the plain sense was shortened to “Between verses on zeal and love’s bond” to stay within forty five words. Day 21, a hard passage: the reading carried mother, father and the two kings as the commentary reads them but dropped the verse’s widest killing, the kingdom with all its subjects, leaving that line to be read literally; the revision adds the kingdom as the sense bases (dvādasāyatanāni … raṭṭhaṃ nāma) and that all are slain by the arahat path, while the plain sense still states the killing in one plain clause. Day 23: only the source line changed, from the cryptic “Buddhist Legends 3 (1921), at Norman 4.2” to the batch form, “Buddhist Legends, vol. 3 (1921), Norman vol. 4, pp. 2 to 3”, with the page range as opened, since the Ānanda exchange crosses the Norman page mark 4.3. The second refuter on day 23 noted that the Pali gloss on the verse’s own words (adhivāsanaṃ ajjhupekkhanameva mama bhāro) would serve equally well, if the owner prefers the lesson drawn from the verse itself to the story; that is noted, not applied.

The second revisions, after the critic’s second reading, which bring the source lines and the verse places to one form. None of them touched a key, a reading or the evidence words; each reopened the packet and found the evidence where it was. Days 7 and 9 named the Pali edition as “GitHub commit 60c7fb3”, and days 13 and 14 named it by the repository before the commit; all four now read “the Pali, Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana edition (VRI), commit 60c7fb3 (2023)”, as every other Pali day does. Days 4 and 6 named Burlingame as “part 2” with no page; both now read “Buddhist Legends, vol. 2 (1921)” with the Norman page on which the Teacher’s words stand, p. 374 for story IV.5 and p. 106 for story VI.1, each found between the inline page marks Burlingame prints. Day 26: the source line gained its place, “Norman vol. 4, pp. 150 to 151”, keeping Norman’s volume apart from Burlingame’s as day 23 does; the refuter confirmed the page mark in the Pali as well as in the story. Days 20 and 21: the sentence and the source line said “on Dhammapada 283” and “on Dhammapada 294” for a single verse, and now say “on verse 283” and “on verse 294”, keeping “Dhammapada N to M” for the pairs Müller prints, as on day 19. Day 25 was revised to add “Norman pp. 115 to 116” to its source line, and that revision was refuted. The refuter found the page right but the sentence wrong in form: in “admonished himself beneath the tree where his old rags hung whenever discontent came”, the last clause attaches to the nearest verb, so a reader meets rags that hung only when discontent came, which the story does not say; its “whenever” governs his going to the tree and admonishing himself.

The third revisions, after the critic’s third reading, which raised two days. Day 25: the critic found that the refuted form still stood in the batch and asked for the refuter’s reorder with the Norman pages. The revision moves the clause beside the verb it governs (a former ploughman who, whenever discontent came, admonished himself beneath the tree where his old rags hung) and gives the source line “Norman pp. 115 to 116”, the page marks printed in the story around the reading; the evidence words are unchanged, and the second refuter reopened story XXV.10, found them verbatim between the marks [115] and [116], and cleared it. Day 13: the critic found that the reading gave the commentary’s reason in terms of the stream enterer without ever saying that the commentary takes the verse’s reward as the fruit of stream entry (sotāpattiphalameva varaṃ uttamanti attho), so a reader had no link between the key verse and the reason, and that “a world ruler” narrowed the commentary’s “one who has exercised rule over so great a domain” (ettake ṭhāne rajjaṃ kāretvāpi), which covers earth, heaven and all worlds. The revision names the identification first and then the reason, with “a ruler of so much”; the key, the source line and the evidence words are unchanged, and the second refuter reopened the Pali on 178, found the words verbatim, and cleared it, noting that story XIII.11 agrees and confirms the locator.

The holes. There are none: all twenty six days are used.

Two things for the operator. The machine recheck of the evidence is recorded and finds all twenty six FOUND, but it was taken before the second and third revisions; those changed no evidence words (on days 13 and 25 the words were compared and are the same), only sentences, locators and edition labels, so it should hold, and a rerun before landing costs little. And the source lines are now alike but for one: day 8 reads “Buddhist Legends (1921), vol. 2, Norman p. 225” where the others put the volume before the year; no critic reading raised it, and it is not wrong.

Traps. Six belong in traps.md. From day 26: a Burlingame story often reports what the monks say of an elder and then gives the Teacher’s correction; the reading is the Teacher’s answer, never the monks’ report, so read to the end of the story before attributing what an elder did. From day 15: a story may frame only one line of a verse; when the reading is of the whole verse, take it from the Pali explanation of that verse, and when it is from the story, say that it frames the line it frames. From day 24: a plain sense that ranks or lists what the key verse ranks or lists is the verse restated, not context, and it tends to misstate one member of the list; give the verse’s place in the chapter instead, and keep the English of the sentence in step with the gloss of the evidence. From day 21: when the commentary reads every line of a hard verse figuratively, the reading carries every line it reads, or the line left out stands to be read literally beside the rest. From day 25: a “whenever” or “while” clause placed at the end of a relative clause attaches to the nearest verb; put it beside the verb it governs, and when a revision is opened for one field, read the whole sentence again, since a refuter will. From day 13: when the commentary first names what a phrase of the verse stands for and then gives its reason, the reading carries the naming as well as the reason, or the reason hangs from a term the verse never uses.
