## The Tanakh, days 1 to 30 (tanakh-01)

Sixty candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the thirty days (Genesis 1 to Exodus 39), in six lanes (days 1 to 4 and 6; 7 to 10 and 12; 13 to 17; 18 to 22; 23 to 27; and 28 to 30) and one lane for the sensitive days (5 and 11). All thirty primaries were judged at first judgement: twenty nine VERIFIED and one CORRECTED, day 10. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the commentator’s Hebrew through packet.js on the key verse, found the evidence words verbatim, checked the context clause against day.js and ran verse.js and the entry check again. Two primaries were refuted, days 5 and 6, each on its context clause and not on its reading; each was judged again with the refutation in hand, CORRECTED, and lived. A critic then read the thirty living entries and held two, days 7 and 12; each was revised, the revision was attacked by a second refuter, and neither revision was refuted, so both revised entries stand. Status gives thirty living primaries, all thirty are used, and there are no holes. The thirty standbys were never verified, because no primary died. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry used is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, the revised wording on days 7 and 12 included, and no key, sentence or source line held a straight quote to be made curly.

The spread. Rashi is named on nineteen of the thirty days, twelve of them alone (1, 7, 12, 13, 17, 18, 20, 23, 24, 27, 28 and 29) and seven beside a second commentator. Ramban is named on eleven days, seven alone (3, 6, 8, 11, 19, 21 and 26) and four as the second voice beside Rashi (2, 4, 10 and 14). Rashbam is named on two days, both beside Rashi (9 and 15). Ibn Ezra is named on two days, alone on day 22 and beside Rashi on day 30. The Midrash is named on two days, by collection and section: Genesis Rabbah 49:9 on day 5 and Exodus Rabbah 33:1 on day 25. Sforno is named on one day, 16. Radak and Metzudat David are not named; Metzudat David has no coverage in the Torah, and on no key verse of these days did Radak’s comment on Genesis offer a fuller or differing reading. Seven days name two commentators (2, 4, 9, 10, 14, 15 and 30), and on each the difference is named and not resolved. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own Hebrew in Sefaria’s export at commit 3f10136, reached through packet.js, and the plan has no conduits. The candidate gate warns that Rashi holds nineteen of the thirty days; the warning is the brief’s default order at work and not an error.

The corrected entry at first judgement. Day 10, on Genesis 32:11, Rashi and Ramban: Rashi reads Jacob’s unworthiness as merit diminished by the kindnesses already shown, so that a sin since the promise might deliver him to Esau, and Ramban quotes Rashi, says it does not fit the language of the verse, and reads katonti as being too small to be worthy of all the kindnesses. The proposal had Ramban reading it as too small ever to have deserved them; the word ever is not in his words (קטון הוא מהיותו ראוי לכל החסדים), so the clause now reads too small to be worthy of them. Nothing else changed.

The corrected entries after refutation. Day 5, on Genesis 18:25, a sensitive day: the refuter found the reading sound, Rabbi Levi in Genesis Rabbah 49:9 (placed on 18:24 to 18:25, the same saying recurring at 39:6) arguing that if God seeks a world there can be no strict justice, and unless He yields a little the world cannot stand. What broke was rule 2: the context clause said the LORD prepares to destroy Sodom and Gomorrah, but in 18:20 to 18:21 the LORD says only that He will go down and see whether they have done according to the cry; destruction is announced only by the messengers at 19:13, and Abraham pleads for the city, not the cities. The second judgement changed the clause to say that the LORD resolves to see whether Sodom and Gomorrah have done as their outcry says, and that Abraham pleads for the city; the reading, the place and the source line are unchanged, and the evidence was recopied character for character from the packet line, since the niqqud had been copied in another order. Day 6, on Genesis 22:1: the refuter found Ramban’s reading sound (a trial is so called from the side of the one tried, whose deeds are in his free power; God commands in order to bring the deed from potential into act, so that he has the reward of a good deed and not of a good heart alone; every trial in the Torah is for the good of the one tried), but found two faults in the context clause and one in the reading. The day’s text, Genesis 20 to 23, never names Ishmael; it says the son of Hagar, the lad and the son of the bondwoman, so the clause now says Hagar’s son. The binding is a hard passage and 22:2 commands a burnt offering, so offer Isaac up, which dropped what the offering is, became offer Isaac as a burnt offering, as rule 6 asks. The words his readiness were not in Ramban, who says the thing (הדבר), and were dropped. The second judgement took the refuter’s wording, forty five words, and replaced the evidence with one contiguous span from the packet.

The revisions. The critic held two entries, and both were revised, both revisions CORRECTED, and both survived a second refuter who reopened the packet. Day 7, on Genesis 24:67, Rashi: the sentence had said that Rashi reads it as Rebekah becoming like Sarah, but the only thing named before the semicolon was Isaac meeting the caravan, so the word it had no antecedent and a reader could not tell what was being read. Rashi’s comment is headed on the words into the tent, Sarah his mother (האהלה שרה אמו), and the revision names them, quoting two words of the key verse, ‘Sarah’s tent’, in curly quotes: Rashi reads Sarah’s tent as Rebekah becoming like Sarah, the lamp burning from Sabbath eve to Sabbath eve, the blessing in the dough and the cloud over the tent returning with her. The reading itself is unchanged, the evidence now carries the comment’s heading verbatim, and the second refuter confirmed that Rashi marks the saying as from Genesis Rabbah but adopts it in his own comment, so naming Rashi is not a false attribution. Day 12, on Genesis 37:4, Rashi: the sentence had said that Rashi reads the brothers’ discredit as their praise, which goes beyond his words; he says that from within their discredit we learn their praise (מִתּוֹךְ גְּנוּתָם לָמַדְנוּ שִׁבְחָם), so the hatred stays a discredit and the praise is learned from it. The critic proposed finds within, which is not one of the verbs rule 1 allows, so the revision kept a permitted verb and rendered his word for learning: Rashi reads the brothers’ discredit as teaching their praise, that they did not say one thing with the mouth and another in the heart. The second refuter found the second clause to be his words and no drift.

Points the verifiers recorded and judged sound. On four days the commentator’s comment stands at a verse other than the key verse, and the place cites where it stands. Day 3: Ramban’s single long comment at 9:12 expounds the words of 9:13 (My bow I have set in the cloud), and the packet has nothing of his at 9:13; the inverted bow is first given as what others said and then adopted in his own voice. Day 8: Ramban’s comment headed with the name Esek at 26:20 runs on through Rehoboth and the words of 26:22, and he has nothing at 26:22; he frames the three wells as an allusion to the Temples. Day 11, a sensitive day: the key verse is 34:25, the killing, and Ramban’s long comment at 34:13 sets aside another view, grants that the townsmen were idolaters, and says that judging them was not given over to Jacob and his sons, that the brothers did violence to men who had trusted their word and killed them for nothing, since they had done them no harm; the sentence keeps the limit he sets and does not call the townsmen innocent. Day 25: the packet’s (see Exodus Rabbah 33:1, printed under Exodus 25:2) line points to a paragraph that opens on 25:2 and closes by citing 25:8 as the proof of its parable, so it is named by collection and section as the roster requires. On day 3 Ramban also gives an esoteric reading of the bow, and the sentence does not claim his plain reading is his only one.

The holes. There are none.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it on the full batch of thirty days, with the revised entries for days 7 and 12 in place, the candidate gate passes with no error and one warning, that Rashi holds nineteen of the thirty days.

Traps. These observations may belong in traps.md. From day 6: the context clause names people as the day’s text names them; a name the reader knows from elsewhere in the book (Ishmael, where Genesis 20 to 23 says only the son of Hagar or the lad) is outside the passage’s own terms, and a search of day.js for the name settles it. From day 5: a context clause must not settle what the text leaves open; before Abraham’s plea the LORD announces an inquiry, not a destruction, and the verdict arrives only later in the day’s passage. From days 3, 8 and 11: in the Torah packets Ramban often writes one long comment headed at an earlier verse that expounds the key verse or the whole episode; run packet.js with its verse option on the verses before the key verse too, and cite the verse the comment is headed at. From day 25: a Midrash Rabbah paragraph printed on an earlier verse can close on the key verse as its proof text; follow the packet’s see line and cite the collection and section, not the verse it is printed under. From day 5: Hebrew evidence copied from the packet can differ only in the order of the niqqud, which a machine recheck will not forgive; copy it from the packet output character for character. From day 6: evidence spliced with an ellipsis was replaced with one contiguous span, which is the safer form wherever one span carries the reading. From day 7: because the key verse is printed above the sentence, a pronoun after the semicolon that points into the key verse rather than back to the context clause leaves the reader guessing; name the words the commentator reads, up to three of the key verse in curly quotes. From day 12: Rashi’s formula that from one thing we learn another (למדנו) makes the first the ground of the second, not the same thing; reads it as teaching keeps that structure where reads it as does not.
