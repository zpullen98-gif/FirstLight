## The Tao Te Ching, days 28 to 54 (tao-02)

Fifty four candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the twenty seven days, in five lanes. All twenty seven primaries were verified: twenty three as VERIFIED at first judgement and four as CORRECTED (days 29, 41, 44 and 53). Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the packet source, and on days 37 to 39 fetched the raw Wikisource revision as well; none was refuted. A critic then read the batch as a reader would, in two runs: the first raised three problems, on days 37, 40 and 45, and the second one more, on day 31. Each of the four went through a revision, each revision was attacked again by a refuter who reopened the source, and none was refuted, so all four revised entries stand. Status gives twenty seven living primaries and all twenty seven are used. The twenty seven standbys were never verified, because no primary fell. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, the four revised ones included, and none held a straight quote to be made curly.

The spread: the Heshang Gong commentary is named on fourteen days (28, 29, 31, 34, 37, 39, 42, 43, 44, 47, 48, 51, 53 and 54) and Wang Bi on thirteen (30, 32, 33, 35, 36, 38, 40, 41, 45, 46, 49, 50 and 52). With the other batches of this plan, which name each on twenty seven days, the Heshang Gong commentary is now named on forty one days and Wang Bi on forty. No day names two commentators. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own Chinese on zh.wikisource, Wang Bi from revision 2354026 (the Huating Zhang edition, 2024) and the Heshang Gong commentary from revision 2019760 (chapters 1 to 37, a normalised text, 2021) for days 28 to 37 and from revision 1552800 (chapters 38 to 81, a normalised text, 2019) for days 39 to 54, each the page packet.js names for the day, and every refuter confirmed that the base line commented on is the line of the day’s key verse. Day 31 names the Heshang Gong commentary because Wang Bi has no comment on that chapter. Legge’s notes were used only as a check: on days 43 and 54 they independently report the same comment, and on day 36 they reject Wang Bi’s reading, which the sentence attributes to Wang Bi alone.

The corrected entries. On day 29 the proposal read the putting away of the three excesses as by itself letting the world transform itself, but the commentary joins it to keeping the mean and practising non action (處中和，行無為), so the reading became “whose putting away, with non action, lets the world transform itself”; “finery in dress and food” also became “fine dress and food” for 服飾飲食. On day 41 the proposal said things are completed by the Tao “without seeing its form”, which made the things the ones who fail to see; Wang Bi’s 不見其成形 says the form of its completing is not seen, so the clause now reads “yet its form goes unseen”. On day 44 the proposal said the commentary takes lasting long as an unwearied spirit, an identity its words do not make; they give an unwearied spirit in governing the self and undisturbed people in governing the state as the ground (故) of lasting long, and the reading now says lasting long is won by these. On day 53 “dread of deliberate measures that lose the Tao’s intent”, which could be read as some measures only, became “dread of any deliberate measure, lest the Tao’s intent be lost”, after 獨畏有所施為，恐失道意; to stay within forty five words “turning on” became “decrying” and “the chapter’s speaker” became “the speaker”. In each the key verse, its place, the commentator and the source line were unchanged.

The revisions. All four came from the critic and all four changed the wording only; in each the key verse, its place, the commentator and the source line are unchanged, and each passed verse.js, entry check and a fresh refutation. On day 31, a hard passage on killing in war, the Heshang Gong comment on the weeping line is 傷己德薄，不能以道化人，而害無辜之民, grief that one’s own virtue is thin, that it could not transform people by the Tao and so harmed innocent people; the entry kept the first half and dropped 而害無辜之民, so the grief lost its object and the reading came out softer than the commentary on the very line that names the killing, against rule 6. The reading now says the weeping is grief that thin virtue, unable to transform by the Tao, harmed the innocent; the second half, the victor’s white mourning as fitting one who strikes only of necessity, still rests on 素服而哭之 and 不得以而誅不祥 and keeps the commentary’s limit, and the plain sense became “the dead of war” to stay plain within forty five words. The evidence now carries both comments. On day 37 the plain sense said only that the closing verse concludes the chapter, which tells the reader nothing; it now says the verse closes a chapter on kings keeping the Tao and meets desire with simplicity, which is what the verse does in answer to the unit before it, where desire rises against the nameless simplicity. The critic’s own wording (“princes and kings who might keep the Tao”) measured forty six words, so it was shortened to “kings keeping the Tao”. The Heshang Gong reading (言侯王鎮撫以道德，民亦將不欲) is untouched. On day 40 the last clause, on being working through non being, read on first sight as a progressive verb and lost Wang Bi’s point 有以無為用, that being takes non being as its use; the close was recast to render that phrase plainly, “the noble rooted in the humble” became “the noble on the humble” to run parallel with “the high founded on the low”, and “a brief chapter” became “a chapter” to stay within forty five words. On day 45 the plain sense again said only that the verse concludes, and the reading ended on “the great things named above”, lines the reader does not see beside the key verse; the plain sense now says the verse makes stillness the rule, and the reading ends on “the chapter’s great things”, which is Wang Bi’s 如上諸大 and is anchored in the plain sense’s great things that seem their opposites. The critic’s wording here also measured forty six words, so “draws a rule from stillness” became “makes stillness the rule”.

Reservations the refuters recorded and judged minor, not misstatements: on day 29 the reading leaves out 處中和; on day 30 the word “only” before relieving distress comes from Wang Bi’s comment on an earlier line of the same chapter (趣以濟難而已矣), not from the comment on the key line; on day 31 “fitting” loosely renders 明 (makes plain), the comment’s framing of the rite as ancient practice (古者) and its grief over the endless wars of later ages are left out, and “the dead of war” is plain but a little impersonal beside a key verse that states the killing directly; on day 32 the skilful are dropped from Wang Bi’s list; on day 40 the reading leaves Wang Bi’s comment on the weakness line unused but claims nothing about it; on day 41 the phrase “hidden and nameless” follows “its work” and could be read as describing the work rather than the Tao; on day 45 “agitation” renders Wang Bi’s 躁, which Legge prints as constant action in the line before the key verse; on day 48 “the Tao” elides the commentary’s own gloss of it as 自然之道. The refuters’ optional wordings for days 30, 41 and 48 were not applied, since an entry may change only through a revision, and any of them may be taken up in one.

The holes. There are none: all twenty seven days are used.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it, the candidate gate now reads eighty one days, the other batches of this plan having landed, and passes with no error and no warning. No day of this batch had to be withdrawn.

Traps. One belongs in traps.md, from day 31: on a hard passage the commentator’s grief or blame has an object, and a reading that keeps the grief and drops what it is for (there, the innocent harmed, 而害無辜之民) softens the commentary as surely as one that drops its limit; read every clause of the comment on the line that names the killing and carry its object as well as its limit. It stands beside the trap from the third batch of this plan on a limit spread across two comments, and like that one it was missed by every pass before the critic. A second belongs there from day 44: where a comment gives the ground of a line (故, therefore), the reading must say the line follows from it, never that the commentator “takes” the line “as” it; “takes A as B” states a gloss, and a ground stated as a gloss is an identity the commentator did not make. A third belongs there from the revisions of days 37 and 45: a plain sense that says the verse closes, ends or concludes the chapter, and nothing more, restates the verse’s place and gives no context; it must say what the verse does in its passage. With it goes a fourth, from day 45: a reading that points to “the things named above” sends the reader to lines not printed beside the key verse, so it must name them or anchor them in the plain sense. Two further cases confirm the observation left open in the first batch, that where Legge’s English and the commentator’s gloss part, the plain sense stays with the chapter and the reading gives the commentator’s sense as his: day 53, where Legge renders 施 as display and the Heshang Gong commentary as 施為, deliberate enacting, and day 36, where Legge’s notes reject Wang Bi’s reading of the state’s instruments as punishment. With three cases now, it may stand in traps.md as a trap. The base texts again decided the choice of commentator on some days, as the existing trap warns: on day 34 Wang Bi reads 以其終不自為大 where the Heshang Gong text has 是以聖人終不為大, Legge’s “Hence the sage”; on day 51 Wang Bi reads 亭之毒之 where the Heshang Gong text has 成之孰之, nearer Legge’s “completes them, matures them”; on day 46 Wang Bi’s text lacks the 車 behind Legge’s carts, and the reading was kept off the carts.
