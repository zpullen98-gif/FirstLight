## The Zhuangzi, days 23 to 44 (zhuangzi-02)

Forty four candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the twenty two days, in four lanes (days 23 to 28, 29 to 34, 35 to 40 and 41 to 44). All twenty two primaries were judged at first judgement: twenty VERIFIED and two CORRECTED, days 24 and 25. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the packet source for the key verse’s own line, and on most days read the raw wikitext of the cited Wikisource revision as well; on days 38 to 40 the refuter also fetched the revision’s url directly. No primary was refuted. A critic then read the twenty two entries as a reader would and raised three problems of prose, on days 29, 33 and 41; each went to a revision, each revision was CORRECTED by its reviser and attacked again by a second refuter who reopened the packet for the cited revision, and none was refuted, so all three revised entries were kept. Status gives twenty two living primaries, and all twenty two survive and are used. The twenty two standbys were never verified, because no primary fell. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor (for days 24 and 25 the entry as corrected at judgement, for days 29, 33 and 41 the entry as revised), and none held a straight quote to be made curly.

The spread: Guo Xiang, the only commentator on this plan’s roster, is named on all twenty two days, and no day names two commentators. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from Guo Xiang’s own Chinese in the Nanhua zhenjing zhushu of the Zhengtong Daozang on zh.wikisource, juan 20 to 35, each at the revision its source line names, and every refuter confirmed that the note used is marked 〔注〕, Guo Xiang’s, and not the 〔疏〕 of Cheng Xuanying. Neither conduit was needed: Legge’s notes and Giles’ notes were not used for any reading.

The corrected entries. Day 24, at first judgement: the reading held (Guo Xiang’s three notes on 不開人之天，而開天之天, 開天者德生 and 開人者賊生 give 開天者，性之動也；開人者，知之用也 and the rest, verbatim the evidence), but the plain sense was a participial fragment with no subject and said the verse closes Kuan Yin’s answer, though a further sentence follows in it; it now places the verse late in Kuan Yin’s answer to Lieh Tzŭ, where two kinds of knowing are contrasted. The reading had given its two glosses in the reverse of the verse’s order; it now names each (the one from God, the artificial) so the mapping cannot be mistaken. Key, ref, at and source line are unchanged. Day 25, at first judgement: the reading held (Guo Xiang’s note after 悲夫。弟子志之，其唯道德之鄉乎 says 不可必，故待之不可以一方也，唯與時俱化者，為能涉變而常通耳), but the plain sense said the disciple asks why the tree lived and the goose died, when he asks on which horn of the dilemma Chuang Tzŭ will rest; it now reads that he is pressed by a disciple over the useless tree spared and the useless goose killed, and to stay within forty five words “what is never certain” became “the uncertain”. Key, ref, at and source line are unchanged.

The revisions. There were three, all on the critic’s first report, all on the prose of the sentence alone; in each the key, ref, at, commentator and source line are unchanged, and the reading still rests on the same note of Guo Xiang’s. Day 29: the plain sense said Chuang Tzŭ “explains his descent”, which a reader seeing only the key verse could take for ancestry or a fall; it now says he “defends going so low”, which the passage bears in Tung Kuo Tzŭ’s question why he goes so low, and the reviser added to the evidence Guo Xiang’s note on 每下愈況 (今問道之所在，而每況之於下賤，則明道之不逃於物也必矣) to show the going low is his subject too. Day 33: the reading said “his work helps things and people give it that name”, which first parses as helping things and people and makes the work the one named; Guo Xiang’s 故人與之名 says people give the Sage the name, so it now reads that because his work helps things, people give him that name. Day 41: the reading said Guo Xiang “reads the chapter as a man at ease”, which makes the chapter a man; his note opens 此篇言, the chapter says, so the word “showing” was inserted: he reads the chapter as showing one at ease without withdrawing to rivers and seas humbling himself before their recluse. The second refuters confirmed the evidence verbatim at revisions 7907027, 5978511 and 6011291, and entry check and verse check pass on all three.

Reservations the verifiers and refuters recorded and judged minor, not misstatements. Day 37 was verified with medium confidence: Guo Xiang has no note on the Yen Ho episode and only three notes on chapter 28, so the entry uses his closing note on the chapter’s broad intent (此篇大意，以起高讓遠退之風) and says so, “closing chapter 28”; that note stands physically at 28.133 to 28.135, in day 38’s text, and goes on to name the faults of the winds of 許由 and 伯夷, which the sentence omits without claiming anything beyond it. Day 40: the evidence, 此章言知足者常足, is nine characters, under the brief’s floor of twelve, but it is Guo Xiang’s whole note on the exchange and cannot be lengthened without taking in Cheng Xuanying’s words; the machine recheck matches it. Day 34: the note sits on the prince’s answer at 25.58, just after the key verse, and the entry’s place says only chapter 25, which is right. Day 41: Guo Xiang’s only 〔注〕 on chapter 31 stands after 吾敢不敬乎 at 31.66, so “his one note on chapter 31” is the exact place; 間 is read as 閒, as in chapter 15. Day 42: the moral runs on from 32.33 to 32.39, so “closing the tale” is defensible rather than exact; Giles’ printed note says something similar, but Guo Xiang’s own words (報其性，不報其為也) are found on the line, so the attribution stands. Day 43: the key stops before the dash in the fourth sentence, and the true Sage is covered in the reading; Giles’ note on four denominations of the ideal man is close to the reading, but again Guo Xiang’s own words (凡此四名，一人耳，所自言之異) stand at the line. Day 44: the sentence folds Guo Xiang’s own former judgement that Chuang Tzŭ was a dialectician into what the hearsay did, a compression that keeps his logic and adds no claim. Day 32: the reading fuses 不動之質 and 忘言之對 into one partner, which is Guo Xiang’s own apposition, and drops 妙斬. Day 30: the “so” joining two points follows his own note, which opens with 死生出入，皆欽然自爾 before concluding there is no gate. Day 33: the mirror is Guo Xiang’s (his note on 25.18 makes 鑑 the mirror), not Giles’ mankind as mirror. Day 36: the same line recurs at 27.7 without “But”; either would pass.

The holes. There are none: all twenty two days are used.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it on the batch as rebuilt after the revisions, the candidate gate passes on twenty two days with no error and no warning. No day had to be withdrawn.

Traps. Six observations may belong in traps.md. First, the packet’s alignment of Chinese lines to Giles’ numbering is again approximate: on day 30 the first two sentences of the key verse at 23.86 are labelled 23.85, and on day 42 the Chinese of the key verse at 32.33 is labelled 32.35; locate by opening words in the packet as in the paragraph numbers. Second, in the later chapters Guo Xiang comments sparsely (three notes on chapter 28, one on the whole of chapter 31, one on the exchange that closes chapter 29, none on chapter 30), so a day may hold no note on its own lines; a chapter note may be used only when the sentence names it as such, and it may stand in another day’s text. Third, the brief’s evidence floor of twelve characters can be longer than a whole note (day 40, nine characters); the whole note verbatim is then the evidence, never padded with Cheng Xuanying’s 疏. Fourth, a note often sits on the line just after the key verse, or on the phrase a line closes (day 25, where 悲夫 carries only 疏; day 34 at 25.58; day 41 at 31.66), so the place in the sentence must name the chapter or the phrase, not claim a note on the verse itself. Fifth, where Giles’ printed note says much what Guo Xiang says (days 42 and 43), the reading stands only because Guo’s own words are found at the line; and Cheng Xuanying’s 疏 sometimes quotes Guo as 郭注云 (day 24), which is a quotation inside the subcommentary, not a second note of Guo’s. Sixth, from the critic: a plain sense or reading that compresses the text into one word (day 29, “descent”) or drops the verb that the commentator’s own 此篇言 supplies (day 41) can pass every gate and still not parse for a reader who sees only the key verse; read each sentence cold, as the reader meets it.
