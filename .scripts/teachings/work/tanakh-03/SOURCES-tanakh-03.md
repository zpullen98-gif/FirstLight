## The Tanakh, days 61 to 90 (tanakh-03)

Sixty candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the thirty days (Deuteronomy 14 to 1 Samuel 16), in five lanes (days 61, 63, 64, 65 and 66; 67, 68, 73, 74 and 75; 76 to 80; 81, 82, 85, 86 and 87; 88 and 89) and four lanes for the sensitive days (62 and 69; 70 and 71; 72 and 83; 84 and 90). All thirty primaries were judged at first judgement: twenty seven VERIFIED and three CORRECTED, days 62, 83 and 84. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the commentator’s Hebrew through packet.js on the key verse, found the evidence words verbatim, checked the context clause against day.js and ran verse.js and the entry check again. One primary was refuted, day 81, on tone and on the naming of its disagreement; it was judged again with the refutation in hand, CORRECTED, and lived. A critic then read the whole batch and raised two problems, on days 70 and 71, both of craft and neither of attribution; each went to a reviser, each revision was CORRECTED, and each was attacked again by a refuter who did not refute it, so status gives the revised entry on both days. Status gives thirty living primaries, and all thirty are used; there is no hole. The thirty standbys were never verified, because no primary died. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, the corrected wording on days 62, 81, 83 and 84 and the revised wording on days 70 and 71 included, and no key, sentence or source line held a straight quote to be made curly. The candidate gate passes with no error.

The spread. Rashi is named on sixteen of the thirty days, nine of them alone (63, 67, 71, 72, 76, 78, 79, 82 and 88) and seven beside a second commentator (61, 62, 64, 69, 80, 81 and 85). Radak is named on thirteen days, alone on nine (68, 70, 73, 74, 84, 86, 87, 89 and 90), first beside Metzudat David on day 77, and as the second voice beside Rashi on three (69, 81 and 85). Metzudat David is named on four days, alone on 75 and 83, and as the second voice on 77 and 80. Ibn Ezra is named on two days, each beside Rashi (61 and 64). Ramban is named on two days, alone on 66 and beside Rashi on 62. Sforno is named once, alone on day 65. Neither Rashbam nor the Midrash is named: on the six Deuteronomy days neither held the fuller comment on the chosen verse, and the Midrash Rabbah has no volume on Joshua, Judges or Samuel. Eight days name two commentators, and on each the difference is named and not resolved. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own Hebrew in Sefaria’s export at commit 3f10136, reached through packet.js, and the plan has no conduits. The candidate gate warns that Rashi holds 59 of the 90 days of the plan so far; the warning is not an error, and this batch leans on Radak and Metzudat David more than the Torah batches could, since from Joshua on they are the only voices beside Rashi.

The corrected entries at first judgement. Day 62, on Deuteronomy 20:10, a sensitive day (the siege laws), Rashi and Ramban: the proposal had Ramban’s two wars differing only after refusal, but Ramban goes on (ונראה שיש הפרש עוד בשאלת השלום) to name a further difference in the terms of peace themselves, so that only went beyond him; the verifier replaced it with his own words, that the section distinguishes the wars only at its end (שהפרשה בסופה תחלק בין שתי המלחמות), and to hold sixty words rendered Rashi’s war of choice as discretionary war, his מלחמת הרשות. The source line now names Ramban’s edition as the packet gives it (On Your Way new). Day 83, on Judges 19:1, a sensitive day, Metzudat David: the reading stood, a king having punished the sinners and spared Israel civil war, in his own words on ומלך אין בישראל. The context clause was corrected: it had said the story ends with the concubine abused to death, but chapter 19 ends at 19:29 to 30 with the dividing of her body and the outcry, and the day’s text does not itself state her death (at 19:28 none answered; her death is stated only at 20:5, which belongs to the next day). The new clause says only what the chapter says: the Levite gives his concubine up to the men of Gibeah, who abuse her all night. Day 84, on Judges 20:21, a sensitive day, Radak: his comment on וישחיתו בישראל opens with אמרו, so the reading is the sages’, which he reports and endorses, and the closing line on God’s honour and human honour is their saying; the verifier added after the sages to the reading and, to hold forty five words, tightened the context clause to Israel losing the first battle over the concubine raped and murdered at Gibeah.

The corrected entry after refutation. Day 81, on Judges 11:39, Rashi and Radak: both halves were found verbatim, Rashi on ותהי חק (neither Jephthah nor Phinehas, who could release the vow, would go to the other, they stood on their greatness, and between them she was lost, ומבין שניהם אבדה) and Radak on ויעש לה את נדרו (a house where she was set apart from people). The refuter held that rendering אבדה as lost left the disagreement soft enough to read as lost to her father, which is close to Radak’s own reading, so that the one difference the sentence names, whether the vow cost her life or her seclusion, was blurred (rules 5 and 6); and that first out to meet him added a word the text does not say. The second judgement took perished for the same Hebrew word, in the sentence and in the gloss, and dropped first, giving who came out to meet him. Key, ref, at, commentators, source line and evidence are otherwise unchanged.

The revisions. The critic read all thirty entries and passed twenty eight; it raised two problems, and neither touched a commentator, a reading or a piece of evidence. Day 70, on Joshua 7:19, Radak: the reading ended on Israel acknowledging the lot, but the context clause said only that Achan was singled out, so the reader met the lot with no antecedent, and the purpose clause (so that his death atone for his sin and Israel acknowledge the lot) read as a stiff, unfinished subjunctive. The reviser found that the day’s JPS text never uses the word lot (it says only taken, 7:14 to 18), so the context clause now keeps the passage’s own word, taken as thief of the devoted thing, and the lot is named in the reading, where Radak names it (שיודו בני ישראל בגורל), as the lot that took him; the purpose clause now reads so his death might atone for him and Israel acknowledge the lot as true, within Radak’s two purposes (שתהא מיתתו כפרתו בהודאת עונו and ויאמרו כי אמת הוא), and the place became 7:19 in place of Joshua 7:19. Day 71, on Joshua 10:12, Rashi: his reading turns on dom, the word behind stand thou still, as silence from song (דום מלומר שירה), which the sentence never said, so silencing the sun’s song arrived unexplained, and the appositions after it were knotted; the context clause’s later killed, as are all souls in the cities taken was awkward. The revision gives the hinge: Rashi hears stand thou still as silence, the sun singing as it moves (שבכל עת הילוכו הוא אומר שירה), hushed and so halted (כל זמן שהוא דומם עומד ואינו מהלך) while Joshua sang in its place (אמר שירה תחת השמש), and by the plain sense as waiting (ופשוטו של מקרא דום לשון המתנה); three words of the key verse are quoted and none of Rashi’s, the verb is now hears, and the place became 10:12 in place of Joshua 10:12. The context clause now reads later killed with all in the cities taken. The refuter on the revision reopened packet.js on 10:12, found every clause in Rashi’s words, and recorded that the compressed context is loose, since the five kings die at Makkedah (10:26) and not in the cities, but not false. On both days the key, ref, at, commentator, source line and evidence are unchanged, and both revised entries pass the entry check at forty five words.

The holes. None. Every day has a living primary, and every entry passes the candidate gate.

The sensitive days. Day 62 states in one plain clause that the siege laws order a resisting far city’s males killed and the land’s nations destroyed, and gives Rashi and Ramban on the call to peace. Day 69, on Joshua 6:17, is spoken before the wall falls and all in the city are put to the sword; Rashi reads the devoting as consecration because the day was the Sabbath, and Radak judges (ונראה) that God told Joshua so, while reporting the rabbis’ contrary view that Joshua decided it, which the sentence leaves aside and does not deny. Day 70, on Joshua 7:19, names the stoning and burning of Achan and all he has in one plain clause and gives Radak’s two purposes of the confession, his death as atonement and Israel’s acknowledging the lot that took him as true; Radak’s other possibility, the ark catching him, is left aside and not denied, and Rashi, who reads the verse differently (Achan defaming the lot), is not named, since Radak’s comment is the fuller and the sentence attributes only his. Day 71, on Joshua 10:12, names the rout of the five kings and their later killing with all in the cities taken plainly, and gives Rashi’s song in place of the sun, then his plain reading of dom as waiting. Day 72, on Joshua 11:18, gives Rashi’s reading that Scripture tells it to Joshua’s discredit, from the Tanchuma he names. Day 83 and day 84 are set out above. Day 90, on 1 Samuel 15:23, names the command against Amalek and Saul’s sparing of Agag plainly, and gives Radak’s own elaboration after כלומר, not the Targum he cites before it. Days 74, 81 and 82 were marked by their verifiers as law or violence and were judged under rule 6 in the same way: day 74 gives Radak’s reason for the high priest’s death as the sages’, from Rabbi Meir; day 82 notes that Samson’s prayer leads to the killing at 16:30.

Points the verifiers recorded and judged sound. Day 61: Ramban quotes Rashi and Ibn Ezra on 14:1 as separate readings, which confirms that the two differ; a loving Father compresses Ibn Ezra’s comparison without adding to it. Day 64: Ibn Ezra argues against the Laban reading of 26:5, and the JPS rendering follows the wandering sense; the sentence attributes each reading to its commentator only. Day 65: Sforno comments twice on 28:2, and the sentence stays inside both; his tying of the blessings to the First Temple is not used. Day 66: Ramban first records the view that this commandment is the whole Torah and then calls correct (והנכון) the reading that it is repentance; the sentence gives his preferred reading. Day 67 and day 82 each use Rashi’s first explanation and leave the second aside without denying it. Day 68: Radak reports the rabbis’ dispute on 1:8 and gives his own view with והנראה; only his own view is attributed to him. Day 73: the fear of being killed as Caleb’s motive is Radak’s own, where Rashi and Metzudat David give fear in general. Day 76: the key stops before the dash that opens the people’s reply and ends at a full stop. Day 78: Rashi names Rabbi Tanchuma as his source, and the sentence names him only as Rashi’s stated source. Day 79: Radak and Metzudat David on 7:7 give the same idolatry reason, which confirms it is Rashi’s and not the translator’s. Day 80: Radak also reports the second reading from Maimonides before his own; the sentence does not touch it. Day 85: Radak calls far fetched the derash that Samuel feared a demon’s voice; the sentence neither reports nor resolves it. Day 87: the proposer joined two passages of Radak with an ellipsis, and the verifier replaced them with one contiguous verbatim span.

Traps that belong in traps.md. First, the context clause may state only what the day’s own passage states: a death the text reports only in the next day’s chapter is not the day’s to state, even where a commentator on the day reads it so (day 83, Judges 19:28 against 20:5). Second, a gloss that softens a hard Hebrew word can move one commentator’s reading toward the other’s and blur the very difference the two commentator form names; render the word at its full weight (day 81, אבדה as perished, not lost). Third, Radak opening with אמרו reports the sages; the sentence must say after the sages or with the sages, or it makes their saying his (days 84 and 74). Fourth, only is a claim about everything a commentator says; check the rest of his comment before writing it (day 62, where Ramban names a further difference). Fifth, the Midrash Rabbah has no volume on Joshua, Judges or the books of Samuel, and Ramban, Rashbam, Sforno and Ibn Ezra do not cover them, so from Joshua on the roster is Rashi, Radak and Metzudat David alone; the gate’s warning on Rashi’s share should be read with that in mind. Sixth, a word the reading leans on (the lot) needs its antecedent; where the commentator names something the day’s translation never names (JPS says taken, never lot), keep the translation’s word in the context clause and let the reading name the commentator’s word as his (day 70). Seventh, where a commentator’s reading turns on the sense of one Hebrew word (dom as silence), the sentence must give that sense, or the image it draws arrives with no hinge (day 71).
