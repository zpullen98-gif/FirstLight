## The Bhagavad Gita, days 1 to 18 (gita-01)

Thirty six candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the eighteen days, in eighteen lanes of one day each. All eighteen primaries were judged VERIFIED at first judgement, each after the verifier ran verse.js on the key, compared the key with Telang’s translation, and reopened the commentary in the packet at the verse named. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the packet source again. One primary was refuted, day 18, on the plain sense alone; it was judged again, CORRECTED, and lived. Three critics then read the whole batch in turn. Their problems were taken through five revisions, on days 11, 12, 14 (twice) and 15; every revision was CORRECTED and then attacked again by a refuter who reopened the packet, none was refuted, and status.js now gives the revised entry for each of those four days. Status gives eighteen living primaries, and all eighteen survive and are used. The eighteen standbys were never verified, because no primary fell. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry in batch.json is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, and none held a straight quote to be made curly.

The spread: Rāmānuja (Viśiṣṭādvaita) is named on twelve days (1, 2, 3, 5 to 12 and 16), nine of them alone (1, 5 to 11 and 16); Śaṅkara (Advaita) on seven (2, 3, 4, 12, 13, 15 and 18), three of them alone (4, 13 and 15); Madhva (Dvaita) on three (14, 17 and 18), two of them alone (14 and 17). Four days name two commentators, and on each the two genuinely differ and the difference is named, not resolved: day 2, Śaṅkara taking the plural of 2.12 to mean the many bodies and not many selves, and Rāmānuja taking it as teaching that souls are many and distinct from God; day 3, Śaṅkara reading the closing “He” of 3.42 as the Self, the witness of reason, and Rāmānuja reading it as desire; day 12, Śaṅkara reading the Unmanifested of 12.5 as the Imperishable Supreme Reality, hard to reach because attachment to the body must be abandoned, and Rāmānuja reading it as the individual soul, reached only with great struggle; day 18, Madhva reading the abandoning of 18.66 as giving up the fruits of duty and any duty not done to please the Lord, and Śaṅkara reading it as renouncing all works. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own words in the translation the roster names, Śaṅkara from A. Mahadeva Sastri (1901), Rāmānuja from A. Govindacharya (1898) and Madhva from S. Subba Rau (1906), all on archive.org, and Telang (SBE 8, 1882) was used only to check each key verse against Besant.

The corrected entry at judgement. Day 18, after refutation: the substance held (the refuter reopened packet.js on 18.66 and found Madhva’s paragraph in Subba Rau saying that outright abandonment of all duties would be inconsistent with the teaching and that Krishna could not then urge Arjuna to fight, and Śaṅkara in Sastri saying that dharma here includes adharma since freedom from all action is taught), but the plain sense said Krishna “gives Arjuna a last charge”. In day.js 18.67 lays a further charge on Arjuna, never to speak the teaching to one without asceticism or devotion, so 18.66 is not his last charge, and “last” is not the passage’s own term. The second judgement changed only the plain sense, to “after promising Arjuna his supreme word, most secret of all, speaks it”, which 18.64 gives in the passage’s own terms; the readings, the key verse and the source line are unchanged, and the entry stands at fifty nine words, within the sixty allowed when two commentators are named.

The revisions. The first critic raised three problems: the spread weighted to Rāmānuja, the phrase “last question” on day 14, and a misplaced relative clause on day 15. The second critic raised the spread again, since the first revisions had not touched it and the gate still warned on it. The third critic, reading the batch after those revisions, raised one problem, on day 11 under rule 6. Five revisions followed, each CORRECTED and none refuted.

Day 11, CORRECTED at revision, under rule 6 (a hard passage, on killing). The reading had said Rāmānuja takes Time as the one who reckons when each king’s life span runs out. The critic found that, with no side named, a reader of the key (which stops at Besant’s “to slay mankind”) could take each king as every king or all mankind, while Rāmānuja on 11.32 limits the reckoning to Dhritarashtra and the other royal dignitaries of his faction, and closes on the warriors ranged in opposition to Arjuna. The reviser reopened packet.js on 11.32 and found both passages; the reading now names the one who reckons when the life span of each king ranged against Arjuna runs out, and the second passage was added to the evidence. The key was kept, not extended, because the next sentence would bring in Besant’s “thou shalt alone survive”, which Telang and all three commentators render as even without thee. To stay within forty five words, the word “shaken” was dropped from the plain sense, which now opens “Asked by Arjuna who the devouring form is”; nothing else changed. The refuter of the revision found both passages verbatim in Rāmānuja’s own comment under 11.32, not a joined segment, and judged “each king ranged against Arjuna” carried by his words, at most a slight individuation of his collective world of kings.

Day 12, CORRECTED at revision, for the spread. The entry had named Rāmānuja alone, reading the Unmanifested of 12.5 as the individual soul. The reviser reopened packet.js on 12.5 and found the schools genuinely differ on the key verse: Śaṅkara in Sastri takes those set on the Unmanifested as those who identify themselves with the Imperishable and contemplate the Supreme Reality, their trouble greater because attachment to the body must be abandoned, while Rāmānuja in Govindacharya glosses avyakta as the individual soul, attained with great struggle. The entry now names Śaṅkara first and Rāmānuja second, both schools given, the difference stated and not resolved; the plain sense, the key verse, its ref and its place are unchanged, Rāmānuja’s reading was trimmed to fit, and the source line now names both editions. The entry stands at fifty four words. The refuter of the revision found both pieces of evidence verbatim, and left two small notes that do not reach the entry: “Imperishable Supreme Reality” joins two of Śaṅkara’s nouns that he treats as one referent, and the plain sense could use Besant’s “Indestructible” (12.1, 12.3) for the translators’ “Imperishable”; the editor may not make that change, so it stays a note.

Day 14, CORRECTED at the first revision, for the phrase. The plain sense had read “Krishna, having described the one beyond the three qualities, answers Arjuna’s last question, how to cross them”. The critic found that 14.21 is one question in three parts (the marks, the conduct, and how he goes beyond the qualities) and that Arjuna asks again at 17.1 and 18.1, so “last question” would be taken as his final question in the Gita. The plain sense became “Krishna answers the last part of Arjuna’s question, how to cross the three qualities”, the clause about the one already described being dropped to keep within forty five words; the Rāmānuja reading (loving devotion as the chief means, since mere reflection on how soul differs from matter can give way to the mind’s old habits) was unchanged at that stage, and the refuter found it verbatim in Govindacharya at 14.26.

Day 14, CORRECTED at the second revision, for the spread. Keeping the revised plain sense, the reading was moved from Rāmānuja to Madhva (Dvaita): in Subba Rau the paragraph marked V. on 14.26 says that Brahman here denotes the intelligent Prakriti and that to attain Brahman is to be as beloved of the Lord as that Prakriti is. Besant’s “Eternal” stands where Madhva’s text, Sastri and Telang have Brahman, so the sentence ties the two with the verse’s own word in curly quotes. The reading keeps only what the words carry; Madhva’s alternative gloss (to enter and remain in that Prakriti until release) is left out, not contradicted. The source line now names Subba Rau (1906), and the entry stands at forty three words. The refuter of this revision reopened the Subba Rau text and found the V. paragraph as quoted, with the A. lead in to 14.26 (how does he overcome the qualities) confirming the plain sense. He noted one fault that lies in the evidence, not the entry: the reviser’s gloss credits the alternative reading to Subba Rau, but it stands inside the V. paragraph and in the translation of the verse itself, so it is Madhva’s second gloss. The operator should correct that gloss in the evidence; the entry does not depend on it.

Day 15, CORRECTED at revision, for the prose. The reading said “like the sun reflected in water, which returns to the sun once the water is removed”, where the relative clause attaches to the water, so that the sentence said the water returns to the sun; Śaṅkara in Sastri at 15.7 says it is the reflected sun that returns to the original sun. The phrase now reads “like the sun’s reflection in water”, which puts the clause on the reflection; nothing else changed, and the entry stays at forty five words.

Points the verifiers recorded and judged sound. Day 3: Rāmānuja’s reading of the pronoun as desire stands in his own commentary body, not only in Govindacharya’s footnote. Day 2: Rāmānuja’s conclusion that souls are many and distinct from God is his own, not the translator’s bracketed gloss. Day 5: the proposer’s evidence spliced two sentences out of order; the verifier replaced it with one continuous passage, and the entry was not changed. Days 8, 9, 11 and 12: the evidence keeps the OCR errors of the scanned Govindacharya as printed (on day 12 “emodted” for embodied, on day 11 “Jam” for I am), since the operator refinds the words by machine. Day 11, a passage on killing: the plain sense states plainly that the opposing warriors are doomed and that Arjuna is bidden to fight, and Besant’s “Time” matches kāla where Telang has “death”; the reading rests on Rāmānuja’s derivation from the root meaning to count, and after revision carries the limit he sets. Day 12: Rāmānuja files his comment on 12.3 to 12.5 under 12.5, and his gloss on 12.1 confirms the Unmanifested as the individual soul. Day 13: Sastri numbers the verse 13.2 as the app does. Day 15: Telang reads “An eternal portion of me it is”, so nothing rests on Besant’s “transformed” or “immortal Spirit”. Day 17: “pure” is Besant’s own word for sattva in 17.2, so the reading adds nothing beyond Madhva’s words.

The holes. There are none: all eighteen days are used.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it on the rebuilt batch, the candidate gate passes on eighteen days with no error and no warning. The warning of the first edit, that Rāmānuja held eleven of the eighteen days alone, is gone: after the revisions to days 12 and 14 he holds nine alone. No day had to be withdrawn.

For the operator. The evidence recheck on disk (recheck.json) was run before the revisions and still lists days 12 and 14 under Rāmānuja alone; it should be run again on the revised evidence for days 11, 12, 14 and 15 before anything lands, day 11 now carrying a second joined passage. The same recheck reports the day 18 evidence FOUND, so the stray angle bracket in Sastri’s Śaṅkara on 18.9, which the first edit feared would swallow the text as far as 18.66, did not in the end hide it.

Traps. Eight observations may belong in traps.md. From day 11: on a hard passage whose key verse speaks of slaying the world or mankind, a reading that says each or all must carry the limit the commentator sets (for Rāmānuja on 11.32, the kings of Dhritarashtra’s faction, the warriors ranged against Arjuna), or the reader at six in the morning takes it as every king; and a key that ends before the next sentence is safer when that sentence carries a translator’s phrase the commentators do not share (Besant’s “thou shalt alone survive” at 11.33). From days 14 and 18: a plain sense that calls anything the last (the last question, the last charge) must be checked against the rest of the work, not only the day; 18.67 carries a further charge after 18.66, and Arjuna asks again at 17.1 and 18.1 after 14.21, so give the place in the passage’s own words (18.64’s supreme word, most secret of all; the last part of the question at 14.21). From day 15: in a simile, a relative clause after “the sun reflected in water” attaches to the water; make the thing the commentator says moves the head noun. From day 3: Besant’s capital “He” at 3.42 suggests a divine subject that Rāmānuja does not read there (for him the pronoun is desire), a case of the plan’s trap that her English may carry an interpretation; the reading must follow the commentator, not the capital. From days 2 and 3: Govindacharya adds bracketed glosses and footnotes to Rāmānuja, and only the commentary body is Rāmānuja’s own. From days 1 and 12: Rāmānuja often comments on a group of verses in one segment (1.4 to 1.47 as one, 12.3 to 12.5 under 12.5), so the place named must be the verse the segment is filed under, and the refuter must confirm the grouping. From days 14, 17 and 18: in Subba Rau’s Madhva the paragraph marked V. is Madhva’s comment and the paragraph marked A. is the lead in, so evidence must be taken from the V. paragraph; and an alternative gloss inside the V. paragraph (often echoed in parentheses in Subba Rau’s rendering of the verse) is Madhva’s, not the translator’s. From days 12 and 14: Rāmānuja is the easiest of the three to quote in Govindacharya, so a plan left to itself drifts toward him; the critic should look for days where Śaṅkara or Madhva comments on the same key verse with a reading that marks his school, as both do on 12.5 and Madhva does on 14.26.
