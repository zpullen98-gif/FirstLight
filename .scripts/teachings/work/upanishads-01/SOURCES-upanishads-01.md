## The Upanishads, days 1 to 12 (upanishads-01)

Twenty four candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the twelve days, in two lanes; the Isa, the Katha and the Kena are read in Swami Paramananda’s translation, whose own notes under each verse are never a source. All twelve primaries were verified: ten as VERIFIED at first judgement and two (days 3 and 6) as CORRECTED. Each primary was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the packet source. One was refuted (day 1); it was judged a second time, CORRECTED, and lived. A critic then read the twelve sentences as prose and raised one problem, on day 1; that entry went through a revision, the revision was cleared by a second refuter, and status.js now gives the revised entry. Status gives twelve living primaries, and all twelve are used. The twelve standbys were never verified, because no primary fell. At this second edit the editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, and none held a straight quote to be made curly. The candidate gate passes with twelve days, no error and one warning, that Śaṅkara holds ten of the twelve days; no other commentator survives unused, since every standby stands unverified, so the warning asks for nothing the editor may do.

The spread. Śaṅkara (Advaita) is named on ten days: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11, alone on eight of them. Madhva (Dvaita) is named on four: alone on days 4 and 12, where, as the refuters note, his reading differs from Śaṅkara’s (on Katha 1.2.20 firm faith in Vishnu and the Lord’s superiority to the self, where Śaṅkara speaks of desire turned from outer objects; on Kena 4.8 truth as the Mīmāṃsā, where Śaṅkara takes it as freedom from deceit), and beside Śaṅkara on days 2 and 6, where the two genuinely differ and each is named with his school, the difference named and not resolved. Rāmānuja is named on no day, since he wrote no commentary on these three Upanishads and no day called for the Śrī Bhāṣya. No entry is hedged, and Müller’s notes were not used. Śaṅkara is read in S. Sitarama Sastri’s translation, volume 1 (the 1905 printing on archive.org) for the Isa and the Kena and The Katha and Prasna Upanishads (second edition, 1928) for the Katha; Madhva in S. C. Vasu’s Sacred Books of the Hindus, volume 1 (1909 to 1911). Every reading was taken from the packet.

The CORRECTED entries at first judgement. Day 3: the proposal had Śaṅkara read the good and the pleasant as coming “mingled”, which drops his qualifier, since for him the two are opposed and only seem mixed to those of poor understanding; the verifier changed it to “arriving as if mingled”, after his own words on 1.2.2, “as it were mingled”, and nothing else. Day 6: the proposal had Madhva read the knower’s self as becoming like the Lord “but never the same”; his words on 2.1.15 deny identity while affirming likeness (“not identically the same”), and “never the same” overstated that, so it became “but not identical with Him”. The plain sense, the sources and the schools were unchanged.

The refutation and the second judgement. Day 1 was refuted for drift. The sentence had Śaṅkara read the covering as seeing “all that moves” as the Self, but his gloss on Isa 1 widens the verse’s own word to “All this universe, movable and immovable”, so the paraphrase narrowed his reading to the surface of the verse. At the second judgement the reading became the universe, moving and unmoving, seen as the Self, with renunciation following from it and “enjoy” glossed as protect, each carried by his words in the packet. The verifier also replaced the plain sense’s “a prayer at the hour of death”, an inference that echoes Paramananda’s note on verse 17, with the day’s own terms, a closing prayer as the body goes to ashes. The key, the source line and the evidence were unchanged.

The revision. One, on day 1, after the critic. The sentence that came out of the second judgement read “The Isa opens eighteen verses that run”, which makes the text perform an opening it does not perform, and ended its reading in the clipped fragment “as the Self, whence renunciation, and ‘enjoy’ as protect”, which left the reader to supply the step Śaṅkara states, that one who so contemplates the Self “is bound to renounce”. The revision adopted the critic’s recast: the plain sense now says the Isa’s eighteen verses run from renunciation and work to a closing prayer as the body goes to ashes, and the reading says renunciation follows from seeing the universe, moving and unmoving, as the Self, with “enjoy” as protect. It is forty five words, at the cap. The commentator, the reading, the key, the ref, the place, the source line and the evidence were unchanged; only the prose was made clear. The second refuter reopened the packet at Isa 1, found all three passages of evidence verbatim, and did not refute. He offered one optional tightening, that the plain sense give verse 17’s wish in its own mood (“a closing prayer that the body burn to ashes”) rather than as an event; it was not taken into the entry, since only a revision may change a survivor, and it is recorded here for the operator.

The holes. There are none: all twelve days are used.

Two things for the operator. The source lines do not share one form: days 2 and 6, which name two commentators, give the edition in brief (“Kaṭha Bhāṣya 2.1.15, trans. S. Sitarama Sastri (2nd ed. 1928)”); days 7 and 8 carry the archive.org identifier; days 9 to 11 read “(pada)” and put the volume before archive.org; days 1 and 3 to 6 give the full title. None is wrong and no gate raised it, but a revision could bring them to one form. And the refuter on day 4 noted that the reading’s “free from desire” uses three of the key verse’s words without curly quotes, referentially; the gate does not flag it, and it is left as the survivor stands.

Traps. Six belong in traps.md. From days 2 and 12: Vasu prints his own Note paragraphs interleaved with Madhva’s bhāṣya; attribute to Madhva only what stands under the heading MADHVA’S COMMENTARY, never what stands in Vasu’s notes or his brackets. From day 9: Sitarama Sastri prints Kena 1.3 as one verse, where some editions divide it as 1.3 and 1.4; give the place by the edition cited, and check it against the app. From day 11: Śaṅkara comments on Kena 3.11 and 3.12 together in one segment; place the reading on the verse whose matter it treats. From day 1: Śaṅkara’s gloss may widen a word of the verse (all that moves becomes the universe, movable and immovable); paraphrase his gloss, not the verse’s surface, and keep the plain sense clear of Paramananda’s notes, which colour inferences such as the hour of death. From days 3 and 6: a commentator’s qualifier is part of his reading; keep “as it were”, and keep likeness distinct from identity, since Madhva denies identity without denying all sameness. From the day 1 revision: a sentence cut down to reach the word cap can drop the step that carries the reading (“whence renunciation”); state the step the commentator states, in a whole clause, and cut elsewhere.
