## The Tanakh, days 91 to 120 (tanakh-04)

Sixty candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the thirty days (1 Samuel 17 to 2 Kings 9), in five lanes (days 91 to 95; 96, 97, 98, 101 and 103; 105 to 109; 110, 111, 112, 113 and 115; 116 and 118) and four lanes for the sensitive days (99 and 100; 102 and 104; 114 and 117; 119 and 120). All thirty primaries were judged at first judgement: twenty seven VERIFIED and three CORRECTED, days 104, 115 and 117. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the commentator’s Hebrew in Sefaria’s export at commit 3f10136 through packet.js on the key verse, found the evidence words verbatim, checked the context clause against day.js and ran verse.js and the entry check again. Three primaries were refuted, days 96, 104 and 111; each was judged again with the refutation in hand, each second judgement was CORRECTED, and all three lived. A critic then read the thirty living entries (critic.json, first round) and raised three problems, on days 96, 107 and 109; each went to a reviser, and each revision was attacked by a second refuter. The revisions of days 96 and 107 were cleared; the revision of day 109 was refuted. At the first edit the critic read the batch again (second round) and raised one problem, day 109, which still carried its unrevised wording; a second revision of day 109 went to a second refuter and was cleared. The critic then read the edited batch a third time (third round) and raised two problems of wording, on days 98 and 117; each went to a reviser and each revision was attacked by a second refuter and cleared. Status now gives thirty living primaries, and all thirty are used; there is no hole. The thirty standbys were never verified, because no primary died. In all, sixty candidates were proposed, thirty verified (twenty seven VERIFIED and three CORRECTED at first judgement), three refuted and three CORRECTED again, six revisions made, of which five were cleared and one was refuted, and thirty survived and were used. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, the corrected wording on days 96, 104, 111, 115 and 117 and the revised wording on days 96, 98, 107, 109 and 117 included, and no key, sentence or source line held a straight quote to be made curly. The candidate gate passes with no error.

The spread. Rashi is named on fourteen of the thirty days, alone on eleven (91, 93, 96, 103, 105, 108, 112, 113, 116, 118 and 120) and first beside Metzudat David on three (92, 99 and 114). Radak is named on eleven days, alone on nine (95, 98, 101, 102, 106, 107, 109, 111 and 117) and first beside Metzudat David on two (94 and 100). Metzudat David is named on ten days, alone on five (97, 104, 110, 115 and 119) and as the second voice on five (92, 94, 99, 100 and 114). Five days name two commentators, and on each the difference is named and not resolved. Ibn Ezra, Ramban, Rashbam and Sforno are not named, because none of them covers Samuel or Kings, and the Midrash is not named, because the Midrash Rabbah has no volume on these books. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own Hebrew in the packet, and the plan has no conduits. The candidate gate warns that Rashi holds 73 of the 120 days of the plan so far; the warning is not an error, and in the Former Prophets the roster is Rashi, Radak and Metzudat David alone, so this batch gives Radak and Metzudat David sixteen of its thirty days as the lead voice.

The corrected entries at first judgement. Day 104, on 2 Samuel 21:1, a sensitive day, Radak: the verifier changed only “with the sages” to “citing the sages”, since Radak sets out Saadia Gaon’s answers first and then gives the sages’ year by year search without choosing, and “with” made him side with the sages. This entry was then refuted and changed again (below). Day 115, on 1 Kings 19:13, Metzudat David: the context clause had said the LORD passes in a still small voice, but 19:11 to 12 say only that He was not in wind, earthquake or fire and that a still small voice came after; that the Glory was in the voice is the commentators’ inference, so the clause now keeps to the text. The reading had taken the quiet voice as God; Metzudat David says the Glory passed in the still small voice because God delights in kindness and does not rouse His wrath, so the reading now attaches kindness over wrath to the Glory’s coming in that voice, with the repeated question as whether Elijah still seeks vengeance (שאל לו שוב, העודך באת הנה לבקש נקמה). Day 117, on 2 Kings 2:24, a sensitive day, Radak: the proposal gave Radak “no good in them”, which is the wording of Rashi and Metzudat David (לחלוחית טובה); Radak’s own is לחלוחית מצוה, so the sentence now says no trace of a commandment in them or their seed, and the context clause now says children, as the JPS passage does at 2:23. That context clause was itself replaced in the third round (below).

The corrected entries after refutation. On attribution, Radak’s comment relays Saadia Gaon’s answers (closing כך סידרם רבינו סעדיה גאון) and then what the sages explained, endorsing neither, so the three year search is not his own reading; Radak was dropped. Day 107, on 1 Kings 5:17, Radak: ‘which would not honour his father’ attached in the sentence to God’s withholding the task from David, whereas Radak (לא רצה לגלות לו הטעם כי האל מנעו כי לא היה כבוד אביו) puts the dishonour in revealing that reason. Traps that belong in traps.md. First, where a commentator relays two others’ answers in turn (Radak on 2 Samuel 21:1 gives Saadia Gaon’s and then the sages’) and chooses neither, neither is his reading, and “citing the sages” does not cure a verb that makes it his; choose a commentator who speaks in his own voice (day 104). Second, where a commentator reports a character’s speech (Radak giving Jeroboam’s pitch at 1 Kings 12:28), every claim in it must stay inside that speech in the sentence (“he tells them”), or a doctrinal claim reads as the commentator’s own; and an exculpation must carry the limit he sets a verse or two on (12:30) or be left out (day 111). Third, the context clause must not take in a commentator’s inference or reason as though it were the text: guarding the baggage (day 96), the LORD in the still small voice (day 115), and seven sons where the text has sons and grandsons (day 104). Fourth, where Rashi, Radak and Metzudat David give one derash in nearly the same words, the sentence must carry the wording of the commentator it names, since the difference can be the point (לחלוחית טובה against לחלוחית מצוה, day 117). Fifth, from Samuel on the Midrash Rabbah has no volume and only Rashi, Radak and Metzudat David cover the text, so the gate’s warning on Rashi’s share should be read with that in mind. Sixth, a paraphrase of a commentator must keep his object: ‘building the house for You is by way of figure’ is not ‘the house is a figure’, and a request he gives in a character’s mouth (בקש שלמה) is not a purpose he states in his own voice (day 109). Seventh, a revision that mends only grammar can carry an older drift forward untouched: the first revision of day 109 mended the dangling participle and kept both drifting clauses, so a reviser asked to fix wording should still reread the commentator before rewriting. Eighth, on a hard passage the context clause is the easiest place to break rule 2, because the event itself seems the natural context; when the key verse already states the hard event, the clause should give what leads to it in the passage (day 117, where it had retold the curse and the bears), and rule 6 is still met because the key verse states it plainly above the sentence.
