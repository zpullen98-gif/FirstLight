## The Qur’an, days 1 to 33 (quran-01)

Sixty six candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the thirty three days, in five lanes (days 1, 2 and 5 to 8; 11 and 13 to 17; 18, 19 and 23 to 26; 27 to 32; and 33) and four lanes for the sensitive days (3 and 4; 9 and 10; 12 and 20; 21 and 22). All thirty three primaries were judged at first judgement: thirty one VERIFIED and two CORRECTED, days 11 and 29. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the commentator’s Arabic through packet.js on the key ayah, found the evidence words verbatim, checked the context clause against day.js and ran verse.js and the entry check again. Two primaries were refuted, days 10 and 23, each on a single word; each was judged again with the refutation in hand, CORRECTED, and lived. A critic then read the whole batch six times, and before the owner’s checkpoint the eight hard days were read again by two reviewers each, one for fidelity to the mufassir and one for plain sense and tone. Twenty six revisions followed in six rounds: two on the first critic reading, sixteen on the second, three on the third, one on the fourth, three on the hard day review and one on the sixth critic reading. Twenty three were kept and three were refuted: on days 7 and 32 in the second round, both revised again in the third and kept, and on day 3 in the hard day round, revised again in the sixth and kept. Six days were revised more than once (3, 7, 16, 20, 22 and 32). Status now gives thirty three living primaries, all thirty three are used, and there are no holes. The thirty three standbys were never verified, because no primary died. This edit is made after the sixth round; the only entry that differs from the previous edit, made after the hard day review, is day 3, which now lands as revised. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry used is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, and no key, sentence or source line held a straight quote to be made curly (the straight apostrophes in the refs of days 6 to 8, 12 to 19, 27 and 28 are left as verse.js prints them, since the ref is copied unchanged).

The spread: al-Tabari is named on twenty seven of the thirty three days, twenty six of them alone (1 to 5, 7 to 13, 16 to 18, 20 to 25, 27 and 30 to 33) and on day 26 beside Ibn Kathir. Ibn Kathir is named on seven days (6, 14, 15, 19, 26, 28 and 29), six of them alone. The Jalalayn, al-Qurtubi, al-Wahidi and al-Tabarsi are not named: the brief makes al-Tabari the default, and on every key ayah but these seven his comment was substantial and carried a stated preference; al-Wahidi gives no occasion that a sentence rests on, and al-Tabarsi has no source in hand. One day names two commentators, and there the two genuinely differ and the difference is named, not resolved: day 26, Ibn Kathir on 12:50 to 12:53 taking 12:53 as the words of the ruler’s wife, confessing the desire behind her advances, since Joseph was not yet present, and al-Tabari on 12:53 taking it as Joseph’s own words. The context clause does not decide who speaks, although Pickthall’s parenthesis in 12:52 gives the speech to Joseph. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own Arabic in the Tarteel QUL text via spa5k/tafsir_api at commit 9ae590a, reached through packet.js. The candidate gate warns that al-Tabari holds fifty of the sixty six days it counts, this batch with those already landed; the warning is the brief’s default order at work and not an error, and no critic reading or hard day reviewer asked to move a day to another commentator.

The corrected entries at first judgement. Day 11, on 4:135: the proposal opened with a rebuke to those who pleaded for wrongdoers, but that rebuke is 4:109, twenty six ayahs back, with the rulings on women, orphans and wives of 4:127 to 4:134 between; tying 4:135 to the pleaders was al-Tabari’s reading (the false vouching for the Banu Ubayriq) carried into the plain sense. The context clause now opens after rulings on women and orphans; the reading half and the place are unchanged. Day 29, on 15:9: Ibn Kathir, in his one comment on 15:6 to 15:9, takes the Reminder as the Qur’an guarded from alteration and holds that more fitting than referring the pronoun to the Prophet. The proposal said he preferred this to referring the guarding to the Prophet, which could be read as the Prophet doing the guarding; the last clause now says the view he sets aside takes the Prophet as the one guarded.

The corrected entries after refutation. Day 10, on 4:58: the refuter found al-Tabari’s stated preference verbatim (the ayah is addressed to those in charge of the Muslims’ affairs, as 4:59 charges the flock with obedience), and his allowance that it may have come down on Uthman b. Talha and cover every trustee is not contradicted. What broke was rule 2: the context clause put the act of 4:51 in the past (those who favoured idolaters) where day.js prints it in the present. The second judgement changed favoured to favour and nothing else. Day 23, on 10:58: al-Tabari’s own gloss makes the bounty Islam and the mercy the Qur’an, and says that the two, jointly, are better than this world’s wealth and treasure, one predicate on a joined subject. The sentence said each worth more, which distributes a comparison his words make together; the second judgement changed each to together and nothing else, and recopied the evidence character for character from the packet, since the earlier copy held a shadda and a fatha in the other order.

The revisions from the first critic reading. Day 20, on 8:61, a hard ayah: the critic found that the reading ended on the two concerning different peoples, an image without its point, and so dropped the limit al-Tabari sets himself, which rule 6 asks the sentence to carry. The reviser reopened the packet on 8:61: after the reports of Qatada, Ikrima, al-Hasan and Ibn Zayd that the verse is abrogated, al-Tabari answers in his own voice that the order to slay in Bara’a does not negate it, because this verse was meant for a people of Scripture with whom God allowed peace on the taking of tribute, while the order to slay meant only the Arab idol worshippers, from whom tribute may not be accepted, each verse standing in force in its own case. The reading then took the verse as unrepealed, as concerning a people of Scripture allowed peace for tribute, whereas At Tawbah’s order to slay means Arab idol worshippers; at that round the Banu Qurayza, whom al-Tabari names as the occasion, were left unnamed. The second refuter found every phrase in his words and did not refute; he noted that Ibn Kathir, on 8:61 to 8:63, disputes the Qurayza occasion because the setting is Badr, which does not touch a sentence that names al-Tabari alone. The hard day review revised this entry again (below). Day 23, on 10:58: the critic found two subjects (an exhortation and a balm for the breasts) taking the singular is announced; the reviser changed is to are and nothing else, and the second refuter, reopening the packet, found that al-Tabari later calls this the interpretation he gave, so the reading is his and not only a report, and that he records the reverse view (the bounty the Qur’an, the mercy Islam) without deciding, while the sentence claims no preference.

The revisions from the second critic reading. The critic carried forward two batch wide problems of the first reading. The first is the divine name outside quotation: days 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 12 wrote Allah in their own prose, while the rest wrote God, and rule 7 asks for God outside quotation. The revisions on days 1 (the praise of God), 2 (God’s sanctuaries), 5 (God never overtaxing), 6 (God’s love) and 12 (waging war on God, a killing God forbids) changed that word and nothing else, word counts unchanged; each reviser reopened the packet on the key ayah and found the evidence verbatim, and the second refuter on each found the reading in the commentator’s own voice and did not refute. The second is the form of the source line: days 27, 30, 31 and 32 opened with Al-Tabari where the roster’s display name is al-Tabari, and days 11 and 13 to 17 named the edition as the Tarteel QUL text without the word Arabic. The revisions on days 11, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 17 now name the edition as the Tarteel QUL Arabic text, and those on days 27, 30 and 31 open with al-Tabari; each changed the source line only, and each was kept. On days 13 and 14 the evidence was recopied from the packet’s own bytes, since the earlier copies held a shadda and a vowel sign in the other order. Two revisions were refuted. On day 7, on 3:92, the reviser changed Allah’s kindness to God’s kindness; the second refuter found the reading sound in al-Tabari’s words (al birr as God’s kindness, His favour in bringing the believers into His Garden, reached by giving of the wealth one loves), but found that the sentence gave no antecedent for them in bringing them into His Garden except those dying in disbelief in the context clause, so that it could be read as al-Tabari bringing unbelievers into the Garden, the reverse of 3:91 and of his gloss, which addresses the believers. On day 32, on 17:78, the reviser changed Al-Tabari to al-Tabari; the second refuter found the reading holds (he prefers the leaning past the zenith, the noon prayer, and the prayer at nightfall as the sunset prayer alone), but that the place failed: the digitisation labels the segment 17:77 to 17:78, yet inside it al-Tabari has his own heading for 17:78, and his comment on 17:77 says nothing of prayer. Both faults were in the entries before revision as well, so at the first edit day 7 fell back to an entry the candidate gate rejects (Allah outside quotation) and was left a hole, and day 32 was used with its old place and its capital.

The revisions from the third critic reading. The critic raised three problems, and each went through revision and was kept. Day 7, on 3:92: the reviser reopened the packet, found that Abu Jaʿfar twice names the addressees as the believers, glosses al birr as God’s favour in bringing them into His Garden and paraphrases the ayah as giving in alms of what they love and wish to keep, of their choicest wealth; the reading now says God’s kindness in bringing the believers into His Garden, reached by giving from the wealth they most wish to keep, forty four words, and the evidence joins the two verbatim pieces with an ellipsis so that the second half of the reading is carried too. The refuter found both pieces under the recheck and did not refute, so day 7 is no longer a hole. Day 32, on 17:78: the place is now on 17:78 in both the sentence and the source line, the source line opens al-Tabari, and a middle piece on the sun passing the zenith was added to the evidence so that past midday is carried by his own words; the refuter found that the sentence claims less than he says (he also puts the afternoon prayer in the span from the leaning to nightfall) but nothing more, and did not refute. Day 22, on 9:122, a hard ayah: the same locator fault as day 32, since the segment is labelled 9:121 to 9:122 but al-Tabari gives 9:121 (spending and crossing valleys) and 9:122 separate headings, and the reading sits under 9:122; the place is now on 9:122 in both the sentence and the source line, the evidence unchanged, and the refuter confirmed his stated preference (the marching party gains the understanding, the view he gives as al-Hasan al-Basri’s, the reading that the stayers learn rejected as impossible) and did not refute.

The revision from the fourth critic reading. The critic, reading the batch after the first edit, raised one problem, on day 16, on 6:152. The context clause said that dealings in trade follow the orphan’s wealth, which a reader at six in the morning can take as trade pursuing the orphan’s wealth, a misstatement of the passage; what is meant is the order of the sacred duties the Prophet is told to recite in 6:151 to 6:152, where the clause on full measure and weight comes after the clause on the orphan’s property. The reviser checked that order against day.js and rewrote the context clause only: among the sacred duties the Prophet is told to recite, the rule of fair trade follows the one on the orphan’s wealth. The key, the commentator, the reading (al-Tabari on ‘beyond its scope’ as asking no payer for more than the due, and no payee to settle for less), the place and the source line are unchanged, and the entry is forty five words. The reviser reopened packet.js on 6:152 and found the evidence as one contiguous span in al-Tabari’s own voice, with no competing reported views; the refuter reopened it again, found the paraphrase carries no more than his words, noted that al-Tabari himself ties the measure clause back to the list of 6:151, and did not refute. The revision was kept. A fifth critic reading, after it, found no problem in the batch.

The hard day review. Before the owner’s checkpoint the eight sensitive days of this batch (3, 4, 9, 10, 12, 20, 21 and 22) were each read by two independent reviewers, one reopening the packet for fidelity to the mufassir and one reading plain sense and tone; days 46 and 56 of later batches were read in the same pass and are not part of this section. Days 4, 9, 10, 12 and 21 passed both lenses. Three days drew findings, all marked should, and each went through revision. Day 20, on 8:61: the tone reviewer found the word allowed dangling (the verse allowing, or the people allowed) and found that the sentence left out the occasion al-Tabari himself names, the Banu Qurayza, who were Jews, a people of Scripture, backed by Mujahid’s report, so that a reader could take the ruling as a general statement about every people of Scripture where al-Tabari ties this verse to one tribe. The reviser reopened packet.js on 8:61 and rewrote the reading: al-Tabari takes it as unrepealed, concerning Qurayza, people of Scripture who may have peace for tribute, while At Tawbah’s order to slay means Arab idol worshippers; in the context clause only an offer of truce became offers of truce, to stay within forty five words. This reverses the choice of the first round to leave Qurayza unnamed: the refuter attacked the entry on rule 4, since surah 8 names neither Qurayza nor a people of Scripture, and held that the plan’s voice and rule 6 require a hard ayah to carry the mufassir’s own occasion, attributed to him, that the day’s text has no name of its own for this group, and that the entry avoids the watch term Jews; he found all three evidence runs verbatim and did not refute. The revision was kept, and day 20 is used as revised. Day 22, on 9:122: the fidelity reviewer found that the reading gave al-Tabari’s second preference (the marching party gains the understanding) but dropped the condition he sets as his first: the ayah forbids everyone going out and leaving the Prophet alone, and concerns detachments sent while the Prophet himself stays in his city, which is how he squares it with 9:120, binding all to go when the Prophet marches in person. The reviser reopened the packet and added that condition to the reading (al-Tabari takes it of detachments sent while the Prophet stays, and the understanding as gained by the marching party, not those who stay, from seeing God’s victory), dropping for the believers after victory to fit forty five words; the evidence now joins four verbatim runs, chosen to avoid the honorific sign. The refuter found all four runs verbatim and both preferences in his own voice, and did not refute. The revision was kept, and day 22 is used as revised. Day 3, on 2:190: the tone reviewer found the sentence softened by what it leaves out. Beside a context clause saying the command is to fight those who fight them, a reading that al-Tabari takes the command as unrepealed leaves him holding a standing command against attackers only; but for him those who fight are the fighting men of the unbelievers as against their women and children, and his paraphrase is to fight and call to God’s religion until they return to obedience or give the jizya, so the line he draws is between combatant and non combatant. The reviser rewrote the reading to take the verse as unrepealed, binding against the unbelievers’ fighting men. The refuter found the substance sound and all three evidence runs verbatim, but refuted on form: unbelievers is on this plan’s watch list, rule 4 names other groups only as the day’s text names them, and Pickthall calls this group disbelievers in 2:191 and never writes unbelievers anywhere in day 3; the reviser had not answered the entry check’s warning. He proposed the same sentence with the disbelievers’ fighting men, which passes the entry check with no warning. Because that revision was refuted, the previous edit used day 3 as it stood before the hard day review, a VERIFIED entry that passed its refuter and the first five critic readings, with the hard day reviewer’s finding on its scope unresolved.

The revision from the sixth critic reading. The critic, reading the batch after the previous edit, raised one problem, on day 3, on 2:190: the tone reviewer’s finding still stood, since the refuter’s repair had been proposed but never landed, and the sentence as it stood left al-Tabari holding a standing command against attackers only, gentler than his own line between combatant and non combatant. The reviser reopened packet.js on 2:190 and wrote the sentence the refuter had proposed: the verse opens the ayahs commanding the believers to fight, slay and drive out those who fight them; al-Tabari takes it as unrepealed, binding against the disbelievers’ fighting men, and reads the forbidden aggression as killing women, children or those who pay tribute. Only the sentence changed (commanding for that command, it for the command, and the clause on the fighting men added), forty five words; the key, ref, place and source line are unchanged. Disbelievers is Pickthall’s word in 2:191 on the same day, and the entry check prints no warning. The evidence joins three verbatim runs from al-Tabari’s own heading for 2:190, sliced from the packet so that the order of the diacritics is kept: his tarjih for the view of ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAziz that the ayah is not repealed, since a claim of repeal without proof is arbitrary; his paraphrase that God commanded fighting against the fighting men of the people of disbelief, not their women and children; and his gloss on do not transgress as not killing a child, a woman or any who have given the jizya of the two Books and the Magians. The refuter reopened the packet, found all three runs exact, found the preference and the paraphrase in his own voice and not Pickthall’s begin not hostilities, and noted that those who pay tribute leaves out his narrowing to the two Books and the Magians but adds nothing, and that leaving out his call them until they return or pay the jizya is a selection and not a softening, since the limit he sets is given. He did not refute. The revision was kept, and day 3 is used as revised, which closes the hard day reviewer’s finding.

The holes. There are none. The one hole of the edit made after the second round, day 7, was filled by its kept revision in that round, and neither the later critic readings nor the hard day review took any day out.

Points the verifiers recorded and judged sound. The hard ayahs (days 3, 4, 12, 20, 21 and 22) state the matter in one plain clause and carry the limit al-Tabari sets: on 2:190, as revised twice, the command is unrepealed by his stated preference and binds against the disbelievers’ fighting men, and the forbidden aggression is killing women, children or those who pay tribute; on 2:216 the duty falls from the rest once enough take it up; on 5:32 saving a life is refraining from a killing God forbids; on 8:61, as revised twice, the peace verse is unrepealed, concerning Qurayza, people of Scripture who may have peace for tribute, while the slaying verse of At Tawbah means the Arab idol worshippers; on 9:6 the one who seeks protection hears the Qur’an and, refusing Islam, is given safe passage home; on 9:122, as revised twice, al-Tabari takes the ayah of detachments sent while the Prophet stays and prefers that the marching party gains the understanding, which departs from Pickthall’s parenthesis (who are left behind), and the reading is his, not the translator’s. Day 26 is marked sexual only because the reading names the advances of the ruler’s wife, plainly and without detail. Day 19 was judged at medium confidence: Ibn Kathir, on 7:205 to 7:206, says this was before the five prayers were imposed beside saying the ayah is Meccan, and his this could be taken as the worship at two times of 50:39 rather than the ayah; the natural reading supports the sentence. Where a commentator treats several ayahs in one comment (Ibn Kathir on 3:31 to 3:32, 7:205 to 7:206, 12:50 to 12:53, 13:27 to 13:29 and 15:6 to 15:9) the place is cited as that passage; where a segment labelled with a range holds al-Tabari’s own heading for the key ayah (2:190 on day 3, 5:87 on day 13, 9:122 on day 22, 17:78 on day 32) the place is that ayah. On days 7, 9, 15 and 20 the proposer’s evidence spliced passages with an ellipsis, and on day 15 began at an honorific sign; the verifier replaced each with one contiguous span from the packet. On day 6 the evidence was recopied from the packet characters because the proposer’s copy differed in the order of a shadda and a fatha, and on day 21 it was likewise recopied character for character. The evidence on days 3, 7, 20, 22 and 32 as revised joins verbatim pieces with an ellipsis, which the recheck splits on. The source lines still name the one edition in four wordings (the Arabic text of the Tarteel QUL library; the Tarteel QUL Arabic text; Tarteel QUL Arabic text without the article; Arabic text of the Tarteel QUL library without it), all under the character limit and all accepted by the gate; the editor copies them as they stand, and a later pass may bring them to the roster’s one form.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it on the full batch of thirty three, with days 20 and 22 as revised on the hard day review and day 3 as revised on the sixth critic reading, the candidate gate passes: sixty six days with those already landed, no error and one warning, that al-Tabari holds fifty of those sixty six days.

Traps. These observations may belong in traps.md. From days 6, 13, 14 and 23: the Arabic in a copy of the evidence often differs from the packet only in the order of a shadda and a vowel sign, which a machine recheck will not find; copy evidence from the packet output character for character, never retype or normalise it. From day 11: a context clause that reaches back many ayahs to the setting the commentator gives (4:135 tied to the pleaders of 4:109) carries his reading into the plain sense; give the ayahs just before the key. From day 23: al-Tabari often makes one joint comparison on a joined subject (Islam and the Qur’an together better than this world); each or both distributes a claim he does not make. From day 10: the context clause must keep the tense in which Pickthall prints the neighbouring ayah. From days 22 and 26: Pickthall’s parentheses sometimes settle a question the mufassirun dispute ((who are left behind) at 9:122, the speaker of 12:52 to 12:53); the reading must follow the commentator, and the context clause must not adopt the parenthesis. From day 26: Ibn Kathir’s Ibn Jarir is al-Tabari, so a report of what Ibn Jarir holds in Ibn Kathir is second hand evidence for al-Tabari and must be checked in al-Tabari’s own comment. From day 20: on an ayah the mufassirun call abrogated or not, a reading that says only that two verses concern different peoples leaves out the limit rule 6 wants; name the peoples as the commentator names them, attributed to him, including the occasion he sets (Qurayza), since leaving it out turns his ruling on one tribe into a general statement. From days 3 and 22: on a hard ayah a reading can be true in every word and still soften the mufassir by dropping the scope he sets (al-Tabari’s combatant line at 2:190, his condition of detachments sent while the Prophet stays at 9:122); read his first tarjih on the whole ayah as well as his gloss on the phrase in hand. From day 3: a revision that brings in a watch list word (unbelievers) is refuted even when its substance holds; when a group must be named, use the translation’s own word for it in the day’s text (Pickthall’s disbelievers), and answer every warning the entry check prints. From day 7: a context clause that names only the people of the ayah before (those dying in disbelief) and never says to whom the key ayah speaks leaves every they and them in the reading pointing at the wrong people; say to whom the ayah speaks, as the brief’s voice asks. From days 3, 22 and 32: the digitisation’s segment labels are not al-Tabari’s own divisions; where a segment labelled with a range holds his own heading for a single ayah, cite that ayah, not the label, and check every ranged place in a batch for this at once rather than one day at a time. From day 16: a context clause that gives the order of rulings in a list by setting a ruling after a bare noun (dealings in trade follow the orphan’s wealth) can be read as one thing pursuing another; name both as rulings (the rule of fair trade follows the one on the orphan’s wealth) so that follows can only mean comes after. From the critic’s batch wide findings and the gate: lanes drift apart on the divine name in prose and on the wording of the source line, and the candidate gate now rejects Allah outside quotation as an error; tell proposers before the lanes run to write God outside quotation and to copy the roster’s source form exactly. From days 3, 7 and 32: a revision that fixes one fault can be refuted over another fault, its own or one the original shares, which leaves the day as it was until a later revision carries every repair; a reviser should read the earlier critic, reviewer and refuter findings on the day and the entry check’s warnings, not only the problem in hand. From day 3: a refuter’s proposed repair does not land by itself, and a refuted revision on a hard day leaves the finding open until a further round carries the repair; after each edit, check that every hard day finding marked should has landed in the batch. From this edit: one run of status.js returned, for days 1, 9 and 14, survivors belonging to another plan’s dry run (Tanakh verses and Jewish commentators under other ids), while three runs before and after it agreed and gave this batch’s own entries; build a batch only from a status run whose survivor ids all carry the batch’s own prefix, and run it twice to see that it is stable.
