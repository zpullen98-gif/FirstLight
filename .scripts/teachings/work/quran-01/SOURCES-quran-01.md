## The Qur’an, days 1 to 33 (quran-01)

Sixty six candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the thirty three days, in five lanes (days 1, 2 and 5 to 8; 11 and 13 to 17; 18, 19 and 23 to 26; 27 to 32; and 33) and four lanes for the sensitive days (3 and 4; 9 and 10; 12 and 20; 21 and 22). All thirty three primaries were judged at first judgement: thirty one VERIFIED and two CORRECTED, days 11 and 29. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the commentator’s Arabic through packet.js on the key ayah, found the evidence words verbatim, checked the context clause against day.js and ran verse.js and the entry check again. Two primaries were refuted, days 10 and 23, each on a single word; each was judged again with the refutation in hand, CORRECTED, and lived. A critic then read the whole batch four times, and twenty two revisions followed in four rounds: two on the first reading, sixteen on the second, three on the third and one on the fourth. Twenty were kept at once; two, on days 7 and 32, were refuted in the second round and revised again in the third, and both of those second revisions were kept. Day 16 was revised twice, in the second round (its source line) and in the fourth (its context clause), and both revisions were kept. Status now gives thirty three living primaries, all thirty three are used, and there are no holes. The thirty three standbys were never verified, because no primary died. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry used is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, and no key, sentence or source line held a straight quote to be made curly (the straight apostrophes in the refs of days 6 to 8, 12 to 19, 27 and 28 are left as verse.js prints them, since the ref is copied unchanged).

The spread: al-Tabari is named on twenty seven of the thirty three days, twenty six of them alone (1 to 5, 7 to 13, 16 to 18, 20 to 25, 27 and 30 to 33) and on day 26 beside Ibn Kathir. Ibn Kathir is named on seven days (6, 14, 15, 19, 26, 28 and 29), six of them alone. The Jalalayn, al-Qurtubi, al-Wahidi and al-Tabarsi are not named: the brief makes al-Tabari the default, and on every key ayah but these seven his comment was substantial and carried a stated preference; al-Wahidi gives no occasion that a sentence rests on, and al-Tabarsi has no source in hand. One day names two commentators, and there the two genuinely differ and the difference is named, not resolved: day 26, Ibn Kathir on 12:50 to 12:53 taking 12:53 as the words of the ruler’s wife, confessing the desire behind her advances, since Joseph was not yet present, and al-Tabari on 12:53 taking it as Joseph’s own words. The context clause does not decide who speaks, although Pickthall’s parenthesis in 12:52 gives the speech to Joseph. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own Arabic in the Tarteel QUL text via spa5k/tafsir_api at commit 9ae590a, reached through packet.js. The candidate gate warns that al-Tabari holds fifty of the sixty six days it counts, this batch with those already landed; the warning is the brief’s default order at work and not an error, and no critic reading asked to move a day to another commentator.

The corrected entries at first judgement. Day 11, on 4:135: the proposal opened with a rebuke to those who pleaded for wrongdoers, but that rebuke is 4:109, twenty six ayahs back, with the rulings on women, orphans and wives of 4:127 to 4:134 between; tying 4:135 to the pleaders was al-Tabari’s reading (the false vouching for the Banu Ubayriq) carried into the plain sense. The context clause now opens after rulings on women and orphans; the reading half and the place are unchanged. Day 29, on 15:9: Ibn Kathir, in his one comment on 15:6 to 15:9, takes the Reminder as the Qur’an guarded from alteration and holds that more fitting than referring the pronoun to the Prophet. The proposal said he preferred this to referring the guarding to the Prophet, which could be read as the Prophet doing the guarding; the last clause now says the view he sets aside takes the Prophet as the one guarded.

The corrected entries after refutation. Day 10, on 4:58: the refuter found al-Tabari’s stated preference verbatim (the ayah is addressed to those in charge of the Muslims’ affairs, as 4:59 charges the flock with obedience), and his allowance that it may have come down on Uthman b. Talha and cover every trustee is not contradicted. What broke was rule 2: the context clause put the act of 4:51 in the past (those who favoured idolaters) where day.js prints it in the present. The second judgement changed favoured to favour and nothing else. Day 23, on 10:58: al-Tabari’s own gloss makes the bounty Islam and the mercy the Qur’an, and says that the two, jointly, are better than this world’s wealth and treasure, one predicate on a joined subject. The sentence said each worth more, which distributes a comparison his words make together; the second judgement changed each to together and nothing else, and recopied the evidence character for character from the packet, since the earlier copy held a shadda and a fatha in the other order.

The revisions from the first critic reading. Day 20, on 8:61, a hard ayah: the critic found that the reading ended on the two concerning different peoples, an image without its point, and so dropped the limit al-Tabari sets himself, which rule 6 asks the sentence to carry. The reviser reopened the packet on 8:61: after the reports of Qatada, Ikrima, al-Hasan and Ibn Zayd that the verse is abrogated, al-Tabari answers in his own voice that the order to slay in Bara’a does not negate it, because this verse was meant for a people of Scripture with whom God allowed peace on the taking of tribute, while the order to slay meant only the Arab idol worshippers, from whom tribute may not be accepted, each verse standing in force in its own case. The critic’s own wording ran to forty six words by the gate’s count, so the reviser wrote the limit and its reason more briefly: the reading now takes the verse as unrepealed, as concerning a people of Scripture allowed peace for tribute, whereas At Tawbah’s order to slay means Arab idol worshippers. The context clause, the commentator, the place and the source line are unchanged, and the entry is forty five words. The Banu Qurayza, whom al-Tabari names as the occasion, are left unnamed because the day’s text does not name them; the two groups the sentence names are named as al-Tabari names them and attributed to him. The second refuter found every phrase in his words and did not refute; he noted that Ibn Kathir, on 8:61 to 8:63, disputes the Qurayza occasion because the setting is Badr, which does not touch a sentence that names al-Tabari alone. Day 23, on 10:58: the critic found two subjects (an exhortation and a balm for the breasts) taking the singular is announced; the reviser changed is to are and nothing else, and the second refuter, reopening the packet, found that al-Tabari later calls this the interpretation he gave, so the reading is his and not only a report, and that he records the reverse view (the bounty the Qur’an, the mercy Islam) without deciding, while the sentence claims no preference.

The revisions from the second critic reading. The critic carried forward two batch wide problems of the first reading. The first is the divine name outside quotation: days 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 12 wrote Allah in their own prose, while the rest wrote God, and rule 7 asks for God outside quotation. The revisions on days 1 (the praise of God), 2 (God’s sanctuaries), 5 (God never overtaxing), 6 (God’s love) and 12 (waging war on God, a killing God forbids) changed that word and nothing else, word counts unchanged; each reviser reopened the packet on the key ayah and found the evidence verbatim, and the second refuter on each found the reading in the commentator’s own voice and did not refute. The second is the form of the source line: days 27, 30, 31 and 32 opened with Al-Tabari where the roster’s display name is al-Tabari, and days 11 and 13 to 17 named the edition as the Tarteel QUL text without the word Arabic. The revisions on days 11, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 17 now name the edition as the Tarteel QUL Arabic text, and those on days 27, 30 and 31 open with al-Tabari; each changed the source line only, and each was kept. On days 13 and 14 the evidence was recopied from the packet’s own bytes, since the earlier copies held a shadda and a vowel sign in the other order. Two revisions were refuted. On day 7, on 3:92, the reviser changed Allah’s kindness to God’s kindness; the second refuter found the reading sound in al-Tabari’s words (al birr as God’s kindness, His favour in bringing the believers into His Garden, reached by giving of the wealth one loves), but found that the sentence gave no antecedent for them in bringing them into His Garden except those dying in disbelief in the context clause, so that it could be read as al-Tabari bringing unbelievers into the Garden, the reverse of 3:91 and of his gloss, which addresses the believers. On day 32, on 17:78, the reviser changed Al-Tabari to al-Tabari; the second refuter found the reading holds (he prefers the leaning past the zenith, the noon prayer, and the prayer at nightfall as the sunset prayer alone), but that the place failed: the digitisation labels the segment 17:77 to 17:78, yet inside it al-Tabari has his own heading for 17:78, and his comment on 17:77 says nothing of prayer. Both faults were in the entries before revision as well, so at the first edit day 7 fell back to an entry the candidate gate rejects (Allah outside quotation) and was left a hole, and day 32 was used with its old place and its capital.

The revisions from the third critic reading. The critic raised three problems, and each went through revision and was kept. Day 7, on 3:92: the reviser reopened the packet, found that Abu Jaʿfar twice names the addressees as the believers, glosses al birr as God’s favour in bringing them into His Garden and paraphrases the ayah as giving in alms of what they love and wish to keep, of their choicest wealth; the reading now says God’s kindness in bringing the believers into His Garden, reached by giving from the wealth they most wish to keep, forty four words, and the evidence joins the two verbatim pieces with an ellipsis so that the second half of the reading is carried too. The refuter found both pieces under the recheck and did not refute, so day 7 is no longer a hole. Day 32, on 17:78: the place is now on 17:78 in both the sentence and the source line, the source line opens al-Tabari, and a middle piece on the sun passing the zenith was added to the evidence so that past midday is carried by his own words; the refuter found that the sentence claims less than he says (he also puts the afternoon prayer in the span from the leaning to nightfall) but nothing more, and did not refute. Day 22, on 9:122, a hard ayah: the same locator fault as day 32, since the segment is labelled 9:121 to 9:122 but al-Tabari gives 9:121 (spending and crossing valleys) and 9:122 separate headings, and the reading sits under 9:122; the place is now on 9:122 in both the sentence and the source line, the evidence unchanged, and the refuter confirmed his stated preference (the marching party gains the understanding, the view he gives as al-Hasan al-Basri’s, the reading that the stayers learn rejected as impossible) and did not refute. No other word of any of these three entries changed.

The revision from the fourth critic reading. The critic, reading the batch after the first edit, raised one problem, on day 16, on 6:152. The context clause said that dealings in trade follow the orphan’s wealth, which a reader at six in the morning can take as trade pursuing the orphan’s wealth, a misstatement of the passage; what is meant is the order of the sacred duties the Prophet is told to recite in 6:151 to 6:152, where the clause on full measure and weight comes after the clause on the orphan’s property. The reviser checked that order against day.js and rewrote the context clause only: among the sacred duties the Prophet is told to recite, the rule of fair trade follows the one on the orphan’s wealth. The key, the commentator, the reading (al-Tabari on ‘beyond its scope’ as asking no payer for more than the due, and no payee to settle for less), the place and the source line are unchanged, and the entry is forty five words. The reviser reopened packet.js on 6:152 and found the evidence as one contiguous span in al-Tabari’s own voice, with no competing reported views; the refuter reopened it again, found the paraphrase carries no more than his words, noted that al-Tabari himself ties the measure clause back to the list of 6:151, and did not refute. The revision was kept, and day 16 is used as revised.

The holes. There are none. The one hole of the first edit, day 7, is filled by its kept revision, and the fourth critic reading raised no problem that took any day out.

Points the verifiers recorded and judged sound. The hard ayahs (days 3, 4, 12, 20, 21 and 22) state the matter in one plain clause and carry the limit al-Tabari sets: on 2:190 the forbidden aggression is killing women, children or those who pay tribute, and the command is unrepealed by his stated preference; on 2:216 the duty falls from the rest once enough take it up; on 5:32 saving a life is refraining from a killing God forbids; on 8:61, as revised, the peace verse stands in force beside the slaying verse of At Tawbah, the one concerning a people of Scripture allowed peace for tribute and the other the Arab idol worshippers; on 9:6 the one who seeks protection hears the Qur’an and, refusing Islam, is given safe passage home; on 9:122 al-Tabari prefers that the marching party gains the understanding, which departs from Pickthall’s parenthesis (who are left behind), and the reading is his, not the translator’s. Day 26 is marked sexual only because the reading names the advances of the ruler’s wife, plainly and without detail. Day 19 was judged at medium confidence: Ibn Kathir, on 7:205 to 7:206, says this was before the five prayers were imposed beside saying the ayah is Meccan, and his this could be taken as the worship at two times of 50:39 rather than the ayah; the natural reading supports the sentence. Where a commentator treats several ayahs in one comment (Ibn Kathir on 3:31 to 3:32, 7:205 to 7:206, 12:50 to 12:53, 13:27 to 13:29 and 15:6 to 15:9) the place is cited as that passage; where a segment labelled with a range holds al-Tabari’s own heading for the key ayah (5:87 on day 13, 9:122 on day 22, 17:78 on day 32) the place is that ayah. On days 7, 9, 15 and 20 the proposer’s evidence spliced passages with an ellipsis, and on day 15 began at an honorific sign; the verifier replaced each with one contiguous span from the packet. On day 6 the evidence was recopied from the packet characters because the proposer’s copy differed in the order of a shadda and a fatha, and on day 21 it was likewise recopied character for character. The evidence on days 7, 20, 22 and 32 as revised joins verbatim pieces with an ellipsis, which the recheck splits on.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it on the full batch of thirty three, with day 16 as revised in the fourth round, the candidate gate passes: sixty six days with those already landed, no error and one warning, that al-Tabari holds fifty of those sixty six days.

Traps. These observations may belong in traps.md. From days 6, 13, 14 and 23: the Arabic in a copy of the evidence often differs from the packet only in the order of a shadda and a vowel sign, which a machine recheck will not find; copy evidence from the packet output character for character, never retype or normalise it. From day 11: a context clause that reaches back many ayahs to the setting the commentator gives (4:135 tied to the pleaders of 4:109) carries his reading into the plain sense; give the ayahs just before the key. From day 23: al-Tabari often makes one joint comparison on a joined subject (Islam and the Qur’an together better than this world); each or both distributes a claim he does not make. From day 10: the context clause must keep the tense in which Pickthall prints the neighbouring ayah. From days 22 and 26: Pickthall’s parentheses sometimes settle a question the mufassirun dispute ((who are left behind) at 9:122, the speaker of 12:52 to 12:53); the reading must follow the commentator, and the context clause must not adopt the parenthesis. From day 26: Ibn Kathir’s Ibn Jarir is al-Tabari, so a report of what Ibn Jarir holds in Ibn Kathir is second hand evidence for al-Tabari and must be checked in al-Tabari’s own comment. From day 20: on an ayah the mufassirun call abrogated or not, a reading that says only that two verses concern different peoples leaves out the limit rule 6 wants; name the peoples as the commentator names them, attributed to him, and leave out an occasion the day’s text does not name. From day 7: a context clause that names only the people of the ayah before (those dying in disbelief) and never says to whom the key ayah speaks leaves every they and them in the reading pointing at the wrong people; say to whom the ayah speaks, as the brief’s voice asks. From days 22 and 32: the digitisation’s segment labels are not al-Tabari’s own divisions; where a segment labelled with a range holds his own heading for a single ayah, cite that ayah, not the label, and check every ranged place in a batch for this at once rather than one day at a time. From day 16: a context clause that gives the order of rulings in a list by setting a ruling after a bare noun (dealings in trade follow the orphan’s wealth) can be read as one thing pursuing another; name both as rulings (the rule of fair trade follows the one on the orphan’s wealth) so that follows can only mean comes after. From the critic’s batch wide findings and the gate: lanes drift apart on the divine name in prose and on the wording of the source line, and the candidate gate now rejects Allah outside quotation as an error; tell proposers before the lanes run to write God outside quotation and to copy the roster’s source form exactly. From days 7 and 32: a revision that fixes one fault can be refuted over another fault the original shares, which leaves the day with both until a later revision carries both repairs; a reviser should read the earlier critic and refuter findings on the day, not only the problem in hand.
