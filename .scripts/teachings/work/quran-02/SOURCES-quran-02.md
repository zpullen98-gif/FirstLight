## The Qur’an, days 34 to 66 (quran-02)

Sixty six candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the thirty three days, in six lanes (days 34 to 39, 40 to 45, 47 to 52, 53 to 55 with 57 to 59, 60 to 65, and 66) and one lane for the sensitive days 46 and 56. All thirty three primaries were judged at first judgement: thirty VERIFIED and three CORRECTED, days 38, 47 and 49. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the commentator’s Arabic through packet.js on the key ayah, found the evidence words verbatim, checked the context clause against day.js and ran verse.js and entry check again. No primary was refuted. A critic then read the batch whole and raised two problems, on days 38 and 58; each went to a revision, each revision was CORRECTED, and each revised entry was attacked again by a second refuter and not refuted. Status gives thirty three living primaries, and all thirty three survive and are used, days 38 and 58 in their revised form. The thirty three standbys were never verified, because no primary fell. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, and none held a straight quote in its key, sentence or source line to be made curly (the refs keep the straight apostrophe exactly as verse.js prints them).

The spread: al-Tabari is named on twenty four days (34, 35, 36, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41, 44, 46, 47, 48, 50, 51, 53, 55, 56, 57, 58, 60, 62, 64, 65 and 66), Ibn Kathir on six (42, 45, 49, 54, 59 and 61), al-Qurtubi on two (52 and 63) and the Jalalayn on one (43, where the source line names al-Mahalli, the author for surah 28). No day names two commentators. al-Wahidi is not named, since no chosen ayah turned on an occasion he gives, and al-Tabarsi is not named, since no source for him is in hand. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own Arabic in the Tarteel QUL text via spa5k/tafsir_api at commit 9ae590a, opened through packet.js, and no conduit was used. On every day where al-Tabari reports several readings, the verifier and the refuter each confirmed that the sentence names only the one he prefers in his own voice (his formula of the more correct of the views, or his word عندي on day 48), or, on days 53 and 55, that the sentence claims no preference at all. Three days were marked as passages on violence (46, the siege of the clans; 50, the sacrifice of the son; 52, Pharaoh’s wish to kill Moses); each states the passage plainly and none softens it.

The corrected entries at first judgement. Day 38, as first proposed, took Al-Mu’minun 23:96, the command to repel evil with what is better, with Ibn Kathir on 23:93 to 23:98; the reading held (he calls repelling ill with good the antidote in dealing with people, so that enmity turns to friendship), but the context clause placed the ayah between the Prophet’s prayers for refuge, when 23:93 to 23:94 is a plea not to be set among the wrongdoing folk and both prayers for refuge come after it at 23:97 to 23:98, so the clause was changed to say the rule of conduct comes after that plea. That entry was later replaced by revision (below). Day 47: the reading held (al-Tabari on 33:72 prefers every sense of trust in religion and among people, since God confined it to no one kind), but the context clause said the ayah comes after warnings to the hypocrites, which stand at 33:60 to 33:61, eleven ayahs back; the ayahs directly before it, 33:70 to 33:71, tell the believers to guard their duty and speak straight, and the clause now says that. Day 49: Ibn Kathir’s one comment on 36:77 to 36:80 gives the occasion (Ubayy ibn Khalaf, or al-ʿAs ibn Wa’il, crumbling a rotted bone), says that on every reckoning the ayahs are general for every denier of the resurrection, and much later, glossing 36:78, says the denier forgot that God made him from nothing; the proposer joined the two with an ellipsis, which cannot be found again as one verbatim run. The reading was cut to the contiguous passage alone, the clause on being made from nothing dropped and the two men he names put in its place, and the evidence replaced with that run as the packet prints it. In days 47 and 49 the key, ref, at and commentator are unchanged.

The revisions. Day 38: the critic found that the batch taught the same line twice, since Ibn Kathir’s gloss on 23:96 (good done to the wrongdoer until enmity turns to friendship) is the sense of 41:34, which day 53 already gives with al-Tabari (repel offence with pardon till the foe is like a near kinsman), fifteen days apart. Day 53 was kept, and day 38 was moved to Al-Mu’minun 23:115, the question whether they thought they were created for naught and would not be returned (verse.js ok, at 2787), a line no other day in the batch uses. The commentator changed from Ibn Kathir to al-Tabari, the plan default, whose one comment on 23:114 to 23:115 says in his own voice that God asks the wretched whether they thought He created them in play and falsehood, and that after death they would not come back alive to be repaid for what they did in the world; the reading paraphrases that and no more, and his note on the two readings of the verb for returning is left out. The context clause now rests on 23:112 to 23:114, where the doomed are asked how long they tarried and told it was but a little; on 23:114 al-Tabari glosses the speaker as God, so the clause’s God questions them is not Pickthall’s addition. The key, ref, at, commentator, sentence, source line and evidence are all new. Day 58: the critic found that the reading contradicted itself on the page, since it gave both halves of al-Tabari’s distinction as command (made to humble themselves before His command; deniers defy only His commands), when his point turns on two different things, God’s decree (qadāʾ), to which all submit, and what He commands them to do, which the denier opposes. The reading was rewritten so that the two terms differ: made to worship and bow to Him, since deniers too yield to His decree, resisting only His bidding. The critic’s own wording ran to fifty words, over the cap of forty five, so the same sense was given in fewer. The key, ref, at, commentator, place, context clause and source line are unchanged, and the evidence is now the contiguous run in which he prefers Ibn Abbas’s reading and answers the objection. Both revised entries run to forty four words and pass entry check.

Evidence tidied at judgement with the entry unchanged. On days 42, 52 and 60 the proposer’s evidence joined two pieces with an ellipsis, and the verifier replaced it with one contiguous run from the packet carrying the same words; the sentence was not touched. On days 39 and 63 the evidence still stands in two pieces of the same comment, each found again by machine; on day 62 the ellipsis stands only for the blessing sign after the Prophet’s name, and on day 51 the evidence spans two consecutive paragraphs of one comment.

Points the verifiers and refuters recorded and judged sound. Day 42: al-Tabari, the default, gives only a single gloss on 27:62, so Ibn Kathir, whose comment is substantial, is named; his “the desperate” is his own gloss of al-mudtarr where Pickthall prints “the wronged one”. Day 52: al-Tabari on the believer’s words gives only a gloss, while al-Qurtubi gives a full question on them, so al-Qurtubi is named; the digitisation prints ولكم for ولكن in his text, and the evidence keeps it as printed with the gloss noting it. Day 36: the ﷺ inside the evidence is the Arabic as printed and appears nowhere in the sentence. Day 37: the reading renders taqwa as fear of God, as al-Tabari glosses it, not Pickthall’s “devotion”; day 57 likewise follows al-Tabari’s most fearful of God, not Pickthall’s “best in conduct”. Day 35: the hadith detail of serpents in the grave is left out under rule 6. Day 48: al-Tabari also says the Scripture inherited is the earlier books passed to the community, which the context clause does not attribute to him and does not contradict. Day 50: the sentence avoids al-Tabari’s identification of the son. Day 51: “only upon repentance” is his own limit, stated in the lines that follow his preference. Day 56: the key stops at the vocative “My Lord!”, which Pickthall punctuates as a sentence end and the gate accepts, since no shorter whole sentence cut of 46:15 reaches the forty years. Day 58: “with Ibn Abbas” reports al-Tabari’s own stated preference for that reading. Day 61: the phrase on reckoning with oneself before being reckoned with echoes an older saying, but Ibn Kathir uses it unattributed as his own gloss. Day 62: al-Tabari reports the Qatada view that 64:16 abrogates 3:102 and then in his own voice denies any clear sign of abrogation; the sentence gives his judgement as his.

Suggestions the refuters offered and the editor did not apply, since the editor may not reword. Day 55: al-Tabari gives the forty mornings explanation beside another, that the sky’s weeping is the redness of its edges; the sentence passes over the second in silence, and the refuter judged this a weak point but not a misstatement, since al-Tabari follows the first with his formula of agreement and the bulk of his reports. Day 58: the second refuter judged the revised sentence sound but noted that “them” still has no antecedent inside the sentence and leans on the key verse printed above, and offered jinn and humankind in its place as an optional clarity edit. Day 59: “the surah turns to prayer” could be read as the surah itself praying. Each may go to a further revision if the operator wishes.

The holes. There are none: all thirty three days are used.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it, the candidate gate passes on thirty three days with no error and one warning, that al-Tabari holds twenty four of the thirty three days (twenty three before the day 38 revision moved that day from Ibn Kathir to him). That follows the brief’s voice, which makes al-Tabari the default and names another mufassir only where his comment on the chosen ayah is substantial; on the nine days where another is named, the verifier checked al-Tabari’s comment first. No other commentator’s candidate was verified and left unused, since every standby stood behind a living primary. No day had to be withdrawn.

For the operator. The evidence recheck should be run on the evidence as it stands after judgement and revision, above all on days 38 and 58, whose evidence is new with the revision, on days 42, 49, 52 and 60, where the evidence was replaced at judgement, and on days 39 and 63, which still carry two pieces.

Traps. Seven observations may belong in traps.md. First, a proposer who joins two stretches of a long tafsir with an ellipsis often joins points that stand far apart with reports between them (day 49); the reading must rest on one contiguous run, and a clause that needs the second stretch is dropped, not bridged. Second, a context clause that places the ayah “between” or “after” something must be checked against the ayahs directly before and after in day.js, not against the surah’s general drift (days 38 and 47 at first judgement: the prayers for refuge come after the key, and the warnings to the hypocrites stand eleven ayahs back). Third, on an ayah where al-Tabari and others set a limit (forbearance before the command to fight, 23:96), naming a mufassir who sets none is allowed only where the passage is not hard and the sentence attributes nothing beyond him; on a hard passage rule 6 requires the limit. Fourth, al-Tabari introduces reports with قيل and gives his own view with his formula of the more correct or with عندي; a reading may call a view his only where he adopts it, and where he adopts a reported view by saying the interpreters held much as he said (day 55), the sentence should still claim no preference over the other view he lists. Fifth, the Tarteel digitisation carries misprints (ولكم for ولكن in al-Qurtubi on 40:28) and blessing signs inside the Arabic; the evidence keeps both as printed so the machine can find it again, and neither may pass into the sentence. Sixth, lanes working apart can each choose a sound entry and still teach the same line twice in one batch, as 23:96 and 41:34 did; the Qur’an repeats its counsels across surahs, so a batch should be read whole for repeated teachings before it lands, and the later or stronger day kept. Seventh, where a mufassir’s point turns on two Arabic terms that English renders alike (amr, command, and qadāʾ, decree, on 51:56), the sentence must give them two different English words, or the reader meets a paradox the commentator never made.
