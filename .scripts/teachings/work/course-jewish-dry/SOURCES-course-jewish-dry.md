## The Tanakh, days 1 to 14 (course-jewish-dry)

This was a dry run of the Jewish tradition course, three days in one lane: day 1 (Brit), day 9 (Shabbat) and day 14 (Rosh Hashanah). It will never land, but it was held to every rule. Six candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each day. All three primaries were judged at first judgement and all three were VERIFIED. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the commentator’s Hebrew through packet.js on the key verse, found the evidence words verbatim, checked the context clause against day.js and ran verse.js and the entry check again. Days 1 and 9 were not refuted. Day 14 was refuted on form and drift, judged again with the refutation in hand, CORRECTED, and lived. A critic then read the three living entries and raised three problems, two on day 1 and one on day 9; both entries went through a revision, both revisions were CORRECTED, and both were attacked again and not refuted. Status gives three living primaries, all three are used, and there are no holes. The three standbys were never verified, because no primary died. At this second edit the editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry used is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, the revised text where a revision was cleared, and no key, sentence or source line held a straight quote to be made curly.

The spread: Rashi is named on two of the three days, alone on day 9 and first on day 14; Sforno is named on day 1; Ramban is named on day 14 beside Rashi. Ibn Ezra, Radak, Rashbam, Metzudat David and the Midrash are not named. One day names two commentators, and there the two genuinely differ and the difference is named, not resolved: day 14, on Leviticus 23:24, where Rashi reads the memorial as the verses of remembrance and of the horn, with the binding of Isaac recalled for Israel’s sake, and Ramban, who quotes Rashi and calls that reading a scriptural support for a rabbinic enactment, reads it as the blast itself, a remembrance before God on the day He judges, with pardon in the ten days. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own Hebrew in the Sefaria export at commit 3f10136, reached through packet.js.

The entries. Day 1, on Genesis 17:7: Sforno, on the words to be a God unto thee and to thy seed after thee, reads that promise as God binding His name upon Abraham and his seed without intermediary, so that through establishing and keeping the covenant they endure for ever. It was VERIFIED at first judgement and CORRECTED in revision (below). Day 9, on Exodus 20:8: Rashi, in his second comment on Remember, takes it as a charge to keep the sabbath day always in mind, so that a fine thing met in the week is kept for it. He marks the comment as from the Mekhilta but adopts it as the verse’s meaning (and so its meaning is), so it is his reading as he gives it. It was VERIFIED at first judgement and CORRECTED in revision (below).

The corrected entry at refutation. Day 14, on Leviticus 23:24: the refuter found three faults, all fixable, with the line, the commentators and the difference right. First, form under rule 9: the source line named only the export commit and no edition actually read; it now names Rashi’s Hebrew as Rosenbaum and Silbermann (1929 to 1934) and Ramban’s as On Your Way New. Second, drift in the Rashi clause: the sentence made the verses of remembrance the agent that recalls the binding of Isaac, where Rashi’s words say the binding is remembered for Israel’s sake; the clause now says the binding of Isaac is recalled for Israel’s sake. Third, drift in the Ramban clause: the sentence put the judgement before ten days of pardon, where Ramban writes that afterwards, in the ten days, He pardons, and the ten days begin with the new year itself; the clause now says with pardon in the ten days, and the second mention of the verse is shortened to there. The sentence is sixty words, the limit for two commentators. Ramban hedges the judgement with it appears but grounds it at once in what the prophets make known, so on the day He judges was judged tolerable under reads. The critic raised nothing on day 14 and it was not revised.

The revisions. Day 1 was revised on two problems the critic raised. First, the reading had attached to the wrong promise: the context clause ends on nations and land, so reads the promise landed on those, where Sforno’s lemma is the promise to be their God; and uniting His name with softened his ליחד שמי עליכם, to bind My name upon you. The reading now says His promise to be their God, without quoting the key (the critic’s suggested a God unto thee is four words of the key, over the three that rule 3 allows), and binding His name to them. Second, under rule 9 the source line named no edition read; it now names Sforno’s Hebrew as On Your Way, as the packet gives it, matching day 14. The revised entry is 44 words and its source line 105 characters. The second refuter reopened the packet, matched both pieces of the evidence by machine, and did not refute; as prose only, it noted that their God and to them have nations as the nearest plural, which the key line above settles (thee and thy seed); the entry stands as the revision gives it. Day 9 was revised on one problem of prose: the command on the seventh day could be read as a command given on that day, so it now says the command about the seventh day, and nothing else changed. The revised entry is 43 words. The second refuter reopened the packet, matched the evidence by machine and did not refute.

The holes. There are none.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it on the batch written at this second edit, the candidate gate passes on three days with no error and no warning.

Traps. Five observations may belong in traps.md. From day 14: a source line that names two commentators must name the edition read for each, not only the export commit. From day 1: the same holds for a single commentator; the first edit let a source line with no edition through on day 1 while day 14 was corrected for it, and the critic caught the inconsistency, so every source line should be checked for its edition, not only the ones a refuter flagged. From day 1: when the context clause ends on one promise and the reading says the promise, the reading attaches to the nearest one; name the lemma’s promise in the reading, in the entry’s own words where quoting it would pass three words of the key. From day 14: Rashi’s lizkor lakhem makes the binding of Isaac remembered for Israel’s sake, and the ten days of repentance begin with Rosh Hashanah itself, so a clause that sets the judgement before ten days of pardon places it outside them, against Ramban’s own afterwards, in the ten days. From day 9: Rashi often ends a comment with a source in parentheses (Mekhilta, Sifra); where he introduces it as the verse’s meaning, the reading is his as he gives it and may be attributed to him, but a comment he reports without adopting should not be.
