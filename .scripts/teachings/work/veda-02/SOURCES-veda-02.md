## The Rig Veda, days 33 to 64 (veda-02)

Sixty four candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the thirty two days, in seven lane files (days 33 to 35, 36 to 38, 39 to 44, 45 to 50, 51 to 56, 57 to 62, and 63 and 64); the second half of the first lane was proposed in its own file. Fifty nine candidates named Sāyaṇa (all thirty two primaries and twenty seven standbys) and five named Yāska, all five of those standbys (days 45, 50, 57, 59 and 64, on Nirukta 10.31, 6.19, 2.21, 5.15 and 2.24 in Sarup’s translation). All thirty two primaries were judged at first judgement: thirty VERIFIED and two CORRECTED, days 41 and 54. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the cited sa.wikisource revision through packet.js on the key verse, found the evidence words again, checked the context clause against day.js and ran verse.js and entry check again. No primary was refuted. A critic then read the whole batch and raised seven problems, on days 41, 43, 45, 49, 50, 54 and 56; each went to a revision, each revision was CORRECTED, and each revised entry was attacked again by a second refuter and was not refuted. A second critic, reading the batch after those revisions, raised one problem more, on day 50; it went to a second revision, which was CORRECTED, attacked again and not refuted. Eight revisions were made in all, on seven days, and all were kept. Status gives thirty two living primaries, and all thirty two survive and are used. The thirty two standbys were never verified, because no primary fell. The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor (for the seven revised days the entry as last revised, for day 54 the entry as corrected at judgement and then revised), and none held a straight quote to be made curly. At this second edit the batch differs from the first edit on day 50 alone, which now carries its second revision.

The spread: Sāyaṇa is named on all thirty two days, and no day names two commentators. Yāska is not named, since his candidates stood only as standbys behind living primaries. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from Sāyaṇa’s own Sanskrit in the Vedārthaprakāśa on sa.wikisource, at the revision each source line names, read and glossed by the proposer and checked again by the verifier, the refuter and, for the seven revised days, the reviser and a second refuter, with Wilson’s translation opened beside it as agreement and never as the ground of the reading. Neither conduit, Wilson’s translation nor Griffith’s notes, carries any reading, so no source line takes the form as given in.

The corrected entries at first judgement. Day 41, on 4.13.5: the reading held (Sāyaṇa glosses nyaṅ as avāṅmukhaḥ, facing downward, and na ava padyate as na hiṃsyate, not harmed, and closes with na ko’pi jānāti, no one knows), but two points of wording were changed. The context clause called 4.13 a dawn hymn, when Sāyaṇa’s introduction assigns it to Agni or to the deities named in each verse and its first four verses follow the Sun rising behind his coursers; it now reads a hymn of sunrise, and the last verse became the closing verse. The reading said the last question means that no one truly knows, when in Sāyaṇa tattvataḥ, truly, qualifies the seeing and his conclusion is simply that no one knows. The proposer’s evidence joined two stretches with an ellipsis, and the verifier replaced it with one continuous span copied from the packet. Day 54, on 5.61.6: the reading held (Sāyaṇa’s introduction makes the deity of the four verses Śaśīyasī, wife of King Taranta, and on the verse he glosses adevatra as one who does not serve the gods with praise and arādhas as one without wealth fit for giving, a greedy man), but the context clause said the verse turns to a woman, when she enters at 5.61.5; it now says the verse follows a blessing on a woman, and thanks to the singer’s patrons became thanks to patrons.

The revisions. Day 41, on 4.13.5: the critic found the reading garbled, since reads ‘falleth not’ as the Sun made the quoted phrase stand for the Sun itself. The reviser reopened the packet and rewrote only the second half: Sāyaṇa now reads the phrase as saying the Sun, facing downward, is harmed by none, and the last question as meaning no one knows. The word though was dropped, since his avāṅmukhaḥ san is simply being face down. Key, ref, at, source line and evidence are unchanged. Day 43, on 4.26.1: the critic found that hears first Vāmadeva read as if a second speaker followed. Sāyaṇa says that with this and the next two mantras Vāmadeva, dwelling in the womb with knowledge of the real arisen in him, declares himself the self of all and bids the people experience their own nature so; the reading now says Sāyaṇa hears in the three verses Vāmadeva, still in the womb and knowing the truth, speak as the self of all. The context clause was trimmed to hold the cap, and the evidence was cut again verbatim from the packet. Day 45, on 4.40.5: the critic found that the Sun, wind and fire had been made the dwellings, when in Sāyaṇa they are the one dweller named in each seat (the haṃsa in heaven is the Sun, the vasu in mid air the wind, the hotā on the altar the Vedic fire). The reading now takes him, the one being, as the Sun in heaven, wind in mid air and fire at the altar, keeping the Sun as one with the Self in all creatures and with unconditioned Brahman; the context clause was trimmed, and the evidence was cut again so that it carries the altar fire. Day 49: the key verse, 5.28.1, printed the app text’s slip the Godswith homage, a missing space, which rule 8 forbids the editor to mend. The reviser did not correct the app text, because js/texts/rigveda/05.js is generated by the text fetching script in .scripts from a cached page of the sacred texts site that carries the same slip, a hand edit would be undone by the next fetch, and a change of words could move the pinned division of the Readings; that correction is left to the owner. The reviser instead took another verse of the same hymn, 5.28.3, on which Sāyaṇa glosses jāspatyam as the work of the two, wife and husband, and suyamam as well ordered, each joined to the other; Wilson follows him. The Viśvavārā reading on 5.28.1 could not survive the change of verse, so the whole entry is new: key, ref, at, sentence, source line and evidence all changed, with the same commentator. Day 50, on 5.30.2, first revision: the critic found that reads it as a seeker made the singer’s search a person, and that sees the rest of the hymn stood without its point. The reading then took the singer as a seer who asked whether Indra, though real, was hard to reach and, told he was easy, saw the rest of the hymn for reaching him. The reviser kept Sāyaṇa’s own verb apaśyat, saw, where the critic had proposed was granted, since his words name no giver, and put hard to reach for durlabha; in the context clause tells of became recounts, to hold the cap. Day 50, second revision: the second critic found that told he was easy dropped to reach, so that it could be heard as a remark on Indra’s temper and not as Sāyaṇa’s sulabhaḥ, and that him pointed first to the seer and then to Indra, so the clause had to be read twice. The reviser took the critic’s shorter context clause (after asking who has seen Indra), put told he was not for sulabhaḥ ity uktaḥ, and ended on for reaching Indra, naming Indra so that no him is ambiguous; he refused the critic’s as the way to him, which adds a sense Sāyaṇa’s indraprāptyartham does not carry. Key, ref, at, source line and evidence are unchanged, and the sentence fell from 45 words to 43. The second refuter found every clause in the Sanskrit and noted only that real is a slight gloss of vidyate, exists, not a drift. Day 54, on 5.61.6: the critic found the queen’s name spelt Sasiyasi without diacritics, though it comes only from Sāyaṇa and the batch gives his other names with them; it is now Śaśīyasī, and nothing else changed. Day 56, on 5.85.6: the critic found that the works had no antecedent, since Sāyaṇa speaks of one deed of Varuṇa, from spreading the mid air to the unfilled sea; works became deed, and nothing else changed.

Points the refuters recorded and judged minor, not misstatements, which the editor did not act on, since the editor may not reword. Day 37: the reading gives Kaśyapa’s birth chamber, which could be read as the room of Kaśyapa’s own birth, though Sāyaṇa (sūtikāgṛhe, with mahaḥ pituḥ glossed as Kaśyapa the father) means the lying in chamber in Kaśyapa’s dwelling. Day 43: Sāyaṇa’s introduction and his gloss also allow Indra as the speaker; the entry gives the reading he develops on the verse and claims no exclusivity, and the second refuter judged it true, but suggested naming the alternative if the cap allows, taking care that the womb belongs to the Vāmadeva reading only. Day 51: the source line names 5.40.5 and 5.40.6 while the sentence and the evidence stand at 5.40.6; and the context clause’s freed the light is a light gloss of the verse’s discovered, borne out by 5.40.8 and 5.40.9. Day 54: the link to Taranta stands in Sāyaṇa’s introduction and on 5.61.5 rather than on 5.61.6 itself, but the verse names her the queen, so the locator stands. Day 56: the singer marvels at the sea loosely names the object of wonder, which is the god’s deed with the sea. Day 57: on 6.9.2 Sāyaṇa ascribes the reading of the web as the sacrifice to the yajñavādinaḥ but glosses it word by word himself, and gives the knowers of the Self their reading as a second one; the sentence carries both and marks the second, and the refuter judged it his exposition, not an overclaim. The minor point first recorded on day 50, that told he was easy leaned on an ellipsis and him had two referents, was taken up by the second critic and is settled by the second revision. Any of the rest may go to a further revision if the operator wishes.

The holes. There are none: all thirty two days are used.

The gate. Run as the brief gives it, the candidate gate passes on thirty two days with no error and one warning, that Sāyaṇa holds thirty two of the thirty two days. That follows the brief’s voice, which makes Sāyaṇa the roster commentator on every verse and names Yāska only where the Nirukta treats the verse; the five Yāska candidates were standbys and were never verified, since every primary lived. No day had to be withdrawn.

For the operator. The evidence recheck should be run on the evidence as it stands after revision, above all on days 43, 45, 49 and 56, whose evidence was cut again from the packet at revision, and on day 41, whose evidence was replaced at judgement with one continuous span; day 50’s evidence is unchanged through both its revisions. The slip the Godswith homage in the app’s Griffith text for 5.28.1 remains in the Readings and wants an erratum in the text fetching script; that is an owner change.

Traps. Eight observations may belong in traps.md. First, the kind of hymn in a context clause must be taken from the verses and from Sāyaṇa’s own introduction to the hymn, which names its deity: a hymn that follows the Sun across the sky is not thereby a dawn hymn (4.13, day 41). Second, a context clause that says the verse turns to someone must be checked against the verse before it in day.js, since a figure often enters one verse earlier (Śaśīyasī at 5.61.5, day 54). Third, Sāyaṇa often gives more than one explanation of a verse, some of them ascribed to others (the yajñavādinaḥ and the ātmavidaḥ on 6.9.2, several ways of explaining 4.58.3, two glosses of bhargaḥ on 3.62.10, Vāmadeva or Indra as speaker of 4.26.1); the sentence must say which is his or that he gives several, and never present an ascribed view as his alone. Fourth, Sāyaṇa sometimes settles a verse on another verse of the hymn (the fourth prayer of 5.40.6 is named by its opening words and explained on 5.40.8), so the place in the sentence and in the source line should name where his words actually stand, and the two should agree. Fifth, a proposer who joins two stretches of the Sanskrit with an ellipsis leaves evidence the machine cannot find again as one run; the evidence must be one continuous span copied from packet.js (day 41). Sixth, in the compressed second half a pronoun, a quoted phrase or an elided complement easily attaches to the wrong thing: reads ‘falleth not’ as the Sun, reads them as the Sun, wind and fire, reads it as a seeker, the works with no antecedent, told he was easy with to reach dropped and him with two referents (days 41, 45, 50, 56); read the reading back as a sentence and ask what each it, him, them and as stands for, and whether a word cut to hold the cap has changed the sense. Seventh, the app’s Griffith text carries at least one typing slip (the Godswith homage, 5.28.1) that verse.js will pass as verbatim; a key verse with a visible slip should be refused at proposal and the slip reported to the owner, since the app file is generated from a cache and a hand edit will not hold. Eighth, Wilson’s note on 5.85.6 drops Sāyaṇa’s na vācyam and so reverses him, making the deed fit the Supreme Lord and not Varuṇa; Wilson’s notes must be checked against the Sanskrit, never trusted as a report of Sāyaṇa. Names that come only from Sāyaṇa take their diacritics, as his Sanskrit spells them, while names in Griffith’s text keep Griffith’s spelling (Śaśīyasī but Taranta, day 54).
