## The Tanakh, days 121 to 150 (tanakh-05)

Sixty candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the thirty days (2 Kings 10 to Jeremiah 23), in six lanes (days 122 to 126; 127, 128, 130, 131 and 132; 133 to 137; 138, 139, 141, 142 and 143; 144 to 148; 149 and 150) and two lanes for the sensitive days (121 and 129; 140). All thirty primaries were judged at first judgement: twenty five VERIFIED and five CORRECTED, days 123, 138, 142, 145 and 146. Each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the commentator’s Hebrew in Sefaria’s export at commit 3f10136 through packet.js on the key verse, found the evidence words verbatim, checked the context clause against day.js and ran verse.js and the entry check again. One primary was refuted, day 139, for drift; it was judged again with the refutation in hand, the second judgement was CORRECTED, and it lived. The machine recheck of the evidence found the cited words in the packet for all thirty entries. A critic then read the whole batch three times. The first pass raised four problems, on days 138, 139, 141 and 142; the second raised one, on day 139 again; the third raised two, on days 123 and 142 again. Each problem went to a reviser, each of the seven revisions was CORRECTED, and each was attacked by a second refuter who reopened the packet on the verse; none was refuted, so all seven were kept. Status gives thirty living primaries, and all thirty are used; there is no hole. The thirty standbys were never verified, because no primary died. In all, sixty candidates were proposed, thirty verified (twenty five VERIFIED and five CORRECTED at first judgement), one refuted and CORRECTED again, seven revisions made after the critic on five days and none of them refuted, and thirty survived and were used.

The editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry in batch.json is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, and no key, sentence or source line held a straight quote to be made curly. This edit read everything again from disk and rebuilt the batch whole from status. Against the batch of the previous edit, two days changed, 123 and 142, and both changes are the third critic pass’s revisions described below; the other twenty eight days are identical. An earlier edit had carried stale wording on days 123, 145 and 146 and a superseded revision of day 139; rebuilding from status at every edit is what keeps such wording out. The candidate gate passes with no error and no warning.

The spread. Rashi is named on eighteen of the thirty days: alone on eight (121, 124, 129, 140, 143, 144, 147 and 149), first beside a second voice on nine (beside Radak on 126, 130, 131, 132 and 135; beside Ibn Ezra on 127, 134 and 142; beside Metzudat David on 133), and as the second voice on one (123). Radak is named on thirteen days: alone on seven (125, 136, 139, 141, 145, 146 and 148), first beside Ibn Ezra on one (128), and as the second voice on five (126, 130, 131, 132 and 135). Ibn Ezra is named on six days: alone on two (137 and 138) and as the second voice on four (127, 128, 134 and 142); he enters with Isaiah, which his commentary covers and Kings does not. Metzudat David is named on four days: alone on two (122 and 150), first beside Rashi on one (123), and as the second voice on one (133). Eleven days name two commentators, and on each the difference is named and not resolved. Ramban, Rashbam and Sforno are not named, because none of them covers the Prophets, and the Midrash is not named, because the Midrash Rabbah has no volume on Kings, Isaiah or Jeremiah. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator’s own Hebrew in the packet, and the plan has no conduits.

The corrected entries at first judgement. Day 123, on 2 Kings 15:5, Metzudat David: his reading of the house set apart as release from the toil of governing, kingship counting as servitude in the sages’ phrase, was found verbatim at the lemma בבית החפשית; but Rashi, the plan’s default, reads the same phrase at the same lemma as a house he made himself among the graves, and Radak gives both, so under rule 5 the verifier named both without resolving them, added Rashi as the second voice and named both editions in the source line. Day 138, on Isaiah 45:7, Ibn Ezra: the proposal’s last clause had God strengthening Cyrus against Babylon’s king, a drift from Ibn Ezra’s הפך, the reverse; the verifier changed it to God strengthening Cyrus and weakening Babylon’s king, as Friedlander renders it, and shortened the wording on darkness to keep within the word limit. The reading that ברא here means decree, that darkness is only the absence of light, and that evil means war or sickness is his own. Day 142, on Isaiah 63:9, Rashi and Ibn Ezra: the proposal gave Rashi’s reading as following the written text, but Rashi nowhere announces that he follows the ketiv; that observation is Radak’s, so the words were removed (the third critic pass later restored a narrower statement, below). Both readings stand in the commentators’ own words at 63:9: Rashi, that He did not afflict them as their deeds deserved and that Michael, prince of the Presence, always saved them; Ibn Ezra, that He was distressed, as it were, and so hastened to save. Day 145, on Jeremiah 6:16, Radak: Radak says to ask concerning (בעבור) the generations that passed and what befell them, not to ask them, so ‘asking past generations’ became ‘asking about past generations’; and the context clause dropped ‘the false prophets’ cry of peace’ for ‘the false cry of peace’, since Jeremiah 6:13 to 14 give that cry to prophet and priest alike. Day 146, on Jeremiah 9:23, Radak: the context clause had said that wisdom, might and riches will not save, which is the gloss of Radak and Metzudat David on 9:22 and not the text, whose own words forbid the wise, the mighty and the rich to glory in them; the clause now reads ‘Forbidding the wise, the mighty and the rich to boast, the LORD names the one ground for glory’. The reading is Radak’s first comment on 9:23, understanding God as grasping that He is one and made all, and knowing Him as walking in His ways; the views of Maimonides and Abraham bar Hiyya later in the same comment are reported, not his, and are not used.

The corrected entry after refutation. Day 139, on Isaiah 48:10, Radak: the refuter reopened Radak on the lemma ולא בכסף and found the evidence verbatim, but his limit is that God afflicted the wicked, who are the dross, with sickness, captivity and bereavement, not to cut them off מכל וכל, altogether; the sentence’s close, the wicked being afflicted but not cut off, dropped that limit and said more than he says. The second judgement changed it to ‘the wicked afflicted but not wholly cut off’, and changed ‘silversmith’s’ to ‘refiner’s’ to match צורף כסף. Rashi and Metzudat David on the verse (the crucible of poverty or servitude set against the fire of Gehinnom) and Ibn Ezra (exile, with the dross as the wicked) read it otherwise, so the reading named is Radak’s own peshat, not the drash he reports.

The revisions of the first critic pass. The critic found three context clauses that were participial fragments with no subject and no main verb, so that the semicolon joined a fragment to a clause, and one near repeat. Day 138, Ibn Ezra on Isaiah 45:7: the clause now reads ‘Closing the LORD’s address to Cyrus, which denies any God beside Him, the verse pairs opposites’, the pairing of opposites being a feature of the verse that Ibn Ezra’s own first lemma also names (הזכיר דבר והפכו); to stay at 45 words ‘darkness being only absence of light’ became ‘darkness as mere absence of light’ and ‘the evil’ became ‘evil’. The reading, key, ref, at and source line are unchanged, and the second refuter found both lemmas verbatim in the packet. Day 139, Radak on Isaiah 48:10: the clause became ‘Midway through rebuking obstinate Jacob, spared for His name’s sake, the LORD recalls his trials’, and ‘all’ was dropped from ‘removes all the dross’ to stay at 45 words; the second refuter judged that this weakened but did not break the sense and did not refute it. Day 141, on Isaiah 57:19: the entry had given Rashi’s far and near (one faithful from youth, one newly penitent), which day 135 already gives from Rashi on 33:13, so the reader would have met it twice in six days; the commentator was changed to Radak, on his lemma בורא, who hears peace in every mouth after the war of Gog and Magog, with war named no more, and the far and near as those far from Jerusalem and near it. His reason, that the war is fought at Jerusalem, is left out and not contradicted. The source line and commentator were changed to match; key, ref and at are unchanged, and the context clause was shortened to fit 45 words. Day 142, Rashi and Ibn Ezra on Isaiah 63:9: the clause now reads ‘Recounting the LORD’s mercies to the house of Israel, the prophet recalls His care for them’, following the recital in the first person that opens at 63:7; both readings, key, ref, at and source line were unchanged. Naming the unnamed speaker of 63:7 ‘the prophet’ is a speaker label, not an authorship claim.

The revision of the second critic pass. The critic found two faults of clarity in the first revision of day 139: ‘since then few would have remained’ read first as time and not as a condition, and ‘his trials’, coming straight after ‘His name’s sake’, could be taken for the LORD’s own. The second revision dropped the aside on the name, named Israel, made the condition explicit and restored ‘all’: ‘Midway through rebuking obstinate Jacob, the LORD recalls Israel’s trials; Radak, on 48:10, reads it as a refining short of the refiner’s, who removes all the dross, for had He done so few would have remained, the wicked afflicted but not wholly cut off.’ It is 44 words. The condition is Radak’s own כי מעטים היו הנשארים אם עשיתי כן, and ‘all the dross’ is his כל הסיגים. The refuter reopened the packet on 48:10, found every clause carried, and did not refute it; the reading, commentator, key, ref, at and source line are unchanged.

The revisions of the third critic pass. Day 123, Metzudat David and Rashi on 2 Kings 15:5: the critic found two faults of clarity. The opening participle dangled, so that ‘Opening a chapter of conspiracies in Israel, Judah’s Azariah does right’ made Azariah the one who opens the chapter; and Rashi’s reading, a house among the graves, was left without its point, because the JPS ‘house set apart’ hides the Hebrew ‘house of freedom’ (בית החפשית) on which both commentators play. The clause now reads ‘At the head of a chapter of conspiracies in Israel, Judah’s Azariah does right yet is stricken’ (Azariah begins and does right at 15:1 to 3, is smitten at 15:5, and the conspiracies come at 15:10, 15:25 and 15:30), and Rashi’s reading now carries his own ground, ‘the dead being called free’, from כְּמָה דְּאַתְּ אֲמַר בַּמֵּתִים חָפְשִׁי; ‘he made himself’ was dropped for length. Metzudat David’s reading is unchanged and was found again. It is 56 words, within the cap of 60 for two. The second refuter reopened the packet on 15:5, found both lemmas verbatim, judged ‘yet is stricken’ a juxtaposition the text itself makes and not a reason it withholds, and did not refute it; key, ref, at, commentators and source line are unchanged. Day 142, Rashi and Ibn Ezra on Isaiah 63:9: the critic found that the JPS prints the read text, ‘He was afflicted’, while Rashi comments on the written text, לא צר with aleph, and glosses it as a negation, לא היצר להם כפי מעלליהם, so that his clause read as a contradiction of the verse printed above it, and the difference from Ibn Ezra, whose reading rests on the other spelling (האמת שהוא בוי״ו), was left unexplained. Rashi’s clause now opens ‘reading the written ‘not’’, which states only what his own lemma and gloss show; it does not claim that he announces a preference for the ketiv, which was the reason the first judgement removed ‘following the written text’. Radak at the same verse confirms the written form with aleph and the read form with vav. It is 60 words, at the cap for two. The second refuter reopened the packet on 63:9, found both readings carried word for word, and did not refute it; the readings otherwise, key, ref, at, commentators and source line are unchanged. None of the seven revisions was refuted.

The holes. None. Every day has a living survivor, and every entry passes the candidate gate.

The sensitive days. Day 121, on 2 Kings 10:29, states in one plain clause that Jehu has Ahab’s house and Baal’s worshippers put to death, and gives Rashi’s only comment on the verse: Jehu held to the calves for fear that the kingdom would return to the house of David, as Jeroboam had feared. Day 129, on Isaiah 6:10, states the charge to harden the people until the land lies waste, and gives Rashi’s reading that the hardening is their own doing, the people refusing to hear the prophets for fear they might understand, repent and be cured; Ibn Ezra takes the verbs as imperatives the prophet declares, a difference the entry does not name and does not deny. Day 140, on Isaiah 52:13, is on the sensitive list for the contested reading of the servant and not under rule 6, since the key verse is not a hard passage; the entry gives Rashi’s own Hebrew at 52:13 and 53:3, that the servant is Jacob, all Israel spoken of as one man, whose righteous will prosper at the end of days, and it names no other tradition. Day 123, on 2 Kings 15:5, was judged under rule 6 for exclusion: the context clause says plainly that Azariah is stricken, and both readings of the house set apart are given, Metzudat David’s and Rashi’s.

Day 138: Rashi, Radak and Metzudat David also read 45:7 as for Israel and against Babylon, but the decree and the absence of light are Ibn Ezra’s own. Day 142: Rashi’s ‘not afflicted’ and Ibn Ezra’s ‘afflicted, as it were’ genuinely differ, and the sentence names both without resolving. Traps that belong in traps.md. First, a limit carried in one word must not be dropped: Radak’s מכל וכל, altogether, turns ‘not cut off’ into ‘not wholly cut off’, and the sentence without it claims more than he says (day 139). Second, a gloss on the preceding verse is not the text: that wisdom, might and riches will not save is how Radak and Metzudat David read Jeremiah 9:22, while the verse itself forbids glorying in them, so the context clause must keep to the verse (day 146). Third, one commentator’s observation must not be put in another’s mouth: the note on the written and read forms of the verse is Radak’s, and Rashi nowhere announces that he follows the written form; what may be said is only what his own lemma shows, that he reads the written word (day 142). Fourth, where the JPS prints the read text (qere) and the commentator comments on the written text (ketiv), the sentence must say so, or his reading will look like a contradiction of the key verse printed above it (day 142). Fifth, a small relational word can reverse a commentator’s sense: Ibn Ezra’s הפך says God does the reverse to Babylon’s king, which ‘against’ blurs (day 138); and Radak’s בעבור means asking about the generations of old, not asking them (day 145). Sixth, where the Hebrew names a craft, the English should follow it: צורף כסף is a refiner of silver, not a silversmith (day 139). Seventh, where a reading turns on a Hebrew word the translation hides, the reading must carry the commentator’s own ground: the JPS ‘house set apart’ is the Hebrew ‘house of freedom’, and Rashi’s graves make sense only with his reason that the dead are called free (day 123). Eighth, a context clause that opens with a participle (‘Closing’, ‘Midway through’, ‘Recounting’, ‘Opening’) needs its own subject and main verb before the semicolon, and that subject must be the one who does the participle’s action; four entries in this batch passed every gate and every refuter as fragments or with a dangling participle and only the critic caught them (days 123, 138, 139 and 142). Ninth, a commentator who gives the same reading on two verses a few days apart should not be named for both: Rashi reads the far and the near alike on Isaiah 33:13 and 57:19, so the later day names the commentator whose reading is distinct (days 135 and 141). Tenth, where the plan’s default commentator reads the chosen lemma differently from the one named, name both under rule 5, not only the one preferred (day 123). Eleventh, the context clause must not narrow a group the text leaves wide: Jeremiah 6:13 to 14 give the false cry of peace to prophet and priest alike (day 145). Twelfth, in a sentence held at the word cap, ‘since then’ reads as time before it reads as a condition, and a lower case ‘his’ placed next to the capitalised divine ‘His’ invites a wrong antecedent; name the referent and make a condition explicit (day 139). Thirteenth, for the editor: the batch must be rebuilt from status.js at every edit, since an earlier edit of this batch carried stale wording that the gate could not catch, because each version passes the form checks.
