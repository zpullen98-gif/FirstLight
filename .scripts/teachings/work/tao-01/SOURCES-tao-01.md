## The Tao Te Ching, days 1 to 27 (tao-01)

Fifty four candidates were proposed, a primary and a standby for each of the twenty seven days, in five lanes. All twenty seven primaries were verified, every one as VERIFIED at first judgement, and each was then attacked by a refuter who reopened the packet source and, in most cases, fetched the raw Wikisource revision himself. One primary was refuted, day 2, on its plain sense alone; it was judged again, CORRECTED, and survives. None of the others was refuted. After the first edit the critic raised one problem, on day 21; that entry was revised, the revision was attacked again and not refuted, and it survives in its revised form. All twenty seven primaries survived and all twenty seven are used. The twenty seven standbys were never verified, because no primary fell. The batch passed the gate with no error and no warning, and the editor changed nothing in any entry: every entry is copied exactly as status.js gives its survivor, and none needed a straight quote made curly.

The spread: Wang Bi is named on fourteen days (1, 2, 5, 10, 11, 12, 14, 16, 17, 21, 22, 23, 25 and 27) and the Heshang Gong commentary on thirteen (3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 13, 15, 18, 19, 20, 24 and 26). No day names two commentators. No entry is hedged: every reading is taken from the commentator's own Chinese on zh.wikisource, Wang Bi from revision 2354026 (the Huating Zhang edition, 2024) and the Heshang Gong commentary from revision 2019760 (chapters 1 to 37, 2021), both as the packet gives them, and every refuter confirmed the base line commented on is the line of the day's key verse. Legge's notes were used only as a check, on days 12 and 13, where they independently report the same comment.

CORRECTED, day 2, the primary, after refutation. Wang Bi's comment under 天下皆知美之為美，斯惡已 (美者，人心之所進樂也；惡者，人心之所惡疾也。美惡，猶喜怒也…喜怒同根…故不可得偏舉也) was found verbatim and carries the reading without drift; the reading, the key verse and the source line stand as proposed. The refuter struck the plain sense: it said that all the world, knowing beauty and skill, thereby knows ugliness and the want of skill, and the second knowing came from Legge's supplied "(the idea of)", an interpretive addition, since 斯惡已 is also read as "that is already ugliness". The plain sense now says that when all the world knows beauty and skill as such, it has with them ugliness and the want of skill, which is the refuter's own wording and passes the entry gate.

CORRECTED, day 21, the primary, by revision. The critic found that the reading ended on "takes 'this' as what has been said above", a pointer with no content, so that the second half gave the reader nothing to hold beyond the recast question. The reviser went back to Wang Bi's comments on the lines just before 以此: 至真之極，不可得名，無名則是其名也。自古及今，無不由此而成 (the utmost of the truly real cannot be named, its name is the nameless, and nothing from of old till now has come to completion except through it) and 眾甫，物之始也。以無名說萬物始也 (the many fathers are the beginning of things, explained by the nameless). The reading now says that Wang Bi takes 'this' as the chapter's preceding account of the nameless, through which all things come to be. The key verse, the ref, the place, the commentator, the source line and the plain sense are unchanged; the revision touched only the closing clause of the reading. A second refuter reopened packet.js and the raw revision 2354026, found all three comments at their lines, judged the new clause a summary of 上之所云 in Wang Bi's own terms and not an addition, and did not refute it. His one reservation, that "how it is known" drops the first person of "How know I", he judged a minor loss and not a misstatement. The entry passes the entry gate at fifty nine words.

Suggestions not taken. Refuters offered optional tightenings on day 4 (the Heshang Gong commentary's 法道不自見, which might read "after the pattern of the Tao, which hides itself"), day 17 (Wang Bi's 下知避之，其令不從, more literally "so that those below evade him and his orders go unheeded") and day 24 (不可久立身行道, "cannot long establish himself and walk the Tao"). Each was marked as not required, and each entry was judged not to misstate the commentary, so all three stand as verified. Refuters also noted alternative readings for standbys or later work: Wang Bi on days 7, 9, 13 and 15, and the Heshang Gong commentary on days 21, 22 and 25. On day 21 the two genuinely differ (Wang Bi takes 此 as what was said above, the Heshang Gong commentary as 今, now, with all things arising by receiving the Tao's essential qi), which would suit an entry naming both.

Consistency, for the operator. The Heshang Gong source lines on days 3, 4 and 6 read "zh.wikisource, revision 2019760 (2021)", while those on days 7 to 26 add "(a normalised text)". Both forms cite the same revision and pass the gate; the editor copied each as it survived. If one form is wanted throughout, it should come by revision, not by the editor.

There are no holes.

Traps. Two from this run belong in traps.md. First, a parenthesis of Legge's can enter the plain sense without being quoted, by turning its gloss into the sentence's verb: on day 2 "(the idea of) what ugliness is" became "knows ugliness", which states the translator's reading as the chapter's sense. The check is to read the plain sense against the key verse with every parenthesis struck out. Second, a commentator's gloss that points back ("this is what was said above", 此上之所云也) is not itself a reading: on day 21 the reading repeated the pointer and was sent back by the critic. When a comment refers to earlier lines, the reading names what those lines say, from the commentator's own comments on them, and the evidence carries those comments too. A further observation, not yet a trap: where Legge's English and the commentator's gloss of the same characters part (day 21, 眾甫, which Legge renders as the beauties of existing things; day 23, 希言, which Legge renders as abstaining from speech and Wang Bi as speech listened for and not heard), the plain sense stays with Legge's wording and the reading gives the commentator's sense as his, without resolving the two.
