### Corrected before landing

**5, Hillesum, one moral duty.** Proposed twice, once against Pantheon 1984 p. 185 and once against Pomerans 1983 without a page. The two Pomerans printings differ: Pantheon 1983 and the British printings read more and more peace and to reflect it towards others, no comma; the Henry Holt 1996 printing reads more and more peace, and to reflect it toward others. The card ships the Holt wording and names that edition and page 218; the entry date, 29 September 1942, is the one every dated reproduction gives, and the page itself was not opened. Confidence medium, and the note says so.

**4, Terence, I am a man.** Riley's prose Terence is Bohn's Classical Library, London, 1853; the proposal's 1874 is a reprint year. The card names 1853. The wording is Riley's sentence for Heauton Timorumenos 77, Chremes speaking.

**6, Wordsworth, little, nameless, unremembered acts.** Wording verbatim in the 1798 Lyrical Ballads. The proposal had the two line counts the wrong way round, and the first verdict then counted the half line Though absent long as a line of its own while quoting the standard count that joins its half line. The critic then read the card and found it opening mid-sentence, On that best portion of a good man's life, so the half line before the famous pair was cut and the card ships the two lines the passage is known by. Counted consistently they are lines 35 and 36 in 1798 and 34 and 35 in the standard text, and the note says both.

**12, Harper Lee, climb into his skin.** The novel prints the sentence in two halves around Scout's Sir? and a dash on either side. The card's spaced ellipsis marks that omission and nothing else; every word is Lee's. The note now says what the ellipsis stands for, which the first verdict had only in its evidence.

**16, Luxemburg, at home in the entire world.** The philosophy lane proposed the sentence without a comma after world, the fiction lane with one, and the verifier ruled on the first. The refuter found that three reviewers quoting the Verso 2011 Shriver text (Kirsch, Hitchens, the IMHO Journal essay that gives page 376) print the comma, and the IMHO page was read again for this file: it prints the comma. Nettl's 1966 rendering prints the sentence without it. Since the source names Shriver, the comma is restored. The note also says what the refuter asked it to say: in the letter the sentence closes a rebuke of sorrow kept for one people only, and alone it reads as the universal line the month wants.

**20, Morrison, a friend of my mind.** Wording and the Knopf pages hold on four scholarly citations. The brief's reporter trap applies: these are Sixo's words as Paul D remembers them to Sethe, and the source named both from the start; the note now says so as the rule asks.

**26, Proust, to possess other eyes.** Scott Moncrieff's wording verbatim, chapter II of The Captive. The edition's sentence does not stop at is; it runs on after a semicolon, and this we can contrive with an Elstir, with a Vinteuil. The card cuts at that clause boundary and closes with a full stop, and the note says so, as it also says that the fountain of Eternal Youth is Scott Moncrieff's flourish and not in the French. January held this line for April; the Calendar For Life lane re-proposed it for February, where the hundred universes of a hundred others is the circle at its widest, and it lands here. ### Found, and kept out, so nobody proposes them again

**The real voyage of discovery consists not in seeking new landscapes, but in having new eyes (Proust).** The circulating compression, again; no published translation reads so. Scott Moncrieff's real sentence ships on day 26.

**There are no foreign lands. It is the traveler only who is foreign (Stevenson).** The Calendar For Life card wording. Stevenson wrote There is no foreign land; it is the traveller only that is foreign, with a semicolon, in The Return, and the sentence goes on. Verified and held for April.

**You can fence yourselves in, but you cannot forever fence it out (Tolkien).** Tolkien printed for ever as two words. Verified with the edition's spelling and held for April; only one Tolkien may land in a month and the almanac bank carries many.

**I rebel, therefore we exist (Camus, with a comma).** The comma is Bower's Part Five reprise; Part One, where the sentence first stands, prints an em dash between rebel and therefore, and that is the form verified and held for September.

**Travel is fatal to prejudice, bigotry, and narrow-mindedness (Twain, with a serial comma).** The 1869 first edition has no comma after bigotry and prints can not as two words. Verified and held for April.

**The love of our neighbour ... What are you going through? (Weil, with neighbour).** Craufurd's Putnam 1951 text reads neighbor; neighbour is the Fontana UK printing. The card names Putnam and ships neighbor.

**It is not my nature to join in hate, but in love (Sophocles, as Perseus prints Jebb).** Perseus modernises. Jebb's own 1891 page reads 'Tis not my nature to join in hating, but in loving, and that ships on day 19.

**Compassion is the chief and perhaps the only law of all human existence (Dostoevsky).** A fuller rendering of the Russian. Eva Martin's 1913 translation, which the card names, reads Compassion is the chief law of human existence, and it is Myshkin's own thought, not Ippolit reporting him; the misattribution table's Idiot trap is a different sentence.

**Not shrewdness but frankness and a love of truth (Ginzburg, as the anthologies read).** The book reads nor; see above.

**Reflect it towards others (Hillesum, British printings).** Real, but the other Pomerans printing; the card names Holt 1996 and ships toward.

**All actual life is encounter (Buber, as Kaufmann).** Kaufmann's 1970 rendering. Gregor Smith 1937, which the card names, reads All real living is meeting.

Nothing on the brief's misattribution table was proposed by any lane. The one line that argues against the theme, Wollstonecraft's It is justice, not charity, that is wanting in the world!, is verified and held for September, where it is the theme.
