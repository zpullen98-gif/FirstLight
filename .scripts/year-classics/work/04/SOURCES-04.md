### Corrected before landing

**1, Machado, the road is made by walking.** Refuted and re-verified. The poem first appeared in the Poesías completas of 1917, not in Campos de Castilla of 1912 as proposed, and a page number that could not be confirmed was dropped. The wording is settled: Betty Jean Craige set these four lines out herself on her University of Georgia page, credited to her Selected Poems of Antonio Machado, Louisiana State University Press, 1978. The card names her and the year.

**2, Mencius, the Way is like a wide road.** The proposal carried a fourth sentence, about going home and finding teachers enough. D. C. Lau's first three sentences are attested at VI.B.2, page 172 of the Penguin translation; the fourth could not be found in Lau's wording and may be a reconstruction, so the card ends at the third and the note says so.

**8, Meng Haoran, a spring morning.** The poem stands on page 108 of The Jade Mountain, not page 152. The number 152 is the scan sequence of the Internet Archive copy, not the printed folio. The wording, the line breaks and the long dash after birds are confirmed. The 1929 edition prints the poet as Mêng Hao-jan, which the note records so that nobody later thinks the card has the wrong man.

**22, Browning, oh, to be in England.** Refuted and re-verified. The card before read who wakes and dated that reading to the 1845 pamphlet. No 1845 printing was opened, and who wakes was found only in the Ward, Lock reprint of 1896; every other edition reads whoever. The wording now follows Browning's first collected Poems of 1849, verified in two scans, and the source names the 1845 first appearance and the 1849 text separately.

**26, Eberhardt, the right to be a vagrant.** Paul Bowles has offering us more liberty, not giving us more liberty, and no comma before and get out. The page number could not be checked against the City Lights book, so it is dropped rather than asserted. The French original is Le droit à l'errance in Pages d'Islam, edited by Victor Barrucand.

### Hedged, and shipping with the hedge in the card

### One thing a reader of day 13 should know

Bayard Taylor does not end the fifth line of the Before the City-Gate speech with a full stop. He ends it with a colon and runs on: Withdrawn to the mountains, a crownless king: / Whence, ever retreating, he sends again / Impotent showers of sleet. The card cuts at that complete clause and the colon becomes the closing full stop. No word is altered and no mark inside the quotation is substituted. This is the handling the March refuter settled for the same case, so the card carries no note, and the disclosure lives here. The one faithful alternative was to run the quotation eight lines to Taylor's own full stop, at the cost of ending a renewal card on sleet.

### Verified, and kept out anyway

**Kālidāsa, may all your path be pleasantness and peace.** Verified to the letter in Arthur W. Ryder's 1912 Everyman text, Act IV, spoken by Invisible beings, and refuted without a scratch. It is dropped on rule 2 alone: the refuter could find no entry for the stanza in any standard reference, and Shakuntala is not the play this quatrain is known by. A beautiful blessing on someone setting out, and it is offered again for December rather than thrown away.

**Nan Shepherd, the philosophy lane's copy.** Rejected as a duplicate, not as a false citation. Two lanes proposed the same sentence. The fiction and essay copy names chapter 2, The Recesses, as well as the page, and had already survived a five-front refutation, so that is the one on day 6.

### Found, and kept out, so nobody proposes them again

**Some men see things as they are and ask why. I dream things that never were and ask why not (Shaw).** This is the version most readers carry and it is Robert Kennedy's 1968 rendering, not Shaw. Shaw's own wording is on day 27, from the Serpent in Back to Methuselah, Part I, Act I: You see things; and you say Why? But I dream things that never were; and I say Why not? Nobody should later tidy the card into the familiar form.

**O snail / Climb Mount Fuji, / But slowly, slowly! (Issa).** Blyth's version, and the epigraph Salinger used. Day 20 prints Hass. See the hedge above.

**Oh, to be in England, and whoever wakes as who wakes (Browning).** A reprint's reading, found only in Ward, Lock of 1896. See day 22.

**Isabelle Eberhardt, giving us more liberty.** The reading in general circulation. Bowles wrote offering. See day 26.

**Meng Haoran at page 152 of The Jade Mountain.** A scan sequence mistaken for a folio. The poem is on page 108. See day 8.

### The critic's read, and 2 rulings

The critic filed two problems and both were upheld.

**Day 27, Shaw, an unmarked splice.** The card ran two Serpent speeches
together: the famous You see things; and you say Why? sentence, and I made the
word dead to describe my old skin. They are not contiguous in Act I, and the
locator names only the act, so a reader could not check the join. Ruled: the
second half is dropped. What is left is one continuous speech, checkable where
the card says it is, and the card still needs no note.

**The English original count.** The report said 12. Okakura Kakuzo wrote The
Book of Tea in English, so day 14 is not a translation and the figure is 13.
The month is under the cap of 16 either way; the number is corrected in the
report block and in the balance line above.

No other line was touched. The Calendar For Life lane did not run for April:
its curator was stopped by a content filter, and the month made its thirty out
of 44 verification survivors and 13 carry ins without it, so it was not
re run. Kalidasa's Act IV blessing was verified to the letter and refuted
without a scratch, and the editor still dropped it for want of a standard
