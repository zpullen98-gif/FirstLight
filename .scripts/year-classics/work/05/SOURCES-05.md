### Corrected entries, and what was corrected

**4 May, Louisa May Alcott, Little Women.** The Roberts Brothers text of 1868
punctuates with semicolons after Jo and after show it, where modernised reprints set
commas. The speaker is named as the novel names her, Mrs March, answering Jo after
the fall through the ice.

**5 May, Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz, Reply to Sor Filotea de la Cruz.** Trimmed to the
sentence confirmed in Peden's translation; the lead-in clause about trivial details
was never checked. The refuter confirmed this wording in the Penguin reprint of 1997,
p. 43.

**6 May, Sophocles, Antigone.** The line number is dropped. The wording and the scene
hold, but the Fitts and Fitzgerald lineation was not opened, and two printings of that
translation number the line differently: a quotation database counts 570 running
through the play, a school printing counts inside Scene 3.

**7 May, Francis Bacon, Of Studies.** The wording shipped is a modernised spelling
text of the Essays, which is the text that was actually read. The 1625 quarto prints
the sentence in the spelling of its day, so the card names the modernisation rather
than claiming the quarto's own wording.

**9 May, Sarah Orne Jewett, to Willa Cather.** The capital Y is the card's, since the
clause runs on from must not be your audience. Annie Fields heads the letter only
Sunday, 13th of December, with no year; 13 December 1908 was a Sunday and is the
accepted date. The full stop marks a cut at world.

**11 May, J. R. R. Tolkien, The Fellowship of the Ring.** Trimmed to the sentence that
stands whole. The book sets he used to say between the two sentences, so they run
together in no edition, and the comma before that tag becomes the terminal stop. The
words are Bilbo's, quoted by Frodo to Pippin. The film says out your door; the book
says out of your door.

**12 May, Henri-Frédéric Amiel, Amiel's Journal.** The translation year is 1888, not
1885: the entry for 2 December 1851 is one Mrs Humphry Ward added to the second
edition, and the 1885 first edition does not have it. Macmillan reads ploughshare, not
the American plowshare. The full stop marks a cut at a complete clause.

**15 May, Michel de Montaigne, Of Experience.** The wording is Hazlitt's later revision
of Cotton as printed by the Navarre Society in 1923, vol. 5, p. 317. The 1877 Reeves
and Turner text reads The great and glorious masterpiece of man is to know how to live
to purpose, which is a different sentence and must not be cited for this one.

**19 May, Leonardo da Vinci, Notebooks.** The spelling putrifies is the 1883 Richter
edition's own and not a slip, so the card says so. Richter files the note among the
Philosophical Maxims, so Philosophy would serve as well as the Diary tag it carries.

**23 May, Natalia Ginzburg, The Little Virtues.** Corrected to the book's nor
shrewdness. The anthologies smooth it to not, and PN Review 44 of 1985 printed Dick
Davis with not and a differently worded first sentence.

**26 May, Anne Bradstreet, Meditations Divine and Moral.** Meditation IX is confirmed
on page 50 of the Ellis edition of 1867, whose spelling hony and whose comma these
are. That edition prints the long s, so refresh and stomach are modernized here.
Modern printings give honey and a semicolon.

**28 May, Pliny the Younger, Letters.** Pliny reports the maxim as his uncle Pliny the
Elder's, inside quotation marks, in the account of the Elder's working day, so the
words are the Elder's and the letter is the nephew's. The source now says so. The
capital N is the card's, the clause standing mid sentence.

**30 May, Lord Chesterfield, Letters to His Son.** The letter number is added because
the maxim recurs in Letter LXXVII, from Dublin Castle, 10 March 1746, without the
opening In truth, and that is the date the reference works give. In Letter LXXXI a
colon follows attention, so the full stop marks a cut at a complete clause.

### Hedged entries, and why

**20 May, Émilie du Châtelet, Discourse on Happiness.** Page 349 of the Chicago volume
of 2009 was never opened. The wording follows the one full quotation of the sentence
that could be reached, in Marcy Lascano's draft of her 2021 article on Du Châtelet and
illusions, which reads prejudice in the singular; a second Lascano paper quotes the
first half with prejudices. The translators Isabelle Bour and Judith P. Zinsser are
named because that is the translation being followed, not because their page was seen.

### Misattributions and false trails, found and kept out

Record these so nobody proposes them again.

* **Man is most nearly himself when he achieves the seriousness of a child at play,
  travelling as Heraclitus** (Calendar For Life card 413). It is Nietzsche, Beyond Good
  and Evil, section 94, in Helen Zimmern's translation, whose year is 1907 and not 1906.
  The real text is held as carry-in 03-cfl-03. Heraclitus never wrote it.
* **Give a man a mask, and he will tell you the truth, travelling as Wilde in his own
  voice** (Calendar For Life card 829). The words are Gilbert's, a character in the
  dialogue The Critic as Artist, Part II, and the real sentence runs Man is least
  himself when he talks in his own person. Give him a mask, and he will tell you the
  truth. Verified in that form as 05-cfl-14 and held for June.
* **He who reads much and walks much, sees much and knows much, travelling as Cervantes**
  (Calendar For Life card 344). Ormsby's Don Quixote reads He who reads much and travels
  much sees and knows a great deal, and Don Quixote says it in wonder at Master Pedro's
  ape. Carry-in 03-cfl-06 carries the real wording and lands on 24 May.
* **Give me books, French wine, fruit, fine weather and a little music played out of
  doors by somebody I do not know, travelling as Keats** (Calendar For Life card 340).
  The card has the word order scrambled. Colvin's text of the letter to Fanny Keats of
  28 August 1819 reads Give me Books, fruit, French wine and fine weather and a little
  music out of doors, played by somebody I do not know. Verified as 05-cfl-09 and held
  for November.
* **It's a dangerous business, Frodo, going out your door.** That is the film. The book
  reads going out of your door, and it is Bilbo's sentence quoted by Frodo, with he used
  to say standing between the two halves.
* **When you want something, all the universe conspires in helping you to achieve it,
  travelling without its speaker** (Calendar For Life card 449). In Alan R. Clarke's
  translation the sentence is Melchizedek's, opens with And, and ends in a full stop;
  the wording usually quoted is the boy recalling him later in Part One, where the
  translation closes it with a comma.
* **One cannot think well, love well, sleep well, if one has not dined well** (Calendar
  For Life card 7, proposed here as 05-cfl-02). The line is genuinely Woolf's, chapter 1
  of A Room of One's Own, and the wording was confirmed. It is out all the same: the
  track already carries it as 01-cfl-15 with a November hold, Woolf already stands at
  three carried lines, and the Mrs Dalloway opening already has May. Proposing it again
  re-mints a ruling the track has made. shipped.js cannot see the carry file, so this
  is the blind spot a refuter exists to close.
* **Your silence will not protect you, Audre Lorde** (proposed as 05-phil-10). The
  wording is certain and the attribution is hers. It is out of scope: the piece was a
  paper delivered at the Modern Language Association Lesbian and Literature panel in
  Chicago on 28 December 1977, the volume is subtitled Essays and Speeches, and the
  brief puts speeches and oratory on the Makers' ground. The closed vocabulary has no
  tag for the form, which is the signal.
* **The cited edition is not always the edition read.** Three refuters caught a card
  naming a paper imprint while the verifier had read a digital text that disclaims
  compliance with any paper edition. Where the page could not be opened, the month
  either dropped the page number (05-cfl-05, Hume) or hedged and said so (20 May).

### The critic's read, and 0 rulings

The critic found nothing to file. The operator read the month and ruled on
nothing, having checked one line against its page: day 3, Plato. The wording
most readers carry belongs to translators later than Jowett, so the Jowett text
was opened at Apology 38a. It reads that daily to discourse about virtue is the
greatest good of man, and that the unexamined life is not worth living. The
card's sentence is Jowett's own, capitalised where the card begins.

Two held cautions were settled this month. Leonardo's iron rusts, carried since
January, lands on 19 May with the ruling that putrifies is the 1883 Richter
edition's own spelling and not a slip. Hamlet's use almost can change the stamp
of nature, held for May since March, is dead: Shakespeare reached the cap of
three for the year on 23 April, and the cap is the cap. Goethe reaches three on
31 May and is likewise finished.
