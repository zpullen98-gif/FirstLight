## November: 30 of 30

Theme: Gratitude and Mortality. The two oldest subjects of literature, counted
together. Thanks, and the last things.

### The one carry-in used

Jane Kenyon, Otherwise, lines 1 to 8, in the Graywolf Otherwise of 1996, lands on
day 5. It was held in April and May because it is gratitude and mortality in one
breath, and that is this month's theme stated rather than fitted: I got out of bed on
two strong legs, and it might have been otherwise. The eight line reading is used,
not the four line one (05-poet-13), because it carries the breakfast and closes on
the refrain; the four line copy is dropped, since its lines have now shipped.

### Placement

Day 1 is Manrique, because the Coplas open by telling the soul to wake, which is what
a reader is doing at six in the morning, and they wake it to mortality. Day 2 is All
Souls, and takes Maeterlinck's There are no dead. Day 11 takes Synge's we must be
satisfied. The gratitude lines gather toward the end of the month, where the harvest
festivals fall: Fisher's communion at the table, the Dear Octopus toast, Oliver,
Carver, and Hammarskjold's Thanks and Yes. The month closes on Landor, ready to
depart, which hands December its opening.

### Corrected entries

Eight of the thirty carry a note because the verifier or the re-verifier changed
something. No entry is hedged. Two HEDGEs survived and are held, not used.

* Day 2, Maurice Maeterlinck, The Blue Bird. The year was corrected to 1910: Act V
  exists only in the six act edition of that year, which inserted the Palace of
  Happiness as Act IV. In the five act edition of 1909 the Graveyard is Act IV. The
  translator's four points of suspension are closed to a full stop, and the note says so.
* Day 6, Yoshida Kenko, Essays in Idleness, section 7. The proposal dropped the clause
  but lingered on forever in the world from the middle of Keene's sentence without
  marking the cut. The sentence ships whole. The variant in this world comes from
  Keene's own Introduction, where he quotes the passage, and is not the section text.
* Day 7, Robert Falcon Scott, Message to the Public. The date 29 March 1912 was
  removed: that is the date of the last journal entry, and the Message itself is
  undated. The sentence stops before the next one, so nothing graphic comes in.
* Day 9, Josef Pieper, In Tune with the World. The page was corrected from 30 to 23.
  Page 30 belongs to the St. Augustine's Press reprint of 1999; the 1965 Harcourt
  printing named in the source is cited at page 23 by independent writers. That page
  itself was not seen.
* Day 12, Fyodor Dostoevsky, letter to his brother Mikhail, 22 December 1849. The page
  was corrected from 6 to 11 in the Koteliansky and Murry volume of 1923. This is their
  wording, not Mayne's every minute can be an eternity of happiness.
* Day 15, Emily Bronte, Wuthering Heights, the closing sentence. The punctuation was
  corrected to the first edition of 1847, which the source names: a comma after heath
  and another after unquiet slumbers. The refuter read the proofread transcription of
  the Newby volume and supplied the page, 416.
* Day 18, Vincent van Gogh, letter 638. The translation credit and the language were
  corrected: the letter is in French, not Dutch, and the Van Gogh Museum edition of 2009
  credits its French letters to Imogen Forster and Sue Dyson jointly.
* Day 26, Martha Nussbaum. The work was corrected. The sentence is not in Love's
  Knowledge. It closes an argument in Mortal Immortals: Lucretius on Death and the Voice
  of Nature, printed in 1989 and collected as chapter 6 of The Therapy of Desire, p. 229.
  The opening And is cut and the capital raised, and the note says so.

### Kept out, and why, so nobody proposes them again

* Heraclitus, You cannot step twice into the same rivers, in Burnet's Early Greek
  Philosophy of 1908. Real Burnet, but it is another rendering of fragment B12, which
  shipped in August in Kahn's wording, and Burnet folds Plato's paraphrase into it.
  shipped.js missed it only because the openings differ. Rejected.
* Ernest Hemingway, A man can be destroyed but not defeated. Already shipped: July 31
  carries it inside the two sentence line that opens But man is not made for defeat.
  shipped.js missed it for the same reason. Rejected.
* Herman Melville, the Loomings sentence with the damp, drizzly November in my soul.
  Rejected for the second time, after July. The citation is sound, but the next
  sentence calls the voyage a substitute for pistol and ball, and the honest note that
  would have to travel with it is itself a content warning. It should not be proposed
  a third time.
* Robert Louis Stevenson, Wine is bottled poetry. The famous form is a corruption.
  Stevenson wrote and the wine is bottled poetry as the tail of a clause about
  Californian vineyards that, he says, still lie undiscovered; lifted alone it turns a
  prophecy into a maxim. Refuted and rejected on re-verification.
* Ian Fleming, You only live twice. It survives, re-tagged Fiction, but the After Basho
  on the epigraph page is Fleming's joke: Bond writes the verse in chapter 11 and Tiger
  tells him it has the wrong number of syllables. No Basho poem stands behind it, and
  it must never be credited to Basho.
* Emily Dickinson, A letter always seemed to me like immortality. That is the corrupt
  Todd text of 1894. Johnson's L330 reads A Letter always feels to me like immortality,
  with Dickinson's capital, and that is what ships.
* Bertrand Russell, How to Grow Old: past rocks is the reprint and quotation site
  corruption of past boulders, and the colon after river is Russell's dash.
* Charles Darwin, the closing sentence of the Origin: by the Creator was added in the
  second edition of 1860 and is not in the 1859 text on p. 490.
* M. F. K. Fisher: There is communion, without the article, is the Goodreads and
  sermon form. Fisher wrote There is a communion.
* Maya Angelou: the singular space between the notes is the quotation trade's form.
  The book prints spaces.
* Kobayashi Issa, the roof of hell, is held as a HEDGE: the Ecco page of Hass's
  Essential Haiku was not opened, the postings disagree on capitals and the comma, and
  the earlier Milosz and Hass rendering in The Separate Notebooks (1984) prints Hell
  with no comma. Whoever lands it must open the page.
* Margaret Edson, Wit, Nothing but a breath, is held as a HEDGE: the Faber page was
  not opened. The variant Very simple, really comes from the 2001 film, not the play.
* Thomas Mann: grant death no dominion is John E. Woods's rendering. The line that
  ships is Lowe-Porter's no sovereignty over his thoughts.
* Francis Bacon, Of Death: the bare date 1625 claimed the quarto's wording, which reads
  Men feare Death. The held card now names the modernised spelling text.
* Rumi, the reed. Two renderings are both held and cannot both stand: 01-cfl-02 reads
  Listen to the reed how it tells a tale, and says Nicholson later revised it to Listen
  to this reed how it complains; 08-cfl-02 gives the revised wording under the same
  date, 1926. Whoever lands the reed must open Nicholson's 1926 page and retire the
  other copy.
* A defect in a SHIPPED card, found by the Maeterlinck refuter and passed to the
  operator: October day 25 cites The Blue Bird, Act IV, scene 2, the Palace of
  Happiness, with the year 1909. That act first appeared in the six act edition of
  1910, and in 1909 Act IV, scene 2 is the Graveyard. October's year should read 1910,
  or the two Maeterlinck cards contradict each other.
