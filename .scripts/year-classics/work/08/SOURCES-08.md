## August: 31 of 31

Theme: Impermanence. The elegies. Poets on the passing of things, and the philosophers on what stays.

### The count

Fifty six candidates were proposed across the four lanes (8 from the Calendar For Life
lane, 16 fiction and essay, 16 philosophy, 16 poetry and drama). Two of the fiction and
essay proposals, 08-fict-13 (Didion) and 08-fict-14 (Baldwin), were the same lines the
philosophy lane had already proposed as 08-phil-15 and 08-phil-12, so fifty four went to
verification. Fifty three survived verification, refutation and re-verification. One died.
The brief routed twenty two carry-ins here as well, so the editor chose from a pool of
seventy five. Thirty one were used and forty four are held.

### Corrected entries

**August 8, Virginia Woolf, Diary.** The work was corrected. The sentence is not in A
Writer's Diary of 1953; it stands in the complete Diary, volume two, under the entry for
17 February 1922, edited by Anne Olivier Bell.

**August 11, Francois Villon, Poetry.** The citation was corrected twice. The proposal
paired page 237 with the year 1870, but page 237 belongs to the Complete Poetical Works of
1887, while Rossetti's translation of 1870 stands at pages 176 to 177. The refrain is now
located by line in the 1870 translation. The wording is unchanged.

**August 12, Tennessee Williams, Drama.** The publisher was corrected. The Glass Menagerie
was first published by Random House in 1945 and reprinted by New Directions in 1949, so
the proposed New Directions edition of 1945 does not exist. The refuter also found a live
variant reading without the word two, and the Random House text carries it.

**August 13, Heloise, Correspondence.** The locator was corrected after refutation. In
Betty Radice's Penguin of 1974 the numbered sequence begins with Heloise's first letter as
Letter 1, and Letter 2 is Abelard's reply, so the original citation would have sent a
reader to a letter by the wrong person. The Latin numbering, where her first letter is
Epistola II, caused the error. An unconfirmed page reference was dropped.

**August 14, Jorge Luis Borges, Essay.** The translator, work and year were corrected after
refutation. The English proposed is not Ruth L. C. Simms's 1964 Other Inquisitions, which
reads differently at both hinges; it is James E. Irby's, in Labyrinths of 1962. Without the
fix, one translator's words would have shipped under another translator's name.

**August 15, Kobayashi Issa, Poetry.** The locator was corrected. The dew verse stands at
pages 103 to 104 of Yuasa's translation of The Year of My Life, not as the closing haiku of
Oraga Haru, which ends on a separate verse.

**August 18, May Sarton, Diary.** The page reference was corrected out. Page 34 could not
be confirmed against the 1973 Norton text, and the dated entry for October 6th locates the
passage on its own. The wording is verbatim in the scan.

**August 19, Praxilla, Poetry.** The wording was corrected to Richmond Lattimore's published
rendering: he writes what I leave behind, not what I have left behind, and keeps lower case
at the second and third line heads.

**August 21, Lady Mary Wortley Montagu, Correspondence.** The date was corrected. The
quotation books give 30 May 1756 and cite Halsband, which could not be opened; two printed
editions put the sentence in the letter to the Countess of Bute of 28 January 1753.

**August 22, Natalia Ginzburg, Essay.** The printing was corrected. The wording quoted sets
forever as one word and realize with a z, which are the accidentals of the Arcade text of
2017; Dick Davis's translation was first published by Carcanet in 1985 and that printing
was never opened, so the source now names both rather than putting 2017 spellings under a
1985 date.

**August 23, Dogen, Philosophy.** Two corrections. The translation is by Robert Aitken and
Kazuaki Tanahashi together, not Tanahashi alone, and a section and page locator was added
where there had been none. Dogen's own next sentence was restored to the quotation, because
the first sentence alone asserts the one way passage he immediately tells the reader not to
suppose.

**August 25, Percy Bysshe Shelley, Poetry.** The citation was corrected. The first printing,
in The Examiner of 11 January 1818, reads No thing beside remains and Colossal Wreck, with a
comma after bare; the wording quoted is that of the collected volume of 1819, page 92, so
the citation names that book. The first note attached to this line made a claim about letter
case and got the letter case wrong, and the refuter killed the note rather than the line.

**August 28, John Keats, Correspondence.** The citation was corrected to name the edition
whose wording this is: Sidney Colvin's Letters of John Keats to His Family and Friends of
1891, page 41, which prints a comma after affections. The Rollins text of 1958 could not be
opened and reference books citing it give a shorter reading.

**August 30, Giuseppe Tomasi di Lampedusa, Fiction.** The locator was corrected. Colquhoun's
English text is divided into chapters, not parts, and Tancredi says this in chapter 1. The
proposed Harvill 1996 page could not be confirmed: printings give page 19 and page 29, so
the page was dropped.

### Hedged entries

**August 20, Samuel Beckett, Drama.** The note hedges the page range only, not the line.
The page range could not be confirmed against the 1954 Grove printing, so the citation
stands at Act II, where Vladimir speaks while Pozzo is calling for help. The English is
Beckett's own translation of his French text of 1952.

Three further lines carry medium confidence without a note, because nothing in them needed
correcting: August 5 (Petrarch, in Robinson's 1898 translation), August 27 (Tang Xianzu, in
Cyril Birch's translation, where no page of Birch was sighted and the wording rests on a
credited reproduction) and August 16 (Arendt, read in the book after the first evidence
proved to be quotation collections). If any of the three is challenged later, August 27 is
the one to open first.

### Misattributions found and kept out

**Auld Lang Syne, under Robert Burns.** Proposed as 08-cfl-08 and hedged by the verifier,
refuted, and rejected on re-verification. Two grounds, either sufficient. It is a song, and
song lyrics are out of scope for this track. And Johnson printed it unsigned in The Scots
Musical Museum under the mark Z, his mark for an old song with additions, and Burns himself
said three of the five stanzas were old, so the byline would be an attribution at best. The
verdict also cited a volume whose scan is a later reissue than the year given. Do not
propose it again.

**Switzerland is a country where very few things begin, but many things end, under Tender Is
the Night.** Shipped on August 26, but not from the novel. Every quotation site hangs this
sentence on Tender Is the Night and it is not there; it is entry 335 in section E of the
Notebooks, edited by Bruccoli. The novel citation is the trap.

**A New Refutation of Time, under Ruth L. C. Simms.** See August 14. The most quoted English
of this passage is Irby's, and it travels under Simms's name because her Other Inquisitions
is the volume people name. Her text reads that carries me away and that mangles me.

**Genjokoan, under Kazuaki Tanahashi alone.** See August 23. The Enlightenment Unfolds
rendering is Robert Aitken's and Tanahashi's jointly.

**Ozymandias, in the 1818 Examiner text.** See August 25. The Examiner reads No thing beside
remains and Colossal Wreck. Anyone quoting the familiar modern wording must cite 1819.

The month proposed nothing from the brief's standing misattribution list, and the Calendar
For Life lane's August candidates that sit on that list (the Nietzsche dancing line, the
Hemingway Madrid lines) were not carried forward.

### The critic's read, and 4 rulings

**Day 15 had no terminal stop, and that one is upheld.** Issa's dew verse ends
on the translator's ellipsis, And yet . . . And yet . . . A card must close on
a stop, and the stop cannot be supplied: that would be minting punctuation into
Yuasa's translation of an elegy for his daughter. No English version of the
poem closes on a stop, because the suspension is the poem. So the verse is held
rather than fixed, and 15 August goes to Ono no Komachi, Kokinshu 113, in
Keene's anthology: verified, attacked and unbroken, the same theme of things
going while one is not looking, the same country, the same form, and it ends
where a card must end. Issa stays at two lines for the year and can be asked
for again.

**The count of women was right about the month as written, and the swap
settles it.** The critic counted eleven against a report of twelve and the
count was correct. Komachi stands where Issa stood, so the month holds twelve
and the report needs no change.

**The country table is refused.** The critic read it as summing to thirty and
naming a German nobody wrote. Counted here, the thirteen figures come to
thirty-one, and the German is Hannah Arendt, born in Hanover, whose line on 16
August is counted an English original as well, which is where the confusion
probably began.

**Fitzgerald under Diary is refused.** The Notebooks are a commonplace book and
not a dated journal, which is true, but this track has tagged the private
notebook Diary since May: Joubert's Pensées on the 1st, Leonardo's notes on the
19th, Butler's Note-Books on the 27th. Retagging one of four would make August
the odd month out, and Essay would claim the pages were composed for a reader,
which is the one thing a commonplace book is not. The vocabulary is closed at
seven tags and Diary is the honest end of it.
