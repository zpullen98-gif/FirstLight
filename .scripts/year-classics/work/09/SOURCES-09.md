### Corrected entries, and what was corrected

**September 3, Saadi, The Gulistan.** The chapter title. Rehatsek calls chapter I
The Manners of Kings in the contents, the chapter heading and every running head.
On the Conduct of Kings belongs to other English Gulistans, not to the named
translation. The wording of the four lines survived every attack, including
afflicts where the widely circulated rendering reads affects.

**September 4, Jane Addams, Twenty Years at Hull-House.** The card prints the
closing clause of a longer sentence, lifted with its first letter raised to a
capital. The note says so. Verified in the 1910 text and in the 1893 Crowell
printing of the paper.

**September 6, Euripides, The Suppliants.** The translation. Rich and weak is the
Random House printing of E. P. Coleridge in The Complete Greek Drama of 1938;
the Bohn text of 1891 reads rich and poor. The full stop marks a cut at a
complete clause.

**September 8, Chinua Achebe, Things Fall Apart.** The locator. The page given
for the Penguin Modern Classics printing of 2001 could not be checked against a
copy, so it was dropped and the 1958 Heinemann first edition is named instead.

**September 9, Ibn Khaldun, The Muqaddimah.** The locator was made precise. In
Rosenthal the sentence is the printed heading of section 41 in Chapter III,
volume 2, page 103, and it introduces the section rather than standing in its
body. The terminal stop replaces the colon that follows it.

**September 10, Emma Lazarus, The New Colossus.** The locator. The quotation
begins in the middle of line 10, after With silent lips, and runs to the end of
the sonnet. The poem encloses the speech in quotation marks, which the card
drops.

**September 13, Christine de Pizan, The Book of the City of Ladies.** The
translation. This is Earl Jeffrey Richards, 1982; the little girls wording that
circulates is Rosalind Brown-Grant's Penguin of 1999. Page 63 is the 1982 Persea
printing and the 1998 reissue paginates differently.

**September 20, William Morris, A Dream of John Ball.** Morris writes one long
sentence. The quotation is cut at a complete clause, its first letter raised to
a capital, and closed with a full stop no edition prints. Every word is in order.

**September 21, Bai Juyi, Watching the Reapers.** The locator. Watching the
Reapers is poem 15 of the whole volume, not the fifteenth of the Po Chu-i
section, where it stands fifth. The source now cites page 41 of More
Translations from the Chinese, Knopf, 1919. Waley prints the poet as Po Chu-i.

**September 22, Kautilya, Arthashastra.** The locator had put the Bangalore
Government Press, which published the translation, in the place of the
translator. The note also says plainly that authorship and date are traditional
and that scholars treat the text as composite.

**September 23, Dag Hammarskjold, Markings.** The locator. The proposal gave page
262, which is not where the passage falls. The verifier, unable to confirm it,
dropped the locator altogether and left a bare title, which the refuter rightly
called no locator at all. Re-verification found the sentence at the foot of page
158, in the 1957 section. The book's own wording, of value to contribute, stands
against the shorter anthology wording.

**September 24, Florence Nightingale, Cassandra.** The page. The sentence stands
on page 25 of the Feminist Press edition edited by Myra Stark, not page 74.

**September 25, Bertolt Brecht, The Caucasian Chalk Circle.** The translator. The
proposal credited Eric Bentley, whose Parables for the Theatre reads Fearful is
the seductive power of goodness. The wording on the card is James and Tania
Stern with W. H. Auden, Plays Volume One, Methuen, 1960.

**September 29, Martin Luther King Jr., Letter from Birmingham Jail.** The
edition. The proposal named chapter 5 of the Harper and Row printing of 1964 and
rested on an anonymous quotation site. Nobody has opened the 1964 book, and that
book is a revision rather than a reprint. The corrected source claims only what
was read: the Martin Luther King, Jr. Papers Project text, which records the
letter's publication in Why We Can't Wait at pages 77 to 100. Confidence is
medium and the note says why.

No entry in the month is a HEDGE. Every line carries a locator that someone
opened, and where nobody could open the printing, the source names the text that
was actually read.

### Kept out, so nobody proposes them again

**Virgil, To tame the Proud, the fetter'd Slave to free.** Genuine Dryden, and
not Virgil. Parcere subiectis means spare the conquered; the freeing of slaves
is Dryden's invention, and the couplet is the tail of a sentence whose head is
To rule Mankind, so Anchises is naming the arts of empire and not a liberation
programme. The witness is also the 1709 Tonson octavo, not the 1697 folio the
proposal named, and the Latin is 6.852 to 853, not 6.853. Survives as a
corrected citation, held out of this month.

**Mark Twain, The Revised Catechism.** The New-York Tribune of 27 September 1871,
page 6, prints it as a catechism set in separate lines with an A. before each
answer. The rules allow no line breaks in prose and no slashes outside verse, so
every form the card could print is one no edition prints. Rejected as unshippable
rather than minted. The quotation books run it together with dashes and lower
case, which is a third form again.

**Thomas Merton at Fourth and Walnut.** Two texts exist and they are not the same
sentence. The private journal of 18 March 1958, printed in A Search for Solitude,
reads in Louisville, at 4th and Walnut, suddenly realized that I loved all the
people. The card must use the Conjectures rewrite, which reads all those people.
Shipping the journal wording under the Conjectures title is the trap.

**Saadi, affects one limb.** The rendering that circulates, including on the Bani
Adam page at Wikipedia, reads affects. Rehatsek reads afflicts. The scan settles
it.

**Brecht, Fearful is the seductive power of goodness.** Bentley's wording, not the
Stern and Auden wording, and the two must never be mixed under one translator
credit.

**Euripides, rich and poor.** The Bohn text of 1891. The Random House Coleridge of
1938 reads rich and weak.

**Rosa Luxemburg, freedom for the one who thinks differently.** Real, in Wolfe's
translation, but it is a marginal note that Luxemburg set beside the main text,
it is absent from Paul Levi's first printing of 1922, and chapter 6 is Wolfe's
editorial division rather than Luxemburg's. Held for a later month with a source
line and note that say all of that.

**Charles Dickens, Mankind was my business.** Not a misattribution but a
collision. February verified it as carry 02-fict-10 and SOURCES.md routed it to
December. The fiction and essay lane re-proposed it here. It stays with December,
and whichever month lands it should correct Stave One to Stave I, which is the
1843 Chapman and Hall setting.

**Ernest Hemingway, I was always embarrassed by the words sacred, glorious, and
sacrifice.** The citation is flawless and the fit is not. It is the canonical
statement that the vocabulary of obligation is an embarrassment, which is this
month's theme inverted, and the verified Sun Also Rises carry already has the
month's one Hemingway slot.

**Anna Laetitia Barbauld, Yes, injured Woman! rise, assert thy right!** Real,
famous, and a palinode. The poem turns against its own opening and closes that
separate rights are lost in mutual love. It survives only with a note that says
so, and it is held rather than shipped bare.

The misattribution sheet's own entries were watched throughout and none of them
reached a verdict: no Gandhi be the change, no Einstein insanity, no Burke
triumph of evil, no Voltaire defend to the death.

### The critic's read, and 0 rulings

nothing, having opened one page: 16 September, Aemilia Lanyer, because old
spelling copied by hand is where a transcription goes wrong without anyone
