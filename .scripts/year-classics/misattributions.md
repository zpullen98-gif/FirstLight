# Famous, and not theirs

Pasted into every curator and verifier brief for The Classics. Each line below
travels under a name that did not write it. Propose the real author with the
real source, or leave it out; never the famous name.

| The line | Travels as | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Be the change you wish to see in the world | Gandhi | Not in Gandhi's works in that form. The nearest is a 1913 Indian Opinion passage on personal change reflecting in the world; the slogan is later. |
| Well-behaved women seldom (rarely) make history | Marilyn Monroe, Eleanor Roosevelt | Laurel Thatcher Ulrich, "Vertuous Women Found", American Quarterly 28 (1976). |
| Insanity is doing the same thing over and over and expecting different results | Einstein | Not Einstein. Earliest in print: a Narcotics Anonymous pamphlet (1981); also in Rita Mae Brown's Sudden Death (1983). |
| What lies behind us and what lies before us are tiny matters compared to what lies within us | Emerson, Thoreau | Henry Stanley Haskins, Meditations in Wall Street (1940). |
| This too shall pass | Lincoln, Solomon, the Sufis | A Persian adage. Lincoln tells it as an Eastern monarch's story in the Wisconsin State Fair address (1859); it is not his line. |
| Be yourself; everyone else is already taken | Oscar Wilde | Not in Wilde. Twentieth-century, author unknown. |
| I disapprove of what you say, but I will defend to the death your right to say it | Voltaire | Evelyn Beatrice Hall, The Friends of Voltaire (1906), summarising his attitude. |
| Whatever you can do, or dream you can, begin it. Boldness has genius, power and magic in it | Goethe | John Anster's very free 1835 rendering of the Prelude to Faust; the German has nothing like it. |
| The only thing necessary for the triumph of evil is for good men to do nothing | Edmund Burke | Not in Burke. The closest is John Stuart Mill's 1867 St Andrews address, and that is not the wording either. |
| There is nothing to writing. All you do is sit down at a typewriter and bleed | Hemingway | Not Hemingway. Traced to sportswriter Red Smith, and not verbatim there. |
| Write drunk, edit sober | Hemingway | Not Hemingway; nearest is Peter De Vries, Reuben, Reuben (1964), about a character. |
| If you want to build a ship, don't drum up the men to gather wood | Saint-Exupéry | A paraphrase; the Citadelle passage says something adjacent and longer. Quote the real passage, in a named translation, or leave it. |
| Dance like nobody's watching | Mark Twain | A 1987 country lyric (Susanna Clark and Richard Leigh, "Come from the Heart"). |
| Not all who wander are lost | Tolkien | Tolkien wrote "Not all those who wander are lost." Already shipped in the lift bank; excluded anyway. |
| The definition of a friend is one soul in two bodies | Aristotle | Diogenes Laërtius reports it of Aristotle, Lives 5.20; cite Diogenes, not Aristotle's works. |
| A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step | Laozi | Real (Tao Te Ching 64) and shipped on the Philosophers; Laozi is banned here anyway. |
| Hell is other people | Sartre | Real (Huis Clos, 1944) but nearly always misread; if proposed, the note must carry Sartre's own gloss. |
| God is dead | Nietzsche | Real (The Gay Science 108, 125) but it is the madman's line and needs the context; not a morning line in any case. |
| The unexamined life is not worth living | Socrates | Real: Plato, Apology 38a, and it is Plato's Socrates. Cite Plato, name the translator. |
| Beauty will save the world | Dostoevsky | Real, but it is Ippolit REPORTING Myshkin in The Idiot, part 3, ch. 5. The speaker goes in the source. |
| To thine own self be true | Shakespeare | Real, but the speaker is Polonius, Hamlet 1.3, and he is not offered as wisdom. Name him. |
| Two roads diverged... I took the one less traveled by | Frost | Real, and shipped in the lift bank; excluded. Frost's own reading of the poem is ironic, which a note would have to say. |
| Do not go gentle into that good night | Dylan Thomas | Real (1951), and grief; publishability decides, and the month. |
| Everything happens for a reason | Aristotle, Marcus | No source. Out. |
| Music is the universal language of mankind | Longfellow | Real, Outre-Mer (1835), but it is prose travel writing, and it is trite. |
| Fortune favours the bold | Virgil | Real (Aeneid 10.284, audentes fortuna iuvat), and it is Turnus, speaking before he dies. Name the speaker or leave it. |

Two speaker traps to carry generally: a line spoken by a character is the
character's, and goes in the source in parentheses after the locator; a line
one character REPORTS of another is the reporter's, and the note says so.
